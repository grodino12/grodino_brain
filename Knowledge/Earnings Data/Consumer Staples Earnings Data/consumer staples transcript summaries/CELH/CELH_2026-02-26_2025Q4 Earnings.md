## Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** Celsius Holdings delivered a transformational 2025 capped by record full-year revenue of $2.5B (+86% YoY) and Q4 revenue of $721.6M (+117% YoY), driven by the acquisitions and rapid integration of Alani Nu and Rockstar Energy alongside 7.5% organic growth in the legacy CELSIUS brand. Positive drivers included record Alani Nu Q4 net sales of $370M (pro forma +136% YoY), a triple-digit start for Alani in the first 6-8 weeks of 2026, a healthy 12.8% Q4 tracked scanner gain for brand CELSIUS, Cherry Bomb LTO selling out in record time, substantial shelf-space wins for the 2026 spring reset (+17% for CELSIUS, triple-digit for Alani), and an approximate 20% dollar share of U.S. tracked energy for Q4. Headwinds management acknowledged included a 7.7% GAAP decline in brand CELSIUS Q4 revenue caused by Pepsi-system order sequencing during the Alani load-in (offset by a ~$25M net positive at the portfolio level), Q4 gross margin compression of 280bps to 47.4% due to Rockstar dilution, integration/transition costs, tariffs, and the Midwest aluminum premium, plus a continued Rockstar retail decline (-10.3% Q4). Management's forward outlook is confident but deliberately hedged — reiterating Alani integration completion by end-Q1 2026, Rockstar completion in H1 2026 (COGS normalization by end-Q2), a return to "low 50s" gross margin by 2H 2026 with mid-50s a multi-year target, and a tone emphasizing execution discipline, tightening peaks-and-valleys with Pepsi, and leveraging a three-brand portfolio to "win at the register."

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - Spring shelf-reset timing and when the space gains translate to reported revenue, with particular interest in Q1 trajectory for brand CELSIUS (cited in Q1, Q5).
  - Mechanics and unwind of the $25M CELSIUS/Alani timing benefit — specifically whether Alani inventory mirrors the CELSIUS-into-Pepsi build of 2022 and implies a 4Q 2026/2027 destocking (cited in Q2).
  - Gross margin bridge to low/mid-50s, with emphasis on the Midwest aluminum premium, tariff exposure, and phasing across 2026 (cited in Q3).
  - SKU rationalization, LTO cadence and retail velocity as distribution expands into new (especially convenience) points of sale (cited in Q4, Q6).
  - Sourcing of shelf gains — category expansion vs. share-taking — and whether the Alani transition resembles the CELSIUS-Bang playbook of 2022 (cited in Q5, Q6).

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - **Optics of the brand CELSIUS Q4 decline vs. +12.8% scanner:** Management repeatedly framed the 7.7%/8% GAAP decline as transitory Pepsi-system sequencing, but analysts pressed on whether quarterly volatility continues into 2026 and whether Alani inventory will unwind similarly (Q1, Q2).
  - **Alani "pull-forward" concern:** Gerald Pascarelli directly asked whether Q4 Alani load-in was a pull-forward; Langhans characterized it as "opportunistic" rather than a pull-forward, but acknowledged Cherry Bomb was loaded in at year-end (Q6).
  - **Gross margin ambiguity:** Bonnie Herzog pressed for mid-50s framing; Langhans explicitly pulled that back as not a 2026 target, only a multi-year aspiration, with 2H 2026 low-50s as the actual goal — a meaningful walk-back of the prior "mid-50s" language analysts had anchored on (Q3).
  - **Velocity dilution from distribution expansion:** Management acknowledged velocities will decline in new markets/channels as Alani expands into white-space points of sale, raising the question of how much of the +76.9% Alani scanner Q4 growth is durable vs. distribution-led (Q4, Q5).
  - **Evasiveness on forward guidance:** Management explicitly stated they "don't provide any forward-looking information" on Q1 revenue, despite multiple attempts by analysts to get directional commentary on the CELSIUS reported-sales trajectory (Q1).

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **Alani integration completion by end-Q1 2026:** Management expects full integration including COGS alignment; this is the key milestone for margin recovery and cleaner brand-level reporting.
  - **Rockstar integration completion end-Q2 2026:** Rockstar COGS alignment and transition from "other income" accounting to finished goods model (U.S. in Q1, Canada in H1 2026). This should unlock the ~280bps of Q4 margin drag.
  - **2026 Spring Shelf Resets (completing end-spring 2026):** 17% space gain for CELSIUS and triple-digit for Alani begin materializing through resets; biggest Alani gains in convenience. This is the key revenue catalyst into the summer selling season.
  - **National Fizz-Free rollout (2026):** Non-carbonated line extension opening a new demand pool ahead of summer.
  - **2026 LTO cadence:** Lime Slush (Alani) already launched, additional LTOs for both Alani and CELSIUS across 2026; Cherry Bomb sold out in record time, validating the playbook.
  - **Midwest aluminum premium and tariff trajectory:** Management flagged these as variables that could accelerate or delay low-50s margin attainment.
  - **Triple-digit Alani 2026 start:** First 6-8 weeks of 2026 are tracking triple-digit growth — a near-term data point that could re-rate sentiment if sustained through Q1 print.

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** Q4 2025 included a ~$25M net positive from timing (CELSIUS orders pushed, Alani load-in pulled). This sets up a Q4 2026 tough comp to the extent the dynamic does not repeat. Alani Q4 2025 net sales of $370M (pro-forma +136%) establishes a very high base.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Brand CELSIUS Q1 2025 carried softness per Fieldly ("some continued softness in the first quarter of 2025"), making Q1 2026 a relatively easier comp. CELSIUS's back half of 2024 was pressured (per Langhans: "both the category and brand CELSIUS experienced pressure in the back half of 2024") — 2H 2026 comps for CELSIUS are thus easier.
  - **Timing Shifts:** Management explicitly described Q4 2025 shipments: CELSIUS shipped one fewer week in the Pepsi DSD system at quarter-end while additional Alani was loaded in, driving the 7.7% GAAP CELSIUS decline vs. 12.8% scanner. This creates an asymmetric comparability issue for Q1 2026 (CELSIUS orders "align more closely to the tracked data" in early 2026 per Langhans) and potentially a 4Q 2026/2027 Alani unwind.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** Alani DSD transition to Pepsi substantially completed by year-end (ACV from ~87% at start of Q4 to 94.2% as of week ended Feb 1, 2026). Triple-digit space gains in 2026 resets for Alani — largest white-space in convenience. 17% space gains for brand CELSIUS at the 2026 reset. Cherry Bomb was first Alani LTO in the Pepsi system — "loaded in at the end of the year" per Langhans — sold out in record time. Rockstar TDPs down 17% YoY per Circana for calendar 2025.

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** No specific FX call-outs; RGM commentary emphasized mix, price-pack architecture, pack strategy — not broad-based price increases. Management explicitly stated: "Revenue growth management for us is not about broad-based price increases."
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** Alani Nu acquired April 1, 2025 (contributed $1.002B revenue in 9 months, 39% of FY25 revenue); Rockstar Energy acquired August 28, 2025 (contributed $55.6M in net sales, with an additional $13M recorded in "other income" due to integration accounting treatment). These acquisitions drive the entire YoY comparability story.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** Rockstar integration accounting is a significant reporting idiosyncrasy — Q4 2025: $45M in net sales + $6M in other income; FY 2025: $56M net sales + $13M other income. U.S. Rockstar to transition fully to finished-goods model in Q1 2026, Canada in H1 2026. Non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA excludes $327.5M of FY distributor termination fees ($80.8M in Q4) and $22.4M inventory step-up; Adjusted Diluted EPS of $1.34 (vs. GAAP $0.25) is materially dependent on these adjustments.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Midwest aluminum premium elevated, tariffs present; both flagged as variables in margin trajectory. Midwest premium and tariffs will pressure margins near-term; timing of subsidence could accelerate margin recovery.

## Q&A Summary

### Question 1 — Spring Shelf Reset Timing & CELSIUS Q1 Trajectory

- **Q: Filippo Falorni (Citigroup):** Asked for an update on spring shelf resets and when the shelf space gains materialize; specifically pressed whether brand CELSIUS could show reported-sales improvement in Q1 2026 given the shelf space wins.
  - "The shelf space gains that you discussed last week at the CAGNY conference for both CELSIUS and Alani, can you give us a bit of an update on the spring shelf space resets and when we should start to see some of the benefits from the shelf space gains? And then in particular for the brand CELSIUS, you explained the gap versus consumption in Q4. That was very helpful to add it to the release, so thank you for that. So could we see an improvement in Q1 as we think about it on a reported sales basis given the shelf space for brand CELSIUS?"

- **A: John Fieldly, CEO / Jarrod Langhans, CFO:** Fieldly said shelf gains historically materialize through end of spring ahead of summer selling season; biggest Alani gains in convenience (white space). He deflected on forward revenue ("Historically, we don't provide any forward-looking information") but acknowledged "there could be gaps going forward within consumption on a weekly or within a moment of time. But over the long term, we'll start to see some more consistency there." Langhans added that portfolio-level tightening will happen faster than brand-by-brand; LTO launch timing (Lime Slush for Alani, upcoming CELSIUS LTOs) will drive data spikes; cited 17% space gains for CELSIUS and Alani triple-digit expansion as drivers.

- **Guidance:**
  - **Shelf Reset Timing:** "historically, we've seen them really materialize through and kind of finalize right around the end of spring" — End of Spring 2026
  - **Alani Convenience Focus:** "biggest gains, especially for Alani, would be in convenience"
  - **CELSIUS Space Gains:** "17% space gains that we had as well"

### Question 2 — $25M Net Benefit Unpacking & Alani Inventory Unwind

- **Q: Peter Grom (UBS):** Asked for brand-level breakdown of the $25M net timing benefit; specifically probed whether Alani inventory would remain elevated and unwind in a 4Q/1Q 2027 dynamic similar to CELSIUS's 2022 Pepsi transition, or whether under-shipment occurs faster.
  - "When we think about the $25 million net benefit from CELSIUS versus Alani, can you help us unpack what that looks like from a brand perspective? And then I guess, Jarrod, maybe more specifically as you think about Alani, would you expect inventory levels to remain elevated as we move through this transition? And similar to kind of what we saw when CELSIUS moved into the Pepsi system a couple of years ago, implying that maybe more of the unwind would be a 4Q, into '27 dynamic? Or would you anticipate maybe kind of some under-shipment to occur faster?"

- **A: Jarrod Langhans, CFO:** Emphasized commitment to tightening peaks/valleys under Pepsi captaincy; disclosed supply chain team visit in January. Described Q4 mechanics: at end of Q4, adjusted an additional week of brand CELSIUS shipments while loading in additional Alani, producing the $25M net benefit. Said brand CELSIUS 2026 orders "align more closely to the tracked data" in initial deliveries but caveated "4 to 5 more weeks in the quarter." Cited 17% CELSIUS and triple-digit Alani 2026 space gains as validation.

- **KPIs:**
  - **Portfolio Net Benefit:** ~$25M in Q4 2025
  - **CELSIUS Q1 2026 Scanner Alignment:** "orders align more closely to the tracked data" in initial 2026 deliveries
- **Guidance:**
  - **Q1 2026 Partial Signal:** "I will caveat that by saying there are 4 to 5 more weeks in the quarter"
  - **CELSIUS 2026 Space Gains:** +17%
  - **Alani 2026 Space Gains:** Triple-digit

### Question 3 — Gross Margin Phasing & Midwest Premium Impact

- **Q: Bonnie Herzog (Goldman Sachs):** Asked about Midwest aluminum premium impact, phasing of gross margins through 2026, and longer-term margin evolution beyond 2026.
  - "I guess I had a question on gross margins. You mentioned you expect your gross margins to return to a more normalized profile, in the mid-50% range, across this year. So maybe first, could you touch on the potential impact that the Midwest premium is having on your business near term? And then second, can you give us a sense of phasing gross margins this year? And then I guess beyond this year, how should we think about the evolution of your margins over the next few years? Can you highlight maybe some of the key puts and takes that we should think about?"

- **A: John Fieldly, CEO / Jarrod Langhans, CFO:** Fieldly cited orbit model, supply chain, purchasing strategies, and vertical integration via co-packer acquisition as levers. Langhans materially reframed the margin target: "our target for this year is to get back to a more normalized low 50s. In terms of the opportunity, we do see ability to move up into the mid-50s like you noted. I wouldn't necessarily call that a '26 target, but definitely a near-term target, into the next handful of years." Provided integration-completion cadence (Alani Q1, Rockstar Q2), cited orbit model, freight, mix, and roll-off of Q4 one-time COGS write-offs/scrap as benefits. Flagged Midwest premium and tariffs as variables that could accelerate or delay the recovery.

- **Guidance:**
  - **2026 Gross Margin Target:** Low 50s (not mid-50s)
  - **Mid-50s Target:** Multi-year, "next handful of years"
  - **Alani Full Integration:** End Q1 2026
  - **Rockstar Full Integration:** End Q2 2026
  - **Q4 One-Time Drags:** COGS write-offs and scrap roll-off in Q1 2026
- **Drivers / Commentary:**
  - **Margin Levers:** "Some other things that are going to benefit us is our orbit model and our freight structure... Our mix, when you kind of look at a blended mix of our price pack and promotion strategy, will also be beneficial."
  - **Macro Variables:** "depending upon where the Midwest premium goes, there could be some impact in terms of timing. And as well as tariffs, if the tariffs kind of subside quicker, then there's an opportunity to get to some of those numbers quicker."

### Question 4 — Portfolio SKU Rationalization, LTO Cadence, Velocity & Retailer View

- **Q: Andrea Teixeira (JPMorgan):** Multi-part: asked about 3-brand SKU trimming opportunities, LTO cadence experience with CELSIUS, velocity dynamics with expanded shelf space, competitive innovation environment, and margin synergies/Pepsi visibility.
  - "I was hoping to see, John, if we step back and think about the 3-brand portfolio and the opportunities of trimming at some point the SKUs and -- or you think that this cadence of LTOs now with Celsius, like how is the experience that you've had? And how do you think velocity, obviously, with the increase in shelf space, you obviously will have a reduction in velocity at some point, but just thinking of how to position the SKUs, how to position the category? ... just a clarification on the margin. It's very encouraging to see that you see the opportunity for synergies and improvements in the execution. I also was encouraged to hear from you guys at CAGNY in terms of the systems and visibility."

- **A: John Fieldly, CEO:** Framed innovation as bringing new consumers/occasions to the category, citing social occasions as a key white space ("alcohol and liquor come into some challenges and headwinds... consumers are switching to energy drinks"). Said portfolio approach allows per-channel/region SKU maximization. Space gains (+17% CELSIUS, +100% Alani) drive velocity focus; LTOs lift the core and build daily-routine frequency. Highlighted female consumer as a structural tailwind. Referenced vertical integration, purchasing strategy, and RGM investment for margin expansion.

- **Drivers / Commentary:**
  - **Alcohol Switch:** "consumers are switching to energy drinks to -- as a replacement. And that's a huge opportunity with our CELSIUS mocktails and dirty Alanis that we have out there."
  - **Portfolio SKU Management:** "We're able to really maximize the value of the portfolio now with CELSIUS, Alani and Rockstar, making sure we're maximizing the SKUs really for the channel and also for a regional basis."
  - **Female Consumer:** "The other big area we see a huge opportunity is with the female consumer. That's a big opportunity."
  - **Velocity Focus:** "Velocity is very important in the category. That's what is going to continue to drive it."
  - **RGM:** "Revenue management, RGM, is a really big component as we maximize the value. We're not just a singular brand anymore going on promotion against many other brands."

### Question 5 — Distribution Source, Shelf Space Sourcing & Velocity with TDP Expansion

- **Q: Kevin Grundy (BNP Paribas):** Three-part: (1) quantify category-level distribution gains, (2) identify where the Alani/CELSIUS shelf gains are sourced from within the category vs. adjacent categories, (3) how to think about velocity growth vs. TDP gains in 2026.
  - "Number one, what -- can you quantify what you sort of estimate the distribution gains to be for the category given the strength? ... Number two, where are the shelf space gains coming from for Alani and CELSIUS? ... just lastly, I think Andrea was sort of touching on this, with respect to velocity."

- **A: John Fieldly, CEO:** Pointed to retailer behavior — convenience retailers optimizing beer coolers, juice and premium water categories under pressure. Cited expanded energy space as the energy category grows as a share of LRB. Large format expanding space too. On velocity, acknowledged Alani new distribution will dilute initial velocities: "we will likely see a lower velocity entering new segments of the regions, within also channels and retailers, that we're going to have to build up those velocities." Emphasized velocity as a major KPI and LTO/innovation as trial/awareness drivers before building daily routine.

- **Drivers / Commentary:**
  - **Space Sources:** "in the convenience channel, we're hearing from a lot of retailers, they're optimizing some of the beer coolers... Juice category as well, and high premium waters as well has been under pressure."
  - **Convenience Share:** "convenience has been a main driver of that, over 60% of sales."
  - **Velocity Expectation:** "Each channel is going to be different. Each market is going to be different. But any time, just like when we saw CELSIUS, as you expand out broader, we did see reduced velocities as that expansion takes place."

### Question 6 — Alani Pull-Forward Check & Alani-CELSIUS Transition Comparison

- **Q: Gerald Pascarelli (Needham):** Two-part: (1) whether any part of the Alani Q4 inventory benefit should be considered a pull-forward in revenue; (2) how the 102% Alani 2026 shelf-space growth compares to the CELSIUS 2022 transition, including whether share-taking dynamics (e.g., Bang) differ.
  - "Are there any parts of the inventory benefit that Alani got this quarter that should in any way be considered a pull forward in revenue? ... going back to the shelf space growth that you're expecting for Alani this year, is there a way for you to broadly contextualize that in terms of what we saw for core CELSIUS back in 2022 when that brand transitioned?"

- **A: Jarrod Langhans, CFO / John Fieldly, CEO:** Langhans characterized Alani load-in as "more of an opportunity than a load-in" rather than a pull-forward — driven by how quickly Pepsi delivered ACV expansion. Acknowledged Cherry Bomb was loaded in at end of year and depleted quickly. Fieldly said CELSIUS entered at lower ACV than Alani in 2022; Alani has similar convenience opportunity. He noted CELSIUS took space from Bang in 2022, but Alani likely won't be replacing brands — category expansion is the source. Female consumer and usage-occasion expansion are unique 2026 dynamics not present in 2022. Cited NACS conference engagement and Pepsi's category captain role as retailer confidence drivers.

- **KPIs:**
  - **Alani vs CELSIUS Starting ACV:** "CELSIUS was at a lower ACV versus where Alani is"
- **Drivers / Commentary:**
  - **Opportunistic Load-In:** "I would call that more of an opportunity than a load-in, that we took advantage of."
  - **Category Expansion vs Replacement:** "although Alani will not likely be replacing brands, the category is expanding. We're hearing retailers expand their shelf presence for energy."
  - **Pepsi Captaincy:** "A lot of retailers really want to make sure you can keep those shelves full, especially with the velocity and how quickly these products turn. So that is really a show of confidence"
