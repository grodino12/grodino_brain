## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** Celsius Holdings closed 2023 with what management called "the best earnings year in our company's history," reporting Q4 revenue of approximately $347 million (up 95% YoY) and full-year revenue of just over $1.3 billion (up 102% / approximately $654 million YoY), making Celsius "truly a billion dollar brand." Positive drivers cited included near-complete US distribution (topping 98% ACV), share gains that made Celsius "the first company to break the 10 share barrier in more than a decade" (a record 11.5 share nationwide in MULOC in the four weeks ending February 11, 2024), continued benefit from full integration into the PepsiCo distribution system, strong non-tracked channel growth (Club sales up 64% in Q4 and 83.6% for the year; Amazon up 72.9% to $100.1 million), a robust innovation pipeline (Celsius Essentials, Vibe flavors, Fizz Free line), and gross margin expansion of more than 660 basis points for the full year to approximately 48%. Acknowledged headwinds were Q4 seasonality (management could not capitalize on display activity the way it can during the summer selling season, with display competition from seasonal items), a Q4 gross margin step-down from Q3 even after adjusting for a prior-quarter promotional allowances accrual, and ongoing internal-control remediation work with "a handful of areas" still needing time. The strategic outlook is confident and growth-oriented but deliberately cautious on guidance: management expects 2024 gross margin "fairly consistent with the Q4 and full-year margin profile," plans to keep sales and marketing spend as a percentage of sales similar in Q1 2024 (with a historical 22%–24% full-year range), and is pursuing methodical international expansion (Canada launched mid-January 2024, UK/Ireland via Suntory starting Q2). Overall tone was upbeat and execution-focused, emphasizing the move "beyond 10% market share," continued investment behind the brand (Ferrari/F1, MLS, Super Bowl activations), and disciplined, ROI-driven spending.

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - **Gross margin step-down and 2024 trajectory (Q1):** Analysts pressed on why Q4 gross margin stepped back from Q3 even after adjusting for the prior-quarter promotional allowances accrual, and how to model margin progression into 2024.
  - **Channel inventory / shipment-to-scan disconnect (Q1):** Mark Astrachan flagged an apparent under-shipment versus what scanner data plus disclosed Amazon and Costco sales implied, probing whether Celsius had under-shipped somewhere in the channel.
  - **Market share trajectory and the "next target" (Q2):** Analysts wanted color on share momentum from here, whether year-to-date improvement was driven by innovation versus shelf resets, and what the new share target is now that 10% has been passed.
  - **International contribution and the Suntory choice (Q2):** Analysts sought sizing of 2024 international revenue contribution, whether additional markets would open in 2024, and why Suntory was selected for the UK and Ireland.
  - **Sales & marketing run-rate, non-tracked channels, and Essentials ramp (Q4, Q5):** Analysts asked whether the 23% S&M rate was the right full-year 2024 assumption, what drove the food-service step-up in PepsiCo revenue, and how far the Celsius Essentials ACV ramp could go.

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - **Shipment vs. scan disconnect:** Mark Astrachan explicitly challenged whether Celsius under-shipped in Q4, citing a "fairly big disconnect" between implied and actual sell-in; management redirected to "days on hand" being "consistent and in good shape" and attributed the gap to seasonality and innovation pipe-fill rather than quantifying it (Q1).
  - **Margin step-down skepticism:** Mark Astrachan implied Q4 margin should have been better and asked whether one-offs were embedded; management characterized the result as something they were "pretty pleased with" while acknowledging "opportunities to improve going forward," without isolating the one-time items (Q1).
  - **Refusal to give forward share guidance:** Peter Grom pressed for a new share target above 10%; John Fieldly explicitly declined — "We're not going to give any forward guidance on the future share" — pointing instead to current trajectories in Amazon (~20 share) and 12 key markets ahead of 15 share (Q2).
  - **No commitment on price increases:** Gerald Pascarelli probed whether Celsius would follow competitor rate increases; John Fieldly stated "we're not going to make any comments on future price increases at this time," leaving potential margin upside unaddressed (Q5).

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **2024 spring shelf resets (Q1–Q2 2024):** Resets began in January and run through May; management expects incremental space, with planning conducted in Q3 2023 at a double-digit share — a key test of whether the Pepsi system translates share into shelf space.
  - **Celsius Essentials national ramp (2024):** The 16-ounce Essentials line reached 49% ACV by February 18, 2024 and is described as incremental (not cannibalizing); the summer reset read will indicate the size of this new platform.
  - **International market openings (2024):** Canada launched mid-January via Pepsi; UK/Ireland via Suntory beginning gradually in Q2; management signaled "additional international expansion this year" — an early test of the international playbook.
  - **Convenience-channel share gains:** Celsius is only at a 10 share in convenience (vs. ~16 in food, ~20 on Amazon); management calls convenience "the biggest opportunity" — a multi-quarter share catalyst.
  - **Non-tracked / food service expansion:** Food service is now more than 12.5% of PepsiCo sales (up from ~10%); management expects to know the size of the broader food-service / universities / hospitals opportunity "over the next 12 months to 24 months."

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** Management noted prior-year (2022) sales and marketing expense was elevated by costs associated with the termination of legacy distributors during the transition to the Pepsi network; reported S&M comparisons therefore require adjustment for those distributor termination expenses (2023 full-year S&M was 20% of sales vs. 24% prior year "adjusted for distributor termination expenses").
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Not explicitly framed by management. The 102% full-year revenue growth and 95% Q4 growth were achieved against a 2022 base ($654M full year, $178M Q4).
  - **Timing Shifts:** Management noted that in January 2024 Celsius "started rolling out all the innovation" across the US, having "filled the pipe" with innovation that offset reductions in legacy products on a net-net days-on-hand basis — i.e., innovation pipe-fill straddled the Q4 2023 / Q1 2024 boundary.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** 2024 shelf-space planning was conducted with retail partners in Q3 2023 when Celsius held a double-digit share — versus 2023 planograms created when Celsius held only an approximately 4.5 share — a structural tailwind to 2024 shelf space. Celsius placed more than 10,000 branded coolers in 2023 (up over 300% YoY) and drove a 60% increase in display activity; Celsius is now fully integrated into PepsiCo's annual planning cycle.
  - **Top Priority Follow-Up Questions:** See STEP 4 follow-up question block below.

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** No FX impact was quantified. On pricing, management declined to comment on future price increases despite a competitor having taken a rate increase; Celsius positions as a "premium-priced product" managing price gaps via pricing/promotional architecture by channel and pack size rather than frontline pricing.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** New product lines materially changed the portfolio mix entering 2024 — Celsius Essentials (16-oz, six SKUs, 49% ACV by mid-February 2024), new Vibe flavors (Astro Vibe, Galaxy Vibe, Cosmic Vibe), and the Fizz Free non-carbonated line. Management said Essentials is bringing in new consumers and is "not cannibalizing existing sales."
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** Management flagged 2023 progress on internal-control remediation (IT general controls remediated, more robust COSO environment) as disclosed in the 10-K issued the morning of the call, with "a handful of areas" still needing additional time — a reporting/control consideration rather than a quantified financial impact.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Management explicitly cited Q4 seasonality: "we do tend to see some seasonality within the fourth quarter relative to the third quarter," driven by reduced display activity (competition from seasonal items) and inventory-on-hand pressure across mass and grocery customers; this is a recurring Q4 pattern rather than a one-time anomaly.

**Q (Good):** "Of the sequential gross margin decline from Q3 to Q4 2023, how many basis points were attributable to the Celsius Essentials launch and innovation mix versus other factors?"

- **Rationale:** Mark Astrachan asked whether one-offs were embedded in the Q4 margin; management cited the Essentials launch and innovation activity qualitatively but did not quantify the basis-point impact, which is necessary to model 2024 margin progression.
- **Question Type:** Presumptive Question with Component Breakdown
- **Question Topic(s):** Gross margin, innovation mix, Celsius Essentials
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 1
- **Question Importance Rank:** 1

**Topic: Shipment-to-Scan Reconciliation**

**Q (Good):** "You said total days on hand with your largest distributor were 'consistent and in good shape' as of 12/31 — in dollar terms, how large was the legacy-product inventory reduction that the innovation pipe-fill offset?"

- **Rationale:** Mark Astrachan flagged a "fairly big disconnect" between implied and actual Q4 sell-in; management answered net-net qualitatively, leaving the gross legacy reduction and innovation fill unquantified.
- **Question Type:** Context Reinstatement + Component Breakdown
- **Question Topic(s):** Channel inventory, days on hand, shipment timing
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 1
- **Question Importance Rank:** 2

**Q (Good):** "Your outlook is for 2024 gross margin 'fairly consistent' with the ~48% Q4 and full-year profile — what specific raw material or packaging cost assumptions underpin that, and at what aluminum or freight level would the framework break?"

- **Rationale:** Management cited macro uncertainty as the reason for not guiding higher; the specific commodity assumptions behind "fairly consistent" were not disclosed.
- **Question Type:** Assumptive Question with Scenario Testing
- **Question Topic(s):** Gross margin outlook, commodity costs
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 1
- **Question Importance Rank:** 3

- **Question Type:** Empathetic Prologue + Precision Technique

- **Topic Importance Rank:** 2

**Q (Good):** "Essentials reached 49% ACV by February 18, 2024 — at what ACV level do you expect the line to stabilize by year-end 2024, and what gates further distribution gains?"
