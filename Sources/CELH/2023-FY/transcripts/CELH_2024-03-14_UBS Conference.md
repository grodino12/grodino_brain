## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** In a fireside chat with UBS analyst Peter Grom, Celsius's CMO Kyle Watson and Chief of Staff Toby David framed Celsius's emergence as a genuine third competitive force in an energy category long dominated by Monster and Red Bull, attributing differentiation to a fitness-centric, "better-for-you," premium brand DNA, a broad 50/50 male/female demographic, and a route to market that began in fitness and vitamin-specialty channels rather than convenience. Positive drivers cited included near-full tracked-channel distribution (98% ACV), substantial runway in breadth of distribution (Celsius averages ~16 SKUs per location versus ~25 for Red Bull/Monster), the PepsiCo distribution partnership unlocking incremental placements and food service / college-campus access, ~80% of new consumers being incremental to the energy category, and a constructive view that energy as a category is taking shelf space from shrinking adjacent categories (coffee, CSDs, dairy, tea, water). Acknowledged headwinds were elevated promotional spend tied to the Celsius Essentials 16-oz national rollout (a deliberate "short-term pain for long-term gain" to induce trial), a Q4 gross margin viewed as "less robust" than some hoped, the competitive re-entry of Bang under Monster, and the structural reality that Celsius's convenience tracked-share (~58% of tracked sales through convenience versus ~70% for peers) lags because convenience was its last channel entered. The strategic outlook is confident but explicitly avoids formal guidance: management reiterated a long-stated belief it can reach a Monster-like ~55% gross margin profile (Monster ~59%, adjusted for the hop-on-freight component) and operating margins in the mid-30s over time, expects S&M as a percent of sales (Q4 2023 at 23%, historical 22%–24%, ~20% over the prior five-to-six quarters) to lever down eventually but "not right now," and expects spring shelf resets to "stair-step" up monthly through May. Overall tone was assertive, competitive, and brand-confident.

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - **What Celsius did differently and who its consumer is:** Peter Grom opened by asking how Celsius succeeded where prior beverage-company entrants failed, who the core customer is, and where new consumers are being sourced from (Q1, Q2, Q3).
  - **Distribution mix, food service sizing, and the Pepsi relationship:** Grom probed the tracked-vs-non-tracked channel mix, how big food service can ultimately be, and the health of the PepsiCo partnership (Q4, Q5, Q6).
  - **2024 growth building blocks and the shelf-reset timeline:** Grom pressed on TDP versus velocity as growth drivers, whether reset optimism is shared by competitors, and exactly when reset benefits would appear in scanner data (Q7, Q8, Q9, Q10).
  - **Market share glide path and competitive threat from Bang:** Grom repeatedly tried to extract a market share trajectory toward mid-teens and asked how the Bang relaunch under Monster could affect momentum (Q11, Q12, Q13).
  - **Margins — gross margin subdued near term and the long-term target:** Grom focused on why Q1 gross margin is subdued, whether a Monster-like 60%+ US gross margin is achievable, and where S&M can go over time (Q15, Q16, Q17).

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - **Refusal to give share or margin guidance:** Peter Grom explicitly said "I'm going to still try to get it out of you anyway Toby"; Toby David repeatedly declined to give a market share target or a precise margin/S&M timeline, offering "color" instead (Q11, Q16, Q17).
  - **Whether shelf-reset optimism is differentiated:** Grom challenged management's constructive reset commentary by noting "a lot of your competitors are also sounding pretty constructive"; Toby David reframed it as the whole energy category gaining space from other categories, and said Celsius targets "wounded gazelles" rather than Red Bull/Monster space (Q9).
  - **Competitive threat from Bang's relaunch:** Grom flagged "concern from at least investors that I speak with" that the Bang relaunch under Monster could alter Celsius's momentum; Kyle Watson said Celsius is "paranoid about everything" but "not as concerned," and Toby David said he would be "amazed" if Bang recaptured its prior ~10% share (Q12).
  - **Gross margin credibility:** Grom said Q4 gross margin was "a little maybe less robust than maybe some people were hoping"; management attributed the softness to above-the-line promotional spend on the Essentials launch and stood by the long-term Monster-like target without committing to timing (Q15).

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **Spring shelf resets completing by end of May 2024:** Management said the total planogram reset will "stair-step" up monthly through May (Circle K is a May reset) — the key near-term driver of breadth-of-distribution gains and a test investors should see in two-to-four-week scanner data.
  - **Celsius Essentials national rollout (Q1 2024):** The 16-oz line, exclusive at 7-Eleven in Q4 2023, rolled out nationally in Q1 2024 (~40% ACV reached quickly per the earnings call) — its trajectory will indicate the size of the new platform and the duration of elevated promotional spend.
  - **UK and Ireland launch (Q2 2024):** Celsius will enter the UK — "one of the largest energy drink markets in the world" — via Suntory in Q2; this is the first major from-zero international market test of the brand playbook.
  - **Q1 2024 earnings call update on Canada and South Florida share:** Management said it will likely give fuller color on the Canada launch and update the South Florida share figure (last cited at 24% on Q3 data) at the Q1 earnings call.
  - **Independent convenience expansion:** Management cited ~250,000 independent convenience stores as a "massive opportunity" reachable through the Pepsi system — a multi-year breadth-of-distribution catalyst.

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** Not specifically quantified. Management noted Q4 2023 S&M ticked up to 23% (from ~20% over the prior five-to-six quarters), reflecting infrastructure investment — a structurally higher spend base than recent quarters.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Not explicitly framed by management.
  - **Timing Shifts:** Management noted spring shelf resets occur primarily in March–April with a meaningful Circle K reset in May; reset benefits will appear progressively in scanner data through the end of May 2024 rather than at a single point.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** Celsius is at 98% ACV in tracked channels but only ~16 SKUs per location versus ~25 for Red Bull/Monster, and ~58% of tracked sales come through convenience versus ~70% for peers — both gaps framed as growth runway. The Celsius Essentials launch drove elevated promotional activity (a near-full-quarter promotion at 7-Eleven in Q4 2023, continuing nationally in Q1 2024). Management said energy as a category is gaining retail space from shrinking adjacent categories.
  - **Top Priority Follow-Up Questions:** See STEP 4 follow-up question block below.

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** No FX impact quantified. On pricing, Toby David said Red Bull has taken price and noted Celsius is watching peers, but "that doesn't mean we're going to take price" — pricing can be toggled via promotional depth (e.g., "two for $4" versus "two for $4.50") to find the margin "sweet spot."
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** The Celsius Essentials 16-oz line is the key portfolio addition — launched exclusively at 7-Eleven in Q4 2023 and rolled out nationally in Q1 2024. Management also said it is exploring a lower-caffeine product (versus the current 200 mg offering) as a potential incremental SKU, without committing to a timeline.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** Toby David flagged an accounting comparability nuance versus Monster: Monster's reported gross margin includes a hop-on-freight component; adjusting for it, Monster's ~59% gross margin compares to roughly 55% on a like basis for Celsius — i.e., the headline Monster gross margin overstates the gap.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Toby David argued energy-drink demand is economically resilient, citing the 2008 downturn when energy showed little slowdown because consumers cut discretionary spend (dining out) before their daily energy drink — i.e., management does not view consumer budget tightness as a driver of the promotional margin pressure. On GLP-1 / weight-loss drugs, management said it views Celsius as "the natural fit" for that consumer given its healthy-halo positioning and is not concerned.

## STEP 8: Macroeconomic & Thematic Commentary

##### Energy Category Resilience in Economic Downturns

- "So for us, I don't think we're looking necessarily at the economic situation, people are buying -- people are buying energy drinks, even back in 2008 when there was a catastrophic economic environment. You didn't really see much of a slowdown in energy because one of the things people will sacrifice is going out to dinners...
