## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** Celsius Holdings reported a strong start to 2019, with total revenue up 20% to $14.5 million, led by North America revenue growth of 41% to a record $11.4 million. Excluding China, revenue from continuing operations rose 35%. Management emphasized aggressive distribution expansion: Target grew to over 1,200 stores (four SKUs, expanding to 1,500 in April/May), CVS rolled out nationally from a 550-store test to over 7,500 stores, and new entry Rite Aid reached 2,300+ of 3,000 stores — together giving Celsius ~11,000 drug-channel locations. New DSD distribution agreements (Anheuser-Busch, Keurig Dr. Pepper, MillerCoors network partners) were secured, and subsequent to quarter-end Celsius signed a transformative agreement with Big Geyser, New York's largest independent non-alcoholic beverage distributor (20,000+ locations). The China royalty restructuring with Qifeng Food Technology was finalized, with Celsius recognizing a $12.2 million note receivable as other income, driving GAAP net income of $11.7 million ($0.20/share) versus a prior-year loss. Adjusted EBITDA turned positive at $878,000 versus a $2.1 million loss in Q1 2018. Headwinds acknowledged were an ~$900,000 international revenue decline (a $1.3 million China revenue reduction from the model shift partly offset by 20% European growth), persistent Target out-of-stocks from warehouse replenishment limitations, gross margin holding flat at 39.5% with near-term promotional pressure from new-account launches, and a cash decline to $2.8 million from $7.7 million on inventory build and China-related settlements. Management's tone was confident and growth-focused, highlighting a refocusing of working capital on North America and an energy-category transformation that is opening retailer doors.
- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - The Big Geyser New York City distribution agreement — its strategic significance, rollout cadence, and margin implications (Questions 1, 4).
  - Trends at Target and CVS, the out-of-stock issue, the move toward a DSD model, and other new marquee accounts (Question 1).
  - Co-packer setup and inventory readiness ahead of the peak summer selling season (Question 2).
  - International modeling ex-China, the Nordic turnaround, and the path of China royalty/interest income (Questions 3, 5, 6).
  - Gross margin trajectory, the share-count jump from preferred conversion, market share/ACV, and competitive dynamics (Questions 6, 7, 8).
- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - Anthony Vendetti probed whether Big Geyser — described as "a pretty tough negotiator" — would carry below-corporate gross margin; management pushed back, saying margins would be "very much consistent with other distributors" with no expected deterioration (Question 8).
  - Anthony Vendetti pressed for China unit-volume detail (up or down versus Q1 2018); management declined, saying it has not historically disclosed unit volumes and reframed the China revenue drop as purely the model-shift recognition change (Question 7).
  - Jeffrey Cohen probed gross margin direction; the CFO offered a 39%-to-low-40s range while the CEO cautioned the company would likely run "on the lower end of those ranges" near-term due to heavier promotions on new DSD launches (Question 6).
  - Jeff Van Sinderen and Jeffrey Cohen probed the durability of the European/Nordic turnaround; management characterized it as "stabilization" rather than expansion, given capital is being directed to North America (Questions 3, 5).
- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **Big Geyser New York City rollout (2019)** : Management described it as "one of the strongest and most transformative agreements to date"; phased rollout to 20,000+ locations starting with grocery, drug, and 7-Eleven is a major distribution catalyst.
  - **Target shift toward a DSD model** : Management is discussing moving some Target regions to DSD to fix recurring out-of-stocks; resolving this unlocks suppressed sell-through.
  - **Target cold-availability and additional SKU (back half of 2019)** : Management is in talks with Target on cold placement and adding another flavor toward the back half of the year.
  - **CVS national rollout and additional drug-channel chains** : CVS is rolling out nationally; Walgreens discussions are underway, with "significant opportunities" in other drugstore chains.
  - **Additional marquee account announcements (convenience and grocery)** : Management said several more marquee accounts would be announced over the coming months.
  - **Fuji Apple Pear national rollout** : New flavor launched in the fitness channel in Q1; national rollout anticipated on the next planogram resets.
- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** Q1 2019 consolidated revenue growth of 20% was depressed by the China model shift; the prior-year Q1 2018 included $1.3 million of China product revenue that does not recur, so ex-China continuing-operations growth was 35%. Going forward, China will be reported as royalty plus interest income rather than product revenue.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Analyst Jeffrey Cohen flagged that Q2 2019 international faces "a super easy comp off last year's $0.77 million for the second quarter," implying favorable second-quarter international comparisons.
  - **Timing Shifts:** Management noted inventory was at "about the highest they've been," strategically built to fill pipe orders for new customers and the summer beverage season — a working-capital pull-forward into Q1.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** A wave of new distribution came on in Q1/post-quarter — CVS (national), Rite Aid (new), Target (expanding to 1,500), DICK'S Sporting Goods, Food Lion, plus DSD agreements (Anheuser-Busch, Keurig Dr. Pepper, MillerCoors) and the Big Geyser agreement. Management quantified a "billboard effect": adding SKUs from two to three nearly doubles sales, and moving from two to four SKUs can quadruple sales in an account.
  - **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** Management stated revenue growth was driven by sales volume "as opposed to increases in product pricing"; pricing, discounts, and allowances were running at the same levels as the prior-year Q1. China royalty/interest income is denominated in Chinese local currency, introducing translation effects.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** The China business model shifted to royalty licensing with Qifeng — Celsius now recognizes a $12.2 million note receivable, an ~$6.9 million fixed licensing fee over five years (then volume-based royalty), and ~$90,000–$95,000 of quarterly interest income, rather than China product revenue. RTDs drive ~85% of revenue; powder products are a secondary point of distribution.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** Total other income rose ~$12.2 million in Q1 2019 from recognition of the China note receivable, the primary driver of the $11.7 million GAAP net income; this gain is backed out of adjusted EBITDA. The share count jumped to ~57.2 million weighted average (from ~47.4 million in Q1 2018) primarily on the conversion of ~6 million Carl DeSantis preferred shares at year-end 2018, removing all preferred stock from the balance sheet.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Management noted the company typically sees "a really big pickup" in volume around this time of year heading into beverage season.

#### Core Questioning Principles

**Topic: Big Geyser Revenue Contribution**

**Q (Good):** "Of Big Geyser's 20,000-plus New York metro locations, how many do you expect to have Celsius authorized and stocked by the end of 2019?"

- **Rationale:** Management called Big Geyser transformative and described a phased rollout but gave no quantified location target for 2019, which is essential to size the New York contribution.
- **Question Type:** Component Breakdown with Time-Bounding
- **Question Topic(s):** Big Geyser, distribution rollout, New York market
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 1
- **Question Importance Rank:** 1

**Topic: Target Out-of-Stocks**

**Q (Good):** "What percentage of Target stores do you expect to convert from warehouse replenishment to a DSD model by the start of the summer selling season?"

- **Rationale:** Management acknowledged out-of-stocks persisted at Target and that DSD conversion is under discussion, but gave no scope or timeline for the conversion.
- **Question Type:** Precision Technique with Time-Bounding
- **Question Topic(s):** Target, out-of-stocks, DSD conversion, route-to-market
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 1
- **Question Importance Rank:** 2

**Topic: Gross Margin Path**

**Q (Good):** "Of the promotional spend pressuring gross margin toward the lower end of the 39%-to-low-40s range, what dollar amount is one-time new-account launch promotion versus ongoing trade spend?"

- **Rationale:** Management said margins would run on the low end near-term due to new-DSD-launch promotions but did not separate one-time launch costs from recurring trade spend.
- **Question Type:** Component Breakdown
- **Question Topic(s):** Gross margin, promotional spend, new-account launches
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 2
- **Question Importance Rank:** 3

**Topic: Cash Runway**

**Q (Good):** "With cash at $2.8 million at quarter-end and inventory at peak levels, at what point in 2019 do you expect operating cash flow to turn positive and rebuild the cash balance?"

- **Rationale:** Cash fell to $2.8 million from $7.7 million with $6.8 million used in operations in the quarter; a specific cash-inflection timeline is needed to assess liquidity.
- **Question Type:** Scenario Testing with Time-Bounding
- **Question Topic(s):** Liquidity, operating cash flow, working capital
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 2
- **Question Importance Rank:** 4

**Topic: China Royalty Recognition**

**Q (Good):** "Of the ~$6.9 million fixed licensing fee from Qifeng, what amount will be recognized as revenue in fiscal 2019 versus the volume-based royalty that follows the five-year term?"

- **Rationale:** Management described the licensing-fee and note-receivable structure but did not specify the 2019 royalty revenue recognition, leaving the China P&L contribution unclear.
- **Question Type:** Component Breakdown with Precision Technique
- **Question Topic(s):** China royalty, revenue recognition, Qifeng agreement
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 3
- **Question Importance Rank:** 5
