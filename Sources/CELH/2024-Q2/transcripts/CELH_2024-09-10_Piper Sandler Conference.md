## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** Toby David, attending solo, used the Piper Sandler conference primarily to provide additional transparency on the Pepsi inventory situation first disclosed "last week" at the Barclays conference. The dominant message: PepsiCo — Celsius's largest individual customer at ~55%+ of North America purchases — purchased approximately $100 million to $120 million less in Q3 2024 versus Q3 2023, because Pepsi had over-stocked Celsius product in 2023 (its first full year as distributor) and is now drawing inventory down to a normalized, optimized level ("cash is king"). David stressed this is an order/timing issue, not a demand issue: Celsius scanner growth is ~10% QTD year-over-year with market share up close to a point, and depletions out of Pepsi warehouses match scanner data. Positive drivers cited were Celsius's strong sugar-free positioning (sugar-free now 50% of category, Celsius the #2 brand with ~25% sugar-free share), a broad, fruit-forward flavor assortment that contrasts with Red Bull/Monster "super SKUs," large 2024 shelf-space gains, strong Amazon performance (#1 or #2 over 18 months, ~20+ share), strong U.S. markets (South Florida mid-20s share, NYC ~20%), and early international traction (Canada to a ~5 share / #4 brand within months; positive UK Tesco reads). Acknowledged headwinds were a stagnant energy category for the past three to four months, macro pressure on a "fragile" consumer that disproportionately hurts Celsius given its over-index on new-to-category consumers, a flat ~11-12 market share for several months, near-term Q3 margin "noise" from the Pepsi order shortfall, aggressive competitor promotional activity (Monster BOGOs), and sales-and-marketing expense (21-23%) well above Monster's. The strategic outlook was confident and patient: management expects the category to normalize, frames 2025 as the recovery year given 2024 space gains, and reaffirmed a return to its margin "framework" by Q4. Tone was candid, transparent and measured, with a clear emphasis on shipments-versus-consumption mechanics and a long-term Monster-comp benchmark.

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - **Pepsi inventory situation and Q4 trajectory:** The analyst opened on the "hot topic" — how the inventory situation evolved and whether further adjustments could occur in Q4 (Q1, Q2).
  - **Category dynamics — sugar vs. sugar-free:** The analyst asked how the slowing category breaks down between sugared and sugar-free energy and how Celsius is positioned (Q3).
  - **Shelf resets, assortment breadth and distribution trajectory:** The analyst probed reset mechanics, how a broad assortment compares to Red Bull/Monster super-SKUs, and where distribution goes from here (Q4).
  - **Market-share headroom and benchmarks:** The analyst asked how to benchmark the ~11-12% share and where it could go (Q5).
  - **International progress, promotional environment, margin upside, innovation and food service:** The analyst covered the international ramp (Canada, UK, Australia/New Zealand), the U.S. promotional environment, margin upside versus Monster, the role of innovation and adjacencies, Pepsi-relationship limits, and the food service opportunity (Q6-Q11).

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - **Shipments vs. consumption disconnect:** The central tension was the gap between Pepsi orders (down ~$100-120M YoY in Q3) and actual register sell-through (scanner +~10% QTD); management repeatedly stressed "their orders are not matching up with what's actually happening at the register" (Q1).
  - **Limited Q4 visibility:** Management explicitly declined to discuss Q4, citing no visibility into Pepsi's new dynamic ordering system: "we're not prepared to talk about Q4 yet because we just don't have visibility into it" — hoping "the bigger swings are behind us" but unwilling to commit (Q2).
  - **Disputed promotional-intensity data:** Management pushed back on Nielsen-cited data suggesting Celsius is "over promotional," arguing Nielsen counts off-shelf displays (where Celsius over-indexes) as promotions and that Celsius actually promotes less deeply than peers (Q7).
  - **Margin comparability with Monster:** Management argued the Monster gross-margin comparison is distorted because Monster historically excluded outbound freight from gross margin (a ~4% difference) — a nuance the analyst partly contested, noting Monster does record outbound freight, in SG&A (Q8, Q9).
  - **Flat share during the category slump:** Management acknowledged share has been "relatively flat for the last three and a half, four months" and that it has been "difficult to really make headwind and grow during this time period" (Q5).

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **Q4 2024 — return to margin framework:** Management said Q4 should see the Pepsi noise largely "gone" and a return to the margin framework "people have seen" — the first signal that the inventory disruption is normalizing.
  - **Category normalization / 2025 recovery:** Management is "very confident" the category normalizes and frames 2025 as the year Celsius capitalizes on its 2024 space gains.
  - **2025 retailer space negotiations:** "Very positive" ongoing retailer meetings for 2025; management would not issue space guidance but flagged the possibility of energy gaining shelf space from weaker categories.
  - **International proof points — UK success:** Management said UK success would draw investor attention, since international (~5% of sales vs. ~40% for Monster) is largely not incorporated into Celsius's valuation; more international announcements were promised "pretty soon."
  - **Australia & New Zealand launches (next couple of months):** Imminent Suntory-partnered market entries.
  - **Innovation pipeline (Essentials and adjacencies):** U.S. growth to come from TDP expansion, velocity and innovation; management is evaluating adjacent categories and "white space" within the Pepsi portfolio.

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** The defining tough comp is the Pepsi over-stocking in 2023: "They were holding quite a bit of product back in 2023, which was their first full year with Celsius" — making Q3 2023 an inflated shipment base against which Q3 2024 orders fall ~$100-120M short.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Not explicitly framed; management positioned 2025 as a recovery year rather than an easy-comp tailwind.
  - **Timing Shifts:** The Pepsi inventory drawdown is the central timing distortion — ~$100-120M less ordered in Q3 2024 vs. Q3 2023, decoupling reported revenue from register sell-through. Q4 visibility is limited because Pepsi's dynamic ordering system is new.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** Celsius secured large 2024 shelf-space gains; velocity is "a little bit challenged at times" as new distribution fills out. Spring resets occurred "a few months ago" for 2024; cut-ins and retailer-exclusive SKU additions occur through the year; most current retailer meetings are for 2025. Food service is ~12% of Pepsi sales (vending, colleges, universities, hospitals, hotels, restaurants).
  - **Top Priority Follow-Up Questions:** See STEP 4 follow-up question block below.

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** No FX quantified. On the promotional environment, management noted the category is ~26 weeks a year on promotion; Celsius is getting "promotionally more motivated this quarter" given a tight consumer, but with shallow-depth promotions (e.g., two for $4.50 instead of two for $4) primarily to secure off-shelf displays.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** International is ~5% of sales versus ~40% for Monster — a structural mix opportunity. Management emphasized Celsius is not contractually forced into any one distribution partner internationally (Pepsi is the "preferred" international partner; Suntory used in select markets), which it argued lets Celsius avoid Monster's U.S.-vs-international margin delta.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** Management flagged a key gross-margin comparability nuance versus Monster: "when you compare comps of Monster, they don't include outbound freight... as part of their gross margin, so that's like a 4% difference." (The analyst later clarified Monster does record outbound freight, within SG&A.) Q2 2024 Celsius gross profit was ~52%; S&M runs ~21-23% of revenue, well above Monster's.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Management described a "fragile" consumer and a stagnant energy category for the past three-to-four months — a "user intensification issue" of reduced purchase frequency and slower new-consumer entry, not category abandonment (no migration to coffee/tea/soda). Management noted most beverage categories are challenged, citing hydration as having had a recent run after being challenged four months prior.

#### Question 7 - International Market-Entry Approach

- **Q: Analyst (Piper Sandler)**: Asked whether the gym/fitness branding piece is the initial approach (e.g., in the UK, with Tesco and others to follow) and whether market entry is phased to build brand identity with a core consumer first.
  - "...is that the initial approach, like say, in the U.K., then let the Tesco's and some of the others to come? Is it a little bit phased in terms of building that brand identity...?"

- **A: Toby David, Chief of Staff**: David said it depends by market — Canada went nationwide quickly given U.S. proximity and marketing bleed-over, whereas other markets start with fitness as the early investment then a core partner retailer (e.g., Tesco in the UK). The rationale: going too wide too fast across many retailers requires creating pull-through everywhere or risks delisting; concentrating on one big retailer lets Celsius focus marketing resources, secures retailer lean-in via exclusivity (better placements, proximity, merchandising), and builds a selling story for broader distribution.
  - "...in many of the markets that we're going to be investing in, fitness is the early investment, but... relatively quickly, we choose to have maybe a core partner retailer that we partner with, like Tesco... if you try to go too wide and go into too many retailers at once... you're going to get delisted pretty quickly."
