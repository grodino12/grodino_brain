## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** Celsius management (CEO John Fieldly and CFO Jarrod Langhans, the latter dialing in from Portugal) used the Evercore conference to address head-on the central investor concern of a sharply decelerating energy-drink category in scanner data. Management's core message was that the category slowdown is real and "disappointing," driven by external factors (weather, delayed resets, gas-pump and rising-cost pressure on consumer spending), but that Celsius itself continues to grow — citing that if Celsius were backed out, the rest of the category would be in negative growth. Positive drivers cited included Celsius's 11.5% national four-week share (June 2 data), the brand up 35% in the latest four-week read, weekly Moolak scans rising from ~40 million in January to ~50 million recently, the largest reset season in company history nearing completion with significant Essentials 16-ounce space gains, and a strong multi-line portfolio (Core/Fruit Forward, Vibe, Fizz-Free, Essentials). The principal acknowledged headwind beyond the category was the Pepsi inventory optimization: the CFO disclosed roughly $20–30 million of Pepsi inventory optimization during the quarter that impacted net revenues, while stressing depletions out of the Pepsi system grew mid-teens versus Q1 and that Pepsi is now "fully" optimized. The strategic outlook was confident but measured: management reaffirmed an "upper 40s" full-year gross margin framework, framed the back half as a likely reset-driven scanner reacceleration into the busiest summer selling season, described a methodical multi-partner international playbook, and indicated M&A and buybacks are board-level discussions but not urgent. Overall tone was reassuring and execution-focused amid a tougher category backdrop.

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - **Category deceleration (Q1):** Robert Ottenstein opened by pressing management to explain why the energy-drink sector has slowed progressively this year and what is needed to turn it around.
  - **Shelf reset progress and timing (Q2, Q3):** The analyst probed how much of the resets are in market, whether they took longer than expected and why, and whether reset completion would reaccelerate scanner data.
  - **Portfolio segmentation and Essentials incrementality (Q4, Q5):** Ottenstein asked how Celsius segments the market across its brand families and pressed repeatedly for early metrics on Essentials' incrementality.
  - **Pricing strategy and zip-code differentiation (Q7):** The analyst asked how management balances volume growth versus pricing and whether differentiated pricing by geography is feasible.
  - **Pepsi inventory and second-half impact (Q11):** Relaying an investor question, the analyst asked how much the Pepsi inventory dynamics affected reported results and the likely second-half impact.
  - **Capital allocation / buybacks to smooth the stock (Q12):** Ottenstein asked about use of cash — M&A versus buybacks — including whether buybacks could offset scanner-driven stock volatility.

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - **Category slowdown vs. Celsius growth:** A central tension was reconciling a decelerating (even negative ex-Celsius) category with management's claim of continued Celsius growth; management leaned on four- and twelve-week reads as more reliable than volatile one-week reads (Q1).
  - **Pepsi inventory "noise" magnitude:** The CFO quantified ~$20–30 million of in-quarter Pepsi optimization that hit net revenues — a direct acknowledgment that reported revenue diverged from underlying demand — while asserting optimization is now essentially complete and the back half is "less of an issue" (Q11).
  - **Reset reacceleration confidence:** Management said reset completion plus marketing should reaccelerate scanner data but hedged — "that's the plan" — leaving the timing and magnitude of any rebound dependent on the category turning (Q3).
  - **Essentials incrementality with thin data:** Ottenstein repeatedly pressed for hard incrementality metrics on Essentials; management conceded the data is "super early" (longest placement ~five months at 7-Eleven) and offered only that Mintel/Numerator data shows "not a lot of cannibalization" (Q4, Q5).
  - **Pricing reticence:** Management explicitly declined to confirm whether a price increase is coming, with the CFO noting Celsius cannot do zip-code pricing the way larger peers might and only controls the price into its distributor (Q7).

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **Reset completion and scanner reacceleration (Summer 2024):** With resets ~85–90% done, management expects the consumer to see the new sets and marketing to drive a scanner reacceleration through summer — the key near-term test.
  - **Pepsi optimization completion (first half 2024):** The CFO expects Pepsi to "finish off their optimization" in the first half, making the back half "less of an issue" for comps — removing a reported-revenue overhang.
  - **NACS October 7, 2024:** Management framed NACS as the "finish line" of its summer playbook and a platform to drive the category into 2025.
  - **Summer innovation slate:** New innovation launching this summer (including the Fizz-Free line expansion and a Fizz-Free variety pack) is positioned to drive momentum.
  - **Pepsi DC-direct shift:** The CFO flagged a future opportunity to ship straight to Pepsi distribution centers (bypassing mixing centers), creating margin improvement on both ends — a multi-year structural margin catalyst.
  - **Capital deployment (M&A / buybacks):** Both are active board-level discussions; a buyback or bolt-on acquisition could become a catalyst, though neither is "of urgency."

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** The CFO flagged that Pepsi "took their inventory up last year" and "took their days on hand up last year" when Celsius was new in the system and growing fast — meaning the prior-year base was inflated by elevated Pepsi inventory, which is now being optimized down.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** The CFO indicated the back half of 2024 is "probably less of an issue when we're looking at comps" because Pepsi's optimization should be complete by the end of the first half — implying easier reported-revenue comparisons in H2 once the inventory drag anniversaries.
  - **Timing Shifts:** Management quantified roughly $20–30 million of Pepsi inventory optimization during the quarter that impacted net revenues; the CFO cautioned the optimization "is not a perfect amount" and Pepsi could "pull an extra million cases out" as growth continues. Shelf resets were delayed this year (grocery normally early January; convenience normally March/April) due to labor shortages, redistributing the reset-driven velocity benefit later into the year.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** The largest reset season in company history was ~85–90% complete, delivering significant additional space and better placements, with notable space gains for the Essentials 16-ounce line. Management noted mid-year cut-ins remain an opportunity. Pepsi system depletions grew mid-teens versus Q1. A future shift to shipping direct to Pepsi DCs (bypassing mixing centers) would carry less days-on-hand inventory, creating "nominal" noise but margin benefit.
  - **Distribution Detail:** Pepsi expanded Celsius's reach into colleges and universities; the Essentials line, launched at 7-Eleven, is rolling into Circle K, Walmart, and "just about every retailer in the country."

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** No FX discussion. On pricing, Red Bull took a mid-single-digit price increase in November 2023 and largely kept it; Monster was "talking about doing something later in the year." Management declined to confirm a Celsius price increase, citing rising oil/transportation costs and a premium-brand positioning that supports pricing where opportunities arise across pack, channel, slotting, and promotional levers.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** Celsius does not rely on a single hero SKU. The Core/Fruit Forward line leans on the better-for-you healthy halo; the Vibe line (Cosmic Vibe, Tropical Vibe) is more experiential with a more aggressive personality; the Fizz-Free line has three flavors (raspberry acai, peach mango green tea, blue raspberry lemonade) with a Fizz-Free variety pack coming, targeting cold-coffee and anti-carbonation consumers; the Essentials 16-ounce line (with essential aminos, replacing the older fitness-targeted Celsius Heat line) targets the 18–24 male consumer versus Celsius's historical 50/50 mix, addressing the ~50%/16-ounce share of the category.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** The CFO explained the days-on-hand methodology — current weekly depletions versus total inventory — noting that if days-on-hand stayed flat year-over-year it would actually be higher in absolute terms because it is based on growing current depletions.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Management cited weather as a factor in the category slowdown and noted consumers "starting to feel the pinch on the gas pump and all the rising costs," with "spending deep cuts" appearing in total beverage. Reset delays were attributed to labor shortages. July and August are described as the "best months of the year" seasonally.
