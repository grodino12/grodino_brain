## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** Celsius reported a record first quarter with revenue of $355.7 million, up 37% year-over-year, and management framed this as strong performance "especially after growing at a triple-digit for the past three consecutive years," noting that Celsius alone drove approximately 47% of total energy-category growth and reached an 11.5% MULOC share (a full point above Q4 2023 and ~4 points above the prior year). Positive drivers included a record gross margin of 51.2% (up from 43.8%, on reduced freight and raw-material costs), 81% adjusted EBITDA growth to ~$88 million, net income up 106% to $65 million ($0.27 per diluted share), Celsius Essentials reaching 54.5% ACV, continued non-tracked channel strength (Club +36%, Amazon +30%), and an emerging international footprint (UK/Ireland live as of April; Australia, France, New Zealand planned). The central acknowledged headwind was inventory movements by its largest customer (PepsiCo, ~62% of Q1 North America revenue): management said Q1 revenue would have been higher absent those inventory fluctuations, attributing the year-over-year variation to elevated Q1 2023 restocking that did not repeat in 2024, and explicitly told analysts not to "bake in a reversal." Other watch items were rising fuel and aluminum costs, the cost of a new March incentive program with Pepsi, and a higher promotional period entering "100 days of summer." The strategic outlook is confident but deliberately conservative on margin: management reiterated its February stance that "high 40s" gross margin is "very achievable" and declined to lock into the 50s, while expecting the full effect of spring shelf resets (one-third complete at quarter-end) to appear in scanner data beginning in July. Overall tone was upbeat, execution-focused, and explicitly aimed at the long-term goal of becoming the number-one global energy brand.

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - **Inventory dynamics and the shipment-to-scan gap:** Nearly every analyst probed the PepsiCo inventory fluctuation — whether the ~$20–25 million would reverse in Q2, whether Celsius is under-shipping demand, what the year-ago comp looks like, and whether further destocking is possible (Q1, Q2, Q5, Q7).
  - **Gross margin sustainability:** Analysts asked whether the 51.2% margin contained one-time items or was pure operating leverage, and how rising aluminum and fuel costs would flow through (Q1, Q4).
  - **Spring shelf resets and the path to track-data benefits:** Analysts wanted detail on where the most significant reset gains are (SKUs, facings, shelf quality), cooler plans, and the phasing of benefits given the July inflection (Q3, Q4, Q7).
  - **The new PepsiCo incentive program:** Analysts pressed on the rationale, what problem it solves, the cost ramp, and the margin implications (Q4, Q5).
  - **Category growth quality and the 31 drill-deep markets:** Analysts asked how much growth is new-to-category versus buy-rate intensification versus brand-shifting, and what is being done differently in the drill-deep markets (Q6).

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - **Under-shipment versus end demand:** Mark Astrachan explicitly said "we can all kind of see where end-demand is versus what your sales are and your sales are a lot lower," challenging the inventory narrative; management redirected to PepsiCo being at "really good inventory levels," strong scan data, and the record 11.5% share, without quantifying the gap (Q2).
  - **Whether the inventory gap persists all year:** Peter Grom pressed on whether the shipment-vs-scan gap would continue through 2024; Jarrod Langhans declined to predict PepsiCo's behavior, said April days-on-hand were consistent with March, and reiterated "I wouldn't bake in a reversal" (Q1, Q7).
  - **Margin guidance conservatism:** Kaumil Gajrawala noted margins were "substantially ahead of plan"; management would not "put the line in the sand" at the Q1 rate, citing rising fuel, aluminum, the Pepsi incentive program cost, and 100-days-of-summer promotional intensity — sticking with a "high 40s" framework despite the 51.2% print (Q1).
  - **The rationale for changing Pepsi terms amid strong momentum:** Michael Lavery asked what problem the new incentive program solves "because obviously there was great momentum in place"; management framed it as standard-practice alignment and incentivization rather than a fix, without specifying a concrete prior gap (Q5).

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **Full shelf-reset effect appearing in scanner data (beginning July 2024):** Management said spring resets — one-third complete at quarter-end, expected fully done by end of June — will deliver "the best shelf space gains in the company history," with the full effect visible in scanner data from July; this is the key near-term test of the breadth-of-distribution thesis.
  - **"100 days of summer" campaign (Q2–Q3 2024):** Celsius enters peak selling season with the best in-store presence in company history, double the sales staff year-over-year, new innovation, and major activations — a test of summer execution.
  - **New PepsiCo incentive program ramp (H1–H2 2024):** The March incentive program is expected to ramp over the first six-to-eight months and be "cruising" by the back half; its alignment effect on shelf and priorities is a catalyst, with an associated cost.
  - **International market openings (Q4 2024–2025):** UK and Ireland are live as of April; Australia, New Zealand, and France are planned to begin gradually in Q4 2024 with phased 2025 expansion.
  - **Convenience-channel share gains:** Management called convenience "the biggest opportunity" for spring resets, with examples like the Publix move from HPC into the energy set; closing the convenience gap is a multi-quarter catalyst.

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** Management flagged that Q1 2024 faces a difficult comparison because Q1 2023 included elevated PepsiCo restocking — restocking "meant to compensate for the fourth quarter 2022 destocking and to prepare for a robust spring resets that were planned in 2023" — which did not repeat in Q1 2024. The CFO quantified roughly $25 million of inventory buildup in Q1 2023. Separately, the club channel benefited in Q4 2023 from a strong Thanksgiving week and Black Friday, a tough comp for the Q1 2024 club channel.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Not explicitly framed as easy comps; management noted Q1 is seasonally slower in the club channel.
  - **Timing Shifts:** Management said PepsiCo inventory days-on-hand had been optimized — Celsius now ships product in roughly seven days versus a prior ~14 days — affecting the timing of revenue recognition; and that Q4 2023 inventory might have been drawn down further if not for innovation pipe-fill, with clear optimization occurring across Q1 2024.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** PepsiCo constitutes ~62% of Q1 2024 North America revenue; spring resets were ~one-third complete at quarter-end and expected fully done by end of June, with the full effect in scanner data from July. Celsius increased its sales and key-accounts team ~85% so far in 2024 and expects 3x as many sales staff by year-end versus the prior year. The Publix transition from the HPC aisle to the energy set illustrates structural placement gains. Celsius placed almost 3,000 branded coolers in Q1.
  - **Top Priority Follow-Up Questions:** See STEP 4 follow-up question block below.

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** No FX impact quantified. Management said per-volume pricing was generally maintained flat across the portfolio; reduced pricing in scanner data reflects Celsius Essentials promotions and an increased mix of variety packs (lower per-ounce cost) rather than a list-price change.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** Celsius Essentials (16-oz) began national distribution in January and reached 54.5% ACV by mid-April (a 5.5-point share increase since February); new 12-oz flavors (Galaxy Vibe, Blue Razz Lemonade, Sparkling Raspberry Peach, Astro Vibe) and on-the-go powder innovations expanded the portfolio. The increased variety-pack mix structurally lowers per-ounce pricing.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** Management noted faster shipment cycles (seven days versus a prior ~14) "will impact some of the revenue recognition standards" going forward — a structural change in how revenue phases relative to depletions.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Management flagged rising fuel costs and a year-over-year increase in aluminum prices as margin watch items; the CFO said Celsius has "locked in a lot of our aluminum pricing" (typically locked around Q4 each year), so near-term aluminum exposure is limited. Q1 is seasonally slower in the club channel.

#### Top Priority Follow-Up Questions

**Topic: PepsiCo Inventory Optimization Floor**

**Q (Good):** "You said total days-on-hand at PepsiCo were consistent from March into April — at what days-on-hand level does the optimization stop, and how many dollars of further destocking would that imply if it continues?"

- **Rationale:** Michael Lavery asked "how low could it go" on destocking; management said it cannot control it and declined to define a floor — the figure is essential for modeling the Q2–Q3 shipment-to-scan gap.
- **Question Type:** Reverse Chronology + Bracketing
- **Question Topic(s):** PepsiCo inventory, days-on-hand, revenue recognition
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 1
- **Question Importance Rank:** 1

**Topic: Shipment-to-Scan Gap Quantification**

**Q (Good):** "Of the gap between Q1 scanner-data growth and the 37% reported revenue growth, how many dollars are attributable to the absence of Q1 2023's ~$25 million restocking versus current-quarter PepsiCo optimization?"

- **Rationale:** Mark Astrachan flagged a visible under-shipment versus end demand; management answered qualitatively and the CFO cited ~$25 million of Q1 2023 buildup but did not bridge the full gap.
- **Question Type:** Component Breakdown + Baseline Establishment
- **Question Topic(s):** Revenue bridge, channel inventory, comparability
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 1
- **Question Importance Rank:** 2

**Topic: PepsiCo Incentive Program Margin Cost**

**Q (Good):** "The new PepsiCo incentive program ramps over the first six-to-eight months and 'cruises' in the back half — how many basis points of gross margin does the program cost at its full run-rate?"

- **Rationale:** The CFO said the program has "obviously a cost" and ramps into the back half but did not quantify the basis-point margin impact, which is needed to model H2 margin.
- **Question Type:** Presumptive Question with Component Breakdown
- **Question Topic(s):** PepsiCo incentive program, gross margin, distribution economics
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 1
- **Question Importance Rank:** 3

**Topic: Gross Margin Trajectory Into H2**

**Q (Good):** "Q1 gross margin was 51.2% — given rising fuel, the incentive program cost, and 100-days-of-summer promotions, what specific gross margin do you expect in Q2 and Q3?"

- **Rationale:** Management stuck to a "high 40s" framework while printing 51.2% and would not "lock in into the 50s," leaving the near-term quarterly margin path undefined.
- **Question Type:** Time-Bounding + Precision Technique
- **Question Topic(s):** Gross margin, freight, promotional calendar
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 1
- **Question Importance Rank:** 4

**Topic: Shelf-Reset Share Benefit Sizing**

**Q (Good):** "You expect the best shelf-space gains in company history with full scanner-data effect from July — by how many points do you expect those resets to lift MULOC share by year-end?"

- **Rationale:** Peter Grom asked directly whether management could put track growth or share performance from resets into context; management described the gains qualitatively as "the best in company history" without a quantified share lift.
- **Question Type:** Reverse Chronology + Precision Technique
- **Question Topic(s):** Shelf resets, market share, breadth of distribution
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 2
- **Question Importance Rank:** 5
