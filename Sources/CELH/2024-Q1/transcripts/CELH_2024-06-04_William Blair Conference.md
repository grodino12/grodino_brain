## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** Celsius Chief of Staff Toby David delivered a slide-driven presentation positioning Celsius as the disruptor of the Red Bull / Monster energy-drink duopoly — the number-three US energy brand and the first in a decade to exceed 10% share. The core narrative emphasized the company's 20-year evolution (from a "negative calorie" beverage near insolvency in 2011 to a fitness-rooted, science-backed "live fit" brand), the structural tailwind of retailers shifting shelf space to energy at the expense of CSDs, water, dairy, and coffee, and Celsius's unique incrementality — 41% of its growth is new-to-category consumers and 79% of consumption is new-to-category, so its retail dollar "travels further." Positive drivers cited included $1.3 billion in FY2023 sales, 48% gross margin, Celsius driving 47% of category growth, household penetration up to 26.8% (grown 11.5% in the last year), eight points of share gain in two years, and a strong runway proof set (number one on Amazon at ~20+ share, 20 share in Florida, 15+ share in 12 states, mid-20s in South Florida) against a ~12 brand share today. The acknowledged nuance/headwind was the Pepsi inventory "noise" — David clarified the pullback was in days-on-hand, not total cases, attributing it to Pepsi getting more efficient in its second full year, and conceded it "creates noise for us as a publicly traded company." The strategic outlook was confident and disciplined: 2024 framed as the biggest growth year ever (98% ACV, so the focus is breadth/depth within the store), a methodical five-year international plan (Canada at 5.5% share, UK/Ireland launched, Australia/France/New Zealand in Q4 2024), and a clear long-term ambition to close the ~$3 billion gap to Monster's US business. Overall tone was emphatically bullish, brand-led, and growth-focused.

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - **Shelf space and reset progress (Q1):** The William Blair analyst asked for an update on shelf-space gains and how far along the reset process is.
  - **Olympics / event marketing leverage (Q2):** An audience questioner asked about leveraging the Olympics and major events; management said timing (Q4 launch) limits Olympics use but the F1/Ferrari deal travels well.
  - **Production capacity and supply chain (Q3):** A questioner probed Celsius's ability to scale production; management pointed to its co-packer-only model and Chief Supply Chain Officer Paul Storey.
  - **Pepsi inventory dynamics (Q4):** A questioner asked about the Pepsi inventory pullback; management clarified it was days-on-hand, not cases, and reflected efficiency rather than weak demand.

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - **Refusal to quantify shelf-space gains:** When asked directly about reset progress, David said Celsius "haven't really publicly stated where we think we're going to land" and "haven't really publicly disclosed," deferring to end-of-month Circana SKU-per-location data — leaving the magnitude of space gains unquantified (Q1).
  - **Pepsi inventory "noise":** David proactively addressed the Pepsi inventory question as a source of investor concern, framing the days-on-hand reduction as benign distributor efficiency while conceding it "creates noise" — an implicit acknowledgment that reported revenue and underlying demand had diverged (Q4).
  - **Reset maturation timing:** David repeatedly cautioned that resets need time to "marinate" — consumers "don't just show up because they heard there were resets done" — tempering near-term expectations even amid a bullish space-gain narrative (Q1).

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **End-of-month Circana SKU data (June 2024):** David said "by the end of the month you'll have a pretty good idea of where we ended up" on SKUs per location — a near-term data point quantifying the reset gains.
  - **Remaining resets at Circle K and Kroger divisions:** David flagged Circle K and some Kroger divisions still had resets to complete; their completion and subsequent maturation is a velocity catalyst.
  - **Q4 2024 international launches (Australia, France, New Zealand):** Building on Canada (5.5% share) and UK/Ireland, these launches are the next test of the methodical international playbook.
  - **F1 / Ferrari partnership activation:** A multi-year global deal positioned as a brand-building catalyst across Europe, APAC, and the US.
  - **Sugar-free category inflection:** David said full-sugar drinks are still the majority of the category but "that's getting ready to flip for the first time" — a structural tailwind for Celsius, which "owns sugar free."

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** Not explicitly framed as tough comps in this presentation. Management cited triple-digit compound annual sales growth 2020–2023 as the high base the business is building from.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Not explicitly framed.
  - **Timing Shifts:** David addressed the Pepsi inventory pullback as a timing/measurement matter: "the inventory pullback wasn't in terms of total cases. It's on days on hand of inventory because our sales continue to grow." He attributed it to Pepsi getting "more efficient" in its second full year and trying to determine the appropriate inventory level — a distortion to reported revenue versus consumer demand rather than a demand change.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** Celsius is at 98% ACV, so growth now depends on breadth/depth within the store rather than store count. David cited the Maverik example — three facings in a top-shelf "three-door set" in December, expanding to "about a shelf and a half" in 2024 (in the underdeveloped Pacific Northwest drill-deep market). Resets remained incomplete at Circle K and some Kroger divisions. Retailers continue shifting shelf space to energy from CSDs, water, dairy, and coffee, with Celsius the biggest beneficiary. The drill-deep market count expanded from 6 (2022) to 18 (2022 expansion) to 22 (2023) to 31 today.
  - **Distribution History:** The 2020 shift to DSD (filling 200+ distributors after Bang moved to Pepsi) and the 2022 Pepsi transition (a $500 million investment for 8.5% of Celsius) drove ACV from 65% to 95% in ~three months — structural step-changes in the distribution base.

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** No FX discussion. No pricing actions discussed in this presentation.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** Celsius has four product lines: the core fruit-forward line and the Vibe line (same formula, "more fun," slightly different demo — 24 flavors total), the newly launched Essentials 16-ounce can (launched Q4 2023 at 7-Eleven, targeting a more male/blue-collar consumer wanting more volume, ~50% of the category is 16-ounce), and On The Go powder sticks (same formula as the 12-ounce can). The bifurcation of core and Vibe lines is a "billboard effect" shelf strategy.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** No specific accounting changes discussed. David noted FY2023 financials of $1.3 billion in sales and 48% gross margin.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** No weather or macro anomalies cited. David noted energy-drink household penetration rose from 64% to 70% in one year and total beverage has been declining while energy grows — a structural category dynamic, not a one-time anomaly.
