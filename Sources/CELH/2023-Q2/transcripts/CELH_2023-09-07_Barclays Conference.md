## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** At the Barclays conference, CEO John Fieldly and EVP Toby David delivered a confident, growth-oriented narrative positioning Celsius as "Energy 2.0" — a brand riding three consumer megatrends (better-for-you, function, and fitness) that has broadened the energy drink usage occasion beyond the traditional pick-me-up to include morning coffee replacement and consumption with meals (notably lunch). The primary positive drivers cited were the PepsiCo partnership (which has "exceeded everyone's expectations" one year after the first case sold October 1, 2022), ACV expansion to 95% (up from ~65%), SKUs per store doubling from 8 to 15, a club/Costco/Amazon channel run rate of approximately $250 million, and category-defining incrementality data — 44% of consumption new to category and ~40% from user intensification, per numerator. Management identified market share momentum as a key proof point, with South Florida share rising to 23.5% (from 21.7% in April) and the brand approaching a 10 share nationally in MULOC energy. Management acknowledged few headwinds but noted operational scaling challenges from rapid growth (some SKUs growing 200-300%), shipping outside production "orbits" which raises COGS/freight, and elevated coaching needs after doubling the organization in 12 months. The strategic outlook centered on 2024 inclusion in PepsiCo's national AOP process, expanding focus markets, secondary/cooler placement initiatives, foodservice expansion (Jersey Mike's national rollout, ~2,000 locations), and methodical international expansion targeting Canada plus one more country in 2024, with a larger rollout in 2025. Management's tone was highly upbeat and emphasized disciplined, ROI-driven, profitable growth.
- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - The host probed brand positioning and differentiation versus traditional energy, and whether the brand has "broader shoulders" — including scope for a lower-caffeine version (Question 1, Question 2).
  - Consumer lifetime value and where Celsius sources its volume (new-to-category versus brand switching) was a central focus (Question 3, Question 4).
  - The host pressed on channel-level growth drivers, the durability of club/e-commerce growth alongside ACV gains, and which channels are best positioned for incremental sales (Question 5, Question 7, Question 8).
  - Supply chain capacity and inventory adequacy given rapid demand were probed (Question 6).
  - Long-term operating margin trajectory as growth normalizes, and international expansion appetite/timeline, closed out the discussion (Question 11, Question 12).
- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - The host implicitly tested the durability of growth at 95% ACV by asking "what's next?" — management's answer leaned on brand awareness expansion, secondary placements, and foodservice rather than new doors (Question 9, Question 10).
  - There was implicit skepticism about whether Amazon/online dominance could translate to brick-and-mortar; management addressed this directly by citing the South Florida share data as a brick-and-mortar proof point (Question 4).
  - The host raised potential cannibalization concern — whether new distribution gains would cannibalize club or Amazon — which management rebutted with "all tides are rising" and user intensification data (Question 8).
  - The host pressed on the long-term operating margin run rate and whether it migrates toward an incumbent peer; management said it could get "very close if not similar" but flagged a freight-classification difference and signaled a deliberate choice not to over-leverage S&M in 2024 (Question 11).
- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **2024 PepsiCo National AOP Inclusion:** For the first time, Celsius is included in PepsiCo's Annual Operating Plan process, enabling national programming, wiring, and execution across key retailers in 2024 — a structural step-up in distribution capability and a key test of accelerated share gains.
  - **2024 Shelf Resets / Revised Planograms:** Coming into reset season at a ~10 share (versus a 3.4 share last year) should raise prioritization and unlock better/secondary placements; the outcome of buyer review meetings is a near-term catalyst.
  - **International Expansion — Canada + One More Country (2024):** Management is finalizing two-country launches with Canada as priority number one, with a larger rollout in 2025; first concrete international proof points arrive in 2024.
  - **Foodservice Ramp (Jersey Mike's, ~2,000 locations):** The national Jersey Mike's rollout is positioned to "prove out our thesis" on foodservice as an incremental growth channel (foodservice is ~11% of Pepsi sales).
  - **Marketing Activation (MLS / Messi, PFL, Live Fit Tour) into 2024:** Management cited "more marketing dollars than we ever had before" and the MLS national deal (with Messi joining Inter Miami) as 2024 brand-awareness drivers.
- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** None explicitly flagged. Management's framing is that 2024 should build on 2023 momentum given AOP inclusion and expanded focus markets.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Not explicitly discussed.
  - **Timing Shifts:** Management noted that in 2023 Celsius worked with PepsiCo on a divisional level rather than nationally because it was not included in the prior AOP cycle (PepsiCo's first case sold October 1, 2022); 2024 inclusion in national programming is a structural change rather than a timing redistribution.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** ACV rose from ~65% to 95% and SKUs per store doubled from 8 to 15; management positioned 2024 growth as coming from activating existing 95%-ACV doors (secondary placements, coolers) plus foodservice and new focus markets, not new ACV gains.
- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** Management noted CSD price increases have narrowed the price gap to energy ($0.50-$0.60 between a 16oz soda and Celsius), supporting trade-up — a category dynamic rather than a Celsius pricing action.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** Foodservice (~11% of Pepsi sales) and the shift toward cold availability/impulse purchasing (versus the historically "warm," take-home sales base) are structural mix changes.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** Management explicitly flagged a peer-comparison adjustment — its primary public peer classifies outbound freight in sales expense, whereas Celsius records outbound freight in COGS; investors must adjust for this when comparing margins.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** None unusual cited; management noted the energy category is growing 10-15% with retailers adding shelf space.
