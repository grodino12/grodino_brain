## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** At the Piper Sandler Growth Frontiers Conference, CEO John Fieldly and CFO Jarrod Langhans delivered a confident growth narrative, noting accelerating top-line momentum (the host cited IRI scanner data showing the brand up 144% in the latest four weeks). Management attributed the strength to three consumer megatrends (better-for-you, function, fitness), the PepsiCo partnership approaching its one-year anniversary (which lifted ACV from ~60% to over 95%), strong unit growth (signaling new consumers entering the category), and ~44% of consumers being new to category per Mintel data. Management emphasized a key strategic point: hitting 95% ACV does not end the growth story — the runway now shifts to within-store distribution gains (Celsius averages ~14.6 SKUs per location versus ~25-30 for the top two players, who also run multiple facings), better placement ("getting out of the gutter"), and the bifurcation of the Core (refreshment-focused) and Vibe (experiential) lines into two distinct brands. Management identified few headwinds but acknowledged it is deliberately giving up some near-term margin to invest behind 100% growth, and noted Q3 would see a sequential margin pullback from elevated promotional/marketing spend. The strategic outlook centered on heading into 2024 buyer/review season at a ~10 share (versus a 3.4 share 12 months ago) which unlocks better prioritization and placements, foodservice expansion (Jersey Mike's national launch, ~2,000 locations, plus hotels, colleges where Pepsi reaches ~60% of the population), and a methodical international approach with 2024 launches treated as planning/blueprint years and a larger rollout in 2025-2026. Management's tone was highly upbeat and disciplined, repeatedly stressing US focus and profitable growth.
- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - Host Michael Lavery probed the top-line drivers behind accelerating measured-retail growth and the brand's differentiation and new-to-category dynamics (Question 1, Question 2).
  - The durability of distribution growth beyond the initial PepsiCo "lift" — within-store SKUs, facings, secondary placements, and the reset/buyer-meeting cycle — was a central focus (Question 3, Question 4).
  - Channel-level questions on Amazon share, cannibalization risk, and the market share ceiling were prominent (Question 5, Question 6).
  - Foodservice mechanics (how Celsius and PepsiCo jointly pursue accounts) and international strategy were probed (Question 7, Question 8).
  - Portfolio innovation beyond the core and the sustainable margin profile / EBITDA outlook closed the discussion (Question 9, Question 10).
- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - The host implicitly tested whether the PepsiCo lift was a one-time event ("you hit October 1, then it's all over"); management rebutted this with data showing total distribution points still rising ~4% per month since January and the within-store SKU runway (Question 3).
  - Cannibalization concern was raised — whether broader brick-and-mortar distribution would erode Amazon or club sales; management countered that "the tides are rising," all channels grew over 100% in Q2, and the channels serve different consumers (Question 5).
  - The host pressed on the market share ceiling given no brand has sustained above a 10% threshold for a decade; management used the ~19 Amazon share and South Florida (23.5%) as proof points that the ceiling is well above the current ~3-4 national share, while conceding Amazon and South Florida are imperfect proxies (Question 6).
  - On margins, the host pressed for a sustainable EBITDA range; management did not commit to a specific figure, instead pointing to Monster's historical margins as the "gold standard" aspiration while explicitly choosing to give up near-term margin to invest behind 100% growth (Question 10).
- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **2024 Buyer / Reset Season (NACS, Walmart, convenience):** Entering review meetings at a ~10 share (versus a 3.4 share last year) raises prioritization; a pre-meeting with Walmart "went extremely well," and the outcome of resets is a near-term driver of facings, placement, and velocity.
  - **Core / Vibe Line Bifurcation:** Splitting the portfolio into two distinct sub-brands allows the sales team to sell unique portfolios and pursue more shelf space (one to three shelves for each line) — a structural shelf-space catalyst.
  - **Foodservice Ramp (Jersey Mike's ~2,000 locations + hotels/colleges):** The Jersey Mike's national rollout is a "really good indicator" for fast-casual performance; Pepsi's foodservice reach (Marriott, Hyatt, Sheraton, ~60% of the college population, Lowe's) is incremental runway.
  - **International Launches (2024 blueprint, 2025-2026 scale):** 2024 is a planning/blueprint year with first launches; the larger rollout is a 2025-2026 catalyst.
  - **On-The-Go Stick / Powder Expansion:** The largely unmarketed powder business is already over $100 million and growing rapidly — a potential hydration-adjacency catalyst if marketed and expanded.
