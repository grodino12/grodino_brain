## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** Celsius reported record Q4 2022 sales of $178 million (up 71% from $104 million) and full-year 2022 revenue of $654 million (up 108% from $314 million), with FY2022 North America revenue up 126%. Management emphasized 2022 as a transformational year — Celsius was the #1 brand driver of energy-category growth (22% of the category's dollar growth, ~$474 million incremental retail sales) and the #3 US energy drink, with ACV reaching ~89.7% (from 59.6% a year ago). The dominant theme was the completed PepsiCo distribution transition; ACV jumped from the mid-60s to the high-80s, and management said it does not anticipate any further material distributor-transition charges. The principal headwinds were transition-related: $194 million of full-year distributor termination expense ($156 million in Q3, $38 million in Q4), a deliberate ~$15 million Q4 over-investment in marketing (~$48 million incremental for the full year), continued unresolved 2021 material weaknesses, an unfavorable January 2023 Flo Rida litigation verdict under appeal, and a negative effective tax rate driven by the $282.5 million non-deductible preferred-stock valuation adjustment. Gross margin improved to 44.4% in Q4 (up ~445 bps YoY), achieving management's mid-40s exit-rate goal. Management's tone was confident and forward-looking, outlining three strategic pillars — top-line growth, operational excellence, and cash generation / EBITDA leverage — and signaling early international-expansion intentions.

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - Reconciling reported North America sales growth (up 74% in Q4) against much stronger Nielsen/IRI scan data (up ~130%) — the pipe-fill, product returns and year-end destocking dynamics (Questions 1, 3).
  - The pace and runway of the ACV / distribution ramp, spring resets, and where the company ultimately lands on a fully distributed basis including non-tracked channels (Questions 2, 4, 11).
  - Sales & marketing spend run rate during the distribution boom and the right go-forward percentage of revenue (Question 5).
  - Gross margin trajectory for 2023 — whether the 44% exit rate implies further sequential improvement and the magnitude of club-channel efficiency upside (Questions 7, 9).
  - The strategic timing and rationale for the international expansion commentary (Question 6).

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - The central point of friction was reconciling the 74% reported North America growth with ~130% scan growth — Kevin Grundy and Mark Astrachan pressed for the bridge; management attributed the gap to the Q3 pipe-fill, year-end working-capital management/destocking by large customers and incomplete distributor transition, while acknowledging it lacks "full transparency on the inventory levels" of its partners (Questions 1, 3).
  - Management was repeatedly non-committal on forward metrics — Fieldly declined to provide forward ACV "guidance" and said items-per-store landing point was something the company is "not really comfortable on commenting on," deferring to April reset data (Questions 2, 9).
  - Sean McGowan pushed hard for a quantified club-channel margin upside; Langhans would only say it "could be a couple percentage points" on that piece of the business while noting club is a small part of the total (Question 9).
  - There was some imprecision around the pipe-fill math — Astrachan probed why the $15-20M Q3 pull-forward wouldn't simply add to a higher Q4 run rate; management confirmed adding it back to Q4 approximates a normalized run rate but cited year-end destocking offsets (Question 3).

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **Spring 2023 retailer resets / April scan data:** Management repeatedly cited spring resets and April data as the key read on additional ACV gains, items-per-store and where the company finishes 2023.
  - **Aldi national rollout (starting Q1 2023):** A new national retailer rollout adds incremental distribution.
  - **BJ's national rollout (Q1 2023):** Full national club expansion at BJ's, plus second SKUs at Costco and Sam's Club.
  - **Club channel inline-packing transition (through 2023):** Moving club packing inline (vs. secondary repacking facilities) is a margin lever, potentially worth a couple percentage points on that business.
  - **OTS / Pepsi "medals" independent program (~150,000 doors) and foodservice:** Large non-tracked distribution opportunity in independents, colleges/universities, healthcare.
  - **International expansion initiatives:** Management said it expects to announce additional international expansion in additional countries in the future.

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** Q3 2022 North America revenue was elevated by the ~$15-20 million one-time Pepsi pipe-fill, creating a difficult comparison for Q3 2023.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** FY2022 stock option expense was $21 million, down $16 million from the prior year (driven by the revaluation of certain 2021-modified share-based awards), aiding G&A comparisons. The huge FY2022 transition-expense load ($194 million) makes 2023 GAAP operating-expense comparisons favorable.
  - **Timing Shifts:** Management reiterated the ~$15-20 million Q3 pipe-fill; year-end working-capital management/destocking by large customers pulled Q4 inventories down. Adding the pipe-fill back to Q4 approximates the normalized run rate.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** ACV rose to ~89.7% (from 59.6%); convenience ACV up ~96% to 89% (from 45.3%); US store count exceeded 210,000 (up ~20% sequentially). Club channel sales were $138 million for 2022 (up 247% from $40 million). Aldi and BJ's national rollouts and ongoing capture of vacated Bang shelf space in spring resets affect comparability.

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** Pricing benefits contributed to the Q4 and FY2022 gross margin improvement; pricing taken was "fully in play in Q4" and will benefit 2023. No new 2023 price increases had been announced. International revenue was helped by new innovation; Europe faced supply-chain challenges on protein-bar raw materials.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** A second SKU (Vibe multipack) launched at Costco in Q4; a second club-pack SKU launched at Sam's Club; pack size is being upsized from 15-pack to 18-pack. The Func Food (FAST) brand name was impaired (the prior Q3 call also cited a $2.4 million FAST brand impairment). Erythritol-containing products are a very minor part of the portfolio (Langhans estimated under ~1% of sales).
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** $194 million FY2022 distributor termination expense (within S&M, funded by Pepsi); the initial Pepsi tranche left ~$35 million of restricted cash due back to Pepsi plus ~$4 million accrued payments to former distributors. The $282.5 million non-deductible Series A preferred-stock valuation adjustment (amortized as contra revenue over 20 years) drove a deferred tax liability and a distorted effective tax rate. FY2022 sales & marketing expense was $353 million (up 372%); a ~$15 million Q4 intentional marketing over-investment versus budget. Material weaknesses from 2021 were not fully cleared.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Management noted Q4/Q1 are more consistent with each other seasonally (smaller than Q2/Q3). Amazon experienced warehouse-movement-related inventory shifts around the holiday season. Recent news on erythritol (potential stroke/heart-attack association) was raised; management said it is "looking into it."

- **Top Priority Follow-Up Questions:**

#### Topic: Underlying Normalized North America Growth

**Q (Good):** "Adjusting your reported 74% 2022Q4 North America growth for the Q3 pipe-fill add-back and year-end customer destocking, what specific clean underlying sell-in growth rate do you view as the true normalized run rate exiting 2022?"

- **Rationale:** Management said adding back the pipe-fill "gets you to kind of a normalized run rate" but never stated the resulting clean growth figure, leaving the bridge incomplete.
- **Question Type:** Assumptive Question with Baseline Establishment
- **Question Topic(s):** Revenue growth, pipe-fill, normalized run rate
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 1
- **Question Importance Rank:** 1

#### Topic: 2023 Gross Margin Trajectory

**Q (Good):** "Beyond reaffirming a mid-40s gross margin for 2023, what specific basis-point upside do you see from the club-channel inline-packing transition once all club volume is consolidated into the same co-packers?"

- **Rationale:** Langhans guided to a flat mid-40s for 2023 and would only quantify club inline-packing upside as "a couple percentage points" on a small piece of the business — the consolidated impact is unquantified.
- **Question Type:** Component Breakdown with Precision Technique
- **Question Topic(s):** Gross margin, club channel, inline packing
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 1
- **Question Importance Rank:** 2

#### Topic: ACV Ceiling and Timing

**Q (Good):** "From the ~89% ACV at year-end, where do you expect ACV to be exiting 2023 once the spring resets and April data are in, and what drives the remaining gap to the high-90s ACV of the two largest energy players?"

- **Rationale:** Management declined forward ACV guidance and deferred to April data; the ACV ceiling and timing are the key distribution-runway unknowns.
- **Question Type:** Reverse Chronology with Baseline Establishment
- **Question Topic(s):** ACV, distribution runway, spring resets
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 2
- **Question Importance Rank:** 3

#### Topic: Sales & Marketing Run Rate

**Q (Good):** "Recognizing the Q4 marketing over-investment was intentional, at what specific percentage of revenue do you expect sales and marketing to settle for the full year 2023 as ACV gains mature?"

- **Rationale:** Management pointed to a ~22% historical run rate for Q1 2023 but did not commit to a full-year 2023 figure given continued investment ahead of distribution gains.
- **Question Type:** Presumptive Question with Time-Bounding
- **Question Topic(s):** Sales & marketing, operating leverage, 2023 outlook
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 3
- **Question Importance Rank:** 4

## STEP 7: KPI Drivers & Qualitative Commentary

#### KPI Drivers & Qualitative Commentary

##### PepsiCo Distribution Transition Completion

- **Associated KPIs:** ACV, FY Distributor Termination Expense, North America Revenue
- **Commentary:**
  - "For the quarter we successfully integrated into the Pepsi distribution system, going from an ACV in the mid 60's to the high 80s very quickly." — Jarrod Langhans, CFO
  - "As of the first quarter of 2023, we have effectively transferred all activities that we had set out to transfer to Pepsi, plus a few additional areas." — Jarrod Langhans, CFO

##### Gross Margin Improvement (Cans, Freight, Pricing)

- **Associated KPIs:** Q4 Gross Margin, FY Gross Profit
- **Commentary:**
  - "The improvements in gross profit margins were due to lower average can prices, improvements in freight lanes from our orbit model and transition into Pepsi as well as pricing benefits." — Jarrod Langhans, CFO
  - "We saw gradual improvement in our gross profit margins across the back half of 2022. As we saw improvements in our average can cost with less higher price international cans, more efficient freight lanes with our orbit model, and also some pricing benefits." — Jarrod Langhans, CFO

##### Intentional Marketing Over-Investment

- **Associated KPIs:** Q4 Sales & Marketing Expenses, ACV
- **Commentary:**
  - "With the huge gains that were made in ACV we felt that it was appropriate to over invest in marketing in support of this expansion. As a percentage of sales, sales and marketing would have been inline with historical rates, had we not incurred the termination expenses and put forth the additional marketing investment." — Jarrod Langhans, CFO
  - "We made a strategic initiative to really continue to invest ahead of those ACV gains to make sure consumers know where we are, where they can purchase Celsius, and continue to drive velocity." — John Fieldly, CEO

##### Club Channel Growth & Margin Mix

- **Associated KPIs:** Club Channel Sales, Q4 Gross Margin
- **Commentary:**
  - "The improvement was made despite the significant growth we saw in the club channel. As this channel has story - had lower margins due to secondary repacking facilities which is required for the pack size." — John Fieldly, CEO
  - "We have found a number of co-packers that can pack that inline for us. So that is definitely an efficiency and will help drive margin as we look out over 2023." — Jarrod Langhans, CFO

##### Reported-vs-Scan Data Reconciliation

- **Associated KPIs:** Q4 North America Revenue
- **Commentary:**
