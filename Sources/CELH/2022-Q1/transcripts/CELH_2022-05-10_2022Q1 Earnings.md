## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** Celsius delivered a record first quarter, reporting total revenue of $133.4 million, up 167% year-over-year, marking its 14th consecutive quarter of sequential growth and a 28% increase over the prior (fourth) quarter. Management credited the outperformance to broad-based, accelerating demand: domestic revenue grew 214% (to $123.5 million) on triple-digit growth across traditional channels, world-class retailer expansion (Sam's Club nationwide rollout, Walmart and Target expansions, a 6,000-location Circle K convenience launch), and rapid scaling of newer club and vending channels, alongside a 395% surge in DSD distributor revenues as the national distribution network reached ~99% of the U.S. population. The principal headwind acknowledged was gross margin compression — gross margin of 40.4% fell from 41.1% a year earlier — driven by higher-cost imported international cans, raw material inflation, and dilutive club-channel mix requiring secondary repack facilities. Management also disclosed an ongoing (previously announced) SEC inquiry with no material updates, and a new CFO transition with Jarrod Langhans replacing the retiring Edwin Negron. The strategic outlook was confident and growth-oriented: management implemented a frontline price increase effective April 1, expects to cycle through high-cost international cans by the end of Q3, and anticipates gross margins recovering toward the mid-40s by Q4 and into 2023. The overall tone was highly optimistic, emphasizing a long runway in convenience (where 70% of energy drinks are sold) and Celsius's incrementality to the category.
- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - Analysts repeatedly probed the **state of the consumer** and recession/inflation risk, and how Celsius's premium positioning and pricing power hold up against a potentially fragile consumer (Question 1).
  - The **April 1 frontline price increase** drew significant attention — its size, portfolio scope, timing of scanner-data visibility, and risk of competitive channel loading following Monster's price action (Questions 1, 2, 5, 8).
  - **Club channel economics and durability** were a recurring theme — DSD-to-club mechanics, velocity vs. other channels, Sam's Club fill-in vs. run-rate, BJ's opportunity, and whether timing distortions would create future comp risk (Questions 2, 3, 4).
  - **Gross margin trajectory** — the cadence of international can cycle-through, freight savings from the orbit model, and whether margin-improvement initiatives still had room to run (Questions 3, 7).
  - **International performance and outlook**, given the ~18% Nordics decline, plus inventory levels and the convenience-channel growth path (Questions 4, 6, 7, 8).
- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - Management **declined to disclose the magnitude of the April 1 price increase** despite direct questioning from Kevin Grundy, stating only that it was a "sufficient amount" (Question 1) — a notable withholding of detail.
  - There was implicit tension between strong reported results and the **margin pressure** management acknowledged would worsen in Q2 before improving — analysts pressed on whether margin-improvement levers were already exhausted (Question 7).
  - Mark Astrachan challenged the **disconnect between the stated April 1 price increase and scanner data showing no price movement**, forcing management to clarify a lag effect that pushes visible pricing into Q3 (Question 5).
  - The **club channel's lower margins and possible timing/inventory-fill distortions** created uncertainty about a clean run-rate, with management conceding it is "a little bit premature" and would have a better read after Q2 (Questions 3, 4).
- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **Sam's Club run-rate clarity after Q2:** Management explicitly said it would have a "better look at the end of the second quarter" on Sam's Club performance after the Q1 fill-in and reorder — a key test of whether the club expansion sustains.
  - **Q3 international can cycle-through / margin recovery:** Management expects to exhaust high-cost international cans by end of Q3, with margins moving toward the mid-40s by Q4 — a meaningful margin inflection point.
  - **Frontline pricing visibility in Q3:** The April 1 price increase is expected to appear in scanner data starting in Q3 and continuing into Q4, providing the first measurable read on pricing power and category pass-through.
  - **BJ's club rollout in 2022:** Management expects a potential larger BJ's reset within the year, extending the club channel growth story.
  - **Summer flavor launches (Strawberry Lemonade, Arctic Vibe):** New flavors arriving at retail over the coming months ahead of the summer beverage selling season, a test of the innovation pipeline.
- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** None explicitly flagged as a non-recurring prior-year benefit creating a difficult future comp; management characterized growth as broad and durable.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Management implied newer-retailer channels (club, vending) are lapping early-stage or pre-launch periods — e.g., Costco Q1 revenue "growing over 1,100% for Q1 of 2021" and vending up ~296%, reflecting comparisons against minimal prior-year bases.
  - **Timing Shifts:** Management noted the Sam's Club nationwide rollout in the quarter "was a fill-in" plus a reorder, flagging that a clean run-rate is not yet established. Kaumil Gajrawala explicitly asked about club inventory-fill timing to avoid a "future comp" distortion; management said Costco revenue is now "standard recurring revenue" but Sam's Club timing remains to be understood after Q2.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** Sam's Club nationwide rollout added 589 locations (more than doubling club store count); national Circle K launch adds over 6,000 locations (completed by Q2); convenience locations up 88% YoY to just under 64,000; total U.S. store count up over 49,000 doors / 53% YoY to over 140,000; Costco's 561 locations (rolled out Q2 2021) drove over 1,100% Costco Q1 growth. Club channel contributed over $26 million of revenue in Q1.
- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** Europe/Nordics sales fell ~18% to $8.5 million, partly attributed to foreign-exchange translation. The April 1 frontline price increase will not be visible in scanner data until ~Q3; total revenue increase was "largely attributable to increases in sales volume as opposed to increases in product pricing." Average price increased 17.4 (units unspecified, per 52-week Nielsen data).
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** Club channel's lower margin profile (secondary repack facilities required) is a structural mix headwind to gross margin; management initiated co-packer reworks to drive cost out. Higher-cost imported international cans are flowing through COGS, expected to peak in Q2 before cycling out by end of Q3.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** New CFO Jarrod Langhans replaced retiring Edwin Negron (announced April 18). Management referenced internal control weaknesses noted in the FY2021 10-K, with remediation expected by year-end. A previously disclosed SEC inquiry remains ongoing with no material update.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Management cited industry-wide beverage margin pressure, raw material inflation, aluminum alloy price volatility, supply chain delays/shortages, labor shortages delaying retailer spring resets, and logistical disruption in Europe (railway shutdowns tied to regional activity).

**Q (Good):** "Of the frontline price increase implemented April 1, what percentage of the total portfolio's revenue base is now carrying the higher list price as of this call?"

**Q (Good):** "Of the gross margin recovery toward the mid-40s expected by Q4, how much comes from cycling out international cans versus channel mix versus the April pricing?"

- **Question Topic(s):** Gross margin, cost structure, pricing

**Topic: International Outlook**
