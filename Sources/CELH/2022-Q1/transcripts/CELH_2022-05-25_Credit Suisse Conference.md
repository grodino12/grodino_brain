## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** In a wide-ranging fireside chat at the Credit Suisse HALO Investment Summit, CEO John Fieldly framed Celsius as a turnaround story now positioned as a category leader — a brand "born in fitness, health and wellness," created by dietary-supplement formulators, backed by six clinical studies, and increasingly viewed as a top energy and broader-beverage player. He emphasized Celsius is the #2 energy drink on Amazon (behind Monster, ~6 share points ahead of Red Bull) and holds a 4+ share in the total energy category per Nielsen, having recently surpassed Rockstar. Management highlighted the ongoing DSD distribution transition — roughly 70–75% converted across 288 independent distributors — as a structural velocity driver, alongside expansion into convenience (where 70% of energy is sold) and Costco (now over 20% of company sales). The principal headwind was margin compression from strategically imported higher-cost international cans, which management expects to cycle through by end of Q2, recovering gross margin toward the mid-40s thereafter. Fieldly described a deliberate three-phase frontline price increase that began April 1, with the full effect not realized until Q4. The tone was confident and growth-oriented, repeatedly stressing a 12-to-24-month "window of opportunity" to grab share while continuing to over-invest in marketing and infrastructure.
- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - Gajrawala probed **top-line sustainability** — whether Celsius's 200%+ scan growth can persist after major one-time channel rollouts (Costco, ABI distribution expansion) (Question 9).
  - **Profitability and the path to Monster-like margins** — how Celsius balances share-gain investment against margin expansion, and whether structural factors prevent it from reaching peer profitability (Questions 10, 11).
  - The **DSD transition** — how far along the switchover is, what the optimal conversion rate is, and how velocity behaves during distribution expansion (Questions 6, 8, 16).
  - **Pricing strategy** — the timing and phasing of the April 1 frontline price increase, and whether competitor actions (Monster, Red Bull) changed Celsius's approach (Questions 12, 13).
  - The **consumer environment** — recession risk, the Walmart/Target profit warnings, and whether a weaker consumer makes new-customer acquisition harder (Questions 14, 15, 16).
- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - Gajrawala pressed on **revenue sustainability** given the "theory of numbers" working against 200%+ growth rates; Fieldly conceded the math is challenging but pointed to a multi-year share-gain window (Question 9).
  - On **margins**, Gajrawala questioned whether anything structural prevents Celsius from reaching Monster-like profitability; Fieldly defended the asset-light orbit model and framed current suppression as temporary (international cans) (Questions 10–11).
  - Gajrawala flagged the **Walmart/Target profit warnings** as a "septic shock through all of CPG" requiring a pricing rethink; Fieldly stood firm that Celsius's price increase is cost-driven and unchanged by peer or retailer dynamics (Question 14).
  - A recurring undercurrent was **disclosure restraint** — management declined to give the price-increase magnitude (only the timing) and noted Celsius does not provide forward-looking information.
- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **International can cycle-through by end of Q2:** Management expects margins to recover toward the mid-40s once the strategically imported high-cost cans are exhausted — a near-term margin inflection.
  - **Three-phase price increase fully realized in Q4:** The April 1 frontline price increase rolls out in phases, with full benefit not visible until Q4 — a measurable pricing-power test.
  - **Orbit model expansion (6 to 13 orbits):** Management cited eventual expansion to 13 orbits as the point where real distribution and supply-chain efficiencies are captured.
  - **New flavor pipeline (Strawberry Lemonade, Arctic Vibe):** Three new flavors in the pipeline targeting the traditional energy consumer, with the marketing team's plans for the back half of 2022 and into 2023.
  - **12-to-24-month share-grab window:** Fieldly explicitly framed the next 12–24 months as a "massive opportunity" window to capture share.
- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** Management acknowledged the "theory of numbers" works against sustaining 200%+ Nielsen scan growth as the base scales; no specific non-recurring prior-year benefit was quantified.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Costco scaled from a Q2-2021 test to over 20% of company sales by Q1 2022, lapping a near-zero prior-year base; ABI distribution expansion similarly lapped low prior-year coverage.
  - **Timing Shifts:** The three-phase price increase deliberately defers new-account and distributor pricing into Q4, so realized pricing benefit is back-half weighted.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** DSD conversion at ~70–75% across 288 independent distributors; 140 retailers nationally; new doors skew toward higher-velocity convenience; the company doubled distribution over the trailing 12 months.
- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** Trailing 52-week price up ~18% per scan data; a three-phase frontline (list) price increase began April 1, full effect in Q4. Raw material costs cited as up over 100% on some inputs.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** Costco grew to over 20% of company sales; channel mix (club lower-margin) influences the mid-40s gross-margin target.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** Q1 2022 EBITDA margin cited at ~11–13%; ~$10 million of operating cash flow added to the balance sheet in Q1; ~$80 million of capital on the balance sheet in October 2021 funded the strategic spot-rate can purchases.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Significant supply chain risk cited — container shortages, Asian port gridlock, and a potential California port labor contract renegotiation (June 6/9) that could cause shutdowns. Walmart and Target profit warnings cited as a CPG-wide shock. Recession risk and inflation pressure broadly acknowledged.

- **A: John Fieldly (CEO)**: Fieldly framed Celsius as a health-and-wellness brand — "essential energy for life" — aligned with a post-pandemic mega-trend in which consumers want their food and beverages to do more than hydrate.
  - "I mean Celsius... it's all about living in healthy... It's a mega trend... We need to live healthier lives better, live longer... and with Celsius and our DNA, we are born in fitness, health and wellness."

#### Question 2 - Brand History & Trajectory Inflection

- **Q: Kaumil Gajrawala (Credit Suisse)**: Asked what changed the trajectory of the product, given the proposition had been similar for a long time.
  - "...What was the moment with that or what happened that really changed the trajectory of the product?"

- **A: John Fieldly (CEO)**: Fieldly recounted Celsius's origins — created by dietary-supplement formulators (not beverage-industry insiders), backed by six clinical studies, originally positioned against the diet category. He described the company as a turnaround story (he joined in 2012), once delisted from NASDAQ and most retailers, and noted the pivotal 2015 transition to position against the larger energy-category TAM, with 7-Eleven as an early believer.
  - "...the product was born from really dietary supplement formulators that created a beverage... It's backed by 6 clinical studies... it is a turnaround story... right around 2015, we've really started to position the transition of the company to go after the energy category, which is a much larger TAM..."

- **Q: Kaumil Gajrawala (Credit Suisse)**: Asked how Celsius thinks about TAM and category size, given the energy category is blurring into sports drinks and hydration.

- **A: John Fieldly (CEO)**: Fieldly argued brands create subcategories to protect shelf space, and noted that two of the top five beverages in the total beverage category are energy drinks — suggesting Celsius is competing for the broader beverage category, not just the energy category as reported.
