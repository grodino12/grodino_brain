## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** CEO John Fieldly and EVP Toby David delivered a celebratory update at the Goldman Sachs Global Staples Forum, framing the prior 12 months as transformational. The headline message: Celsius reached "$1 billion brand status at retail" on a trailing-52-week basis as of April 26, 2023, while category share roughly doubled — from 3.7% twelve months ago to a 7.5% share at Q1 2023 (March 26) and an 8% share by April 26. Management credited the October 2022 transition of US distribution from independent Anheuser-Busch distributors to PepsiCo, which drove ACV to 95% (94-95%) far faster than the 12-18 months internally expected, and emphasized strong incrementality (about 68% of Celsius sales are incremental to the category — 24% new-to-category consumers plus 44% user intensification). On headwinds, management acknowledged fierce and intensifying competition (Celsius is the uncomfortable #3 brand), near-term gross margin pressure in 1H 2023 from destabilizing its low-mileage "orbit" shipping model during the Pepsi integration, the higher-cost club channel requiring repacking, and macro-driven uncertainty around pricing and stepped-up summer promotions. The strategic outlook is confident and ambitious: management reiterated a mid-40s% gross margin for 2023 (with upper-mid-40s possible in 2H and over the next few years via vertical integration), pointed to 2024 as a superior year once fully integrated into Pepsi's annual operating plan, and outlined methodical international expansion (UK, Canada, Germany, Japan, Australia) potentially beginning late Q4 2023 into Q1 2024. The tone throughout was upbeat, energetic and "hyper paranoid" about competition.

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - **Share gains and source of share** — Herzog probed the key drivers behind the doubling of share, the role of the Pepsi relationship, where share was being sourced from, and whether a 10% share is within reach (Questions 1, 2, 3).
  - **Competitive defense** — given intensifying competition entering the better-for-you energy space, how worried is management and how does Celsius defend its share (Question 4).
  - **Innovation pipeline and shelf resets** — the comparison of the 2024 pipeline to last year, and how much incremental shelf/cooler space was won in the spring resets (Questions 5, 6, 7).
  - **Pricing, promotions and gross margin** — the magnitude and timing of past price increases, plans for incremental pricing and summer promotions, and quantification of 1H margin pressure from the Pepsi integration (Questions 9, 10, 11, 12).
  - **International expansion** — color on the size and timing of the international investment and Pepsi's role as preferred international distributor (Question 16).

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - **Magnitude of 1H 2023 gross margin pressure** — Herzog explicitly asked management to quantify the first-half margin pressure; management declined to give a specific number, reaffirming mid-40s for 1H with potential upper-mid-40s in 2H (Question 11).
  - **Pricing posture amid macro uncertainty** — Herzog pressed on incremental pricing; Fieldly was cautious, noting "a lot of uncertainty right now with the category on how much pricing consumers can take," and that promotions would average down the realized benefit of last year's ~6% increase (Question 9).
  - **Promotion intensity and traffic softening** — management acknowledged retailers are requesting more promotions because they are "hearing some traffic slowing down," a softer demand signal partly at odds with the otherwise bullish narrative (Question 10).
  - **Competitive threat as #3 brand** — Herzog highlighted that Celsius achieved its gains despite "stepped up competition"; Fieldly conceded it is "a little bit of an uncomfortable position to be in number 3" with players "coming after us, below us" (Question 4).
  - **Margin comparability vs. Monster** — Fieldly flagged that Celsius includes outbound freight in COGS while Monster classifies it as a selling expense, an implicit pushback on direct margin comparisons (Question 13).

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **Late-June 2023 reset visibility:** Toby David said the full picture of how the spring resets went would be clear by late June, making it a near-term data point on incremental shelf gains.
  - **2H 2023 gross margin step-up:** Management expects upper-mid-40s gross margin in the back half as the Pepsi orbit-model integration synchronizes, a key margin inflection.
  - **2024 as first fully integrated Pepsi AOP year:** Because Celsius integrated outside Pepsi's 2023 annual operating plan, 2024 is positioned as a "superior opportunity," making the 2024 selling-season setup a catalyst.
  - **International market entry (late Q4 2023 into Q1 2024):** Toby David flagged possible entry into the UK, Canada, Germany, Japan or Australia around year-end, a new growth vector.
  - **Summer 2023 marketing programs:** Q2/Q3 programs (Oasis Vibe launch, central vibe store coast-to-coast, event activations) represent a test of brand momentum in the peak "100 days of summer."

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** Not explicitly framed, though management implied future YoY share-gain comparisons get harder given the doubling of share over the prior 12 months.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Management cited the Bang Energy disruption as a tailwind Celsius "capitalized on" with an aggressive sales organization during the prior selling season — implying some prior-period benefit from a competitor's distress.
  - **Timing Shifts:** Celsius integrated into the Pepsi system on October 1, 2022, outside Pepsi's 2023 AOP; management said everything for 2023 was "jammed into the Pepsi system outside of their normal" operating cadence, distorting 2023 versus a "cohesive" 2024.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** ACV reached 95% at quarter-end (a ~37-share-point ACV gain on recent resets in convenience); SKUs per location rose to 13.6 (MULO+C, four-week data as of March 26) from roughly eight a year prior; convenience SKU count moved from 3-4 toward double digits. Management noted 70% of energy drinks are sold in convenience and gas.

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** Celsius took an average ~6% price increase last year (2022); management said the full 6% would not flow through in 2023 because promotional deal-downs average it in.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** Club channel (Costco, Sam's, BJ's) entered ~12 months prior carries higher cost due to reworking/repacking; the move from a hand-packed 15-pack to an in-line 18-pack improved efficiency. Mix shifts can move gross margin given channel cost differences.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** Management explicitly flagged a structural comparability difference vs. Monster — Celsius includes outbound freight in cost of goods sold whereas Monster treats it as a selling expense — affecting margin-profile comparisons.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Management cited macroeconomic uncertainty constraining further pricing and noted retailers report traffic slowing, prompting more promotional requests heading into the seasonally critical summer.
