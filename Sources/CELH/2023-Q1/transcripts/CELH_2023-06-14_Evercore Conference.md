## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** At Evercore ISI's 3rd Annual Consumer and Retail Conference, CEO John Fieldly and EVP Toby David delivered a confident, story-driven update positioning Celsius as the leader of the "second wave" of energy drinks. The core message: Celsius has evolved from a niche fitness brand (historically blocked from traditional convenience) into a strong #3 energy brand with an ~8% national share, built on three fast-growing food-and-beverage megatrends — better-for-you without sacrificing flavor, more function (a thermogenic with clinical studies), and a premium fitness-lifestyle positioning. Positive drivers cited were high incrementality (68% of consumption incremental — 24% new-to-category, 44% user intensification), over-indexing with Hispanic and African-American and female consumers, a surprising ~10% food-service share of Pepsi revenue (a "huge unlock" suggesting Celsius is broader than energy), a ~19% Amazon share with no cannibalization despite distribution expansion, and ~$50 million of Q1 club sales (a ~$200 million run rate). Headwinds and tensions acknowledged were the intensifying competitive "street war" — Red Bull responding to share loss with stronger January/February promotional activity, a crowded "second wave" of competitors, and contentious retailer relationships as Amazon scaled. The strategic outlook is decidedly ambitious: management has shifted its tone from "going after energy" to a "total beverage play," sees a long runway in the core portfolio (timing, sequencing, discipline emphasized), and hinted at future adjacencies including a lower-caffeine product. The tone throughout was upbeat, candid and combative about competition.

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - **Brand differentiation** — what distinguishes Celsius from the crowded "second wave" of new-wave energy brands (Question 1).
  - **Demographics** — gender, income and ethnic mix and whether growth comes from new demographics (Questions 4, 5).
  - **Pricing and promotion / competitive response** — how to think about increased promo as Red Bull responds to share loss (Questions 7, 8).
  - **Pepsi alignment as a competitive advantage** — whether being aligned with Pepsi changes how the big players compete (Question 9).
  - **Long-term strategy, Amazon incrementality, and caffeination** — three-to-five-year strategy and adjacencies, the Amazon channel's incrementality and margin profile, and whether a lower-caffeine product is on the roadmap (Questions 11, 12, 14).

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - **Competitive "street war" and Red Bull's promotional response** — the moderator pressed on Red Bull reacting to share loss with promo; management acknowledged Red Bull's strong Q1 promotional activity (primarily grocery, also convenience) that has since normalized, framing it as a "street war" (Questions 7, 8).
  - **Whether Pepsi alignment deters competitors** — the moderator probed the "big kid in the schoolyard" dynamic; management deflected from claiming a deterrent, instead stressing Pepsi enables national execution that the prior fragmented 300-distributor network could not (Question 9).
  - **Big-player energy launches failing** — the moderator noted Coke Energy "didn't really work" and Mountain Dew Energy is "stumbling"; management reframed this positively as the category mainstreaming and pulling CSD consumers into energy (Question 3).
  - **Amazon as positive or negative with retailers** — the moderator asked whether a strong Amazon presence is a challenge; management conceded it has been "contentious" with some key accounts but worked through it (Question 12).
  - **Caffeination ceiling** — the moderator probed whether 200mg caffeine limits meal occasions; management acknowledged a lower-caffeine opportunity "down the road for sure" but declined specifics (Question 14).

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **Food service expansion:** Management called the ~10% food-service share of Pepsi revenue (after only ~3 months) a "huge unlock" — capturing basket rings via Grubhub/delivery and lunch occasions is a major growth vector.
  - **One-year Pepsi anniversary (October 2023):** Management framed the upcoming anniversary as validation of the partnership and the platform for national competitive execution.
  - **2024 flavor and pack-size innovation:** New flavors are in the 2024 pipeline; pack-size innovation and variety packs/large format are growth levers on the ~13.6-items-per-store base.
  - **Adjacent categories / Pepsi portfolio white space:** Management is refining a three-to-five-year strategy that now includes looking at white space within Pepsi's portfolio for innovative offerings.
  - **Lower-caffeine product:** Management confirmed a lower-caffeinated opportunity "down the road for sure," a potential expansion into meal and different occasions.

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** Not explicitly framed; the ~8% national share (heading toward 10%) and 140% scan growth set a high base.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Management referenced Bang Energy "banging down the door" as the prior category catalyst Celsius's second wave built on.
  - **Timing Shifts:** Not specifically quantified in this transcript (unlike the Stifel appearance the prior week, where ~$20M of Q1 order timing was cited).
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** Convenience is the "last frontier" Celsius has now entered; management is building a delta versus #4/#5 brands while closing the gap to Red Bull and Monster. Red Bull took strong January/February promotional activity that has since normalized.

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** Red Bull and Monster took pricing in fall 2022 that has largely "stuck"; the energy category runs 24-28 promotional weeks per year, and 2023 summer promo is heavier than 2022.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** Club channel (~$50M Q1, ~$200M run rate) is untracked in scan data; food service is a new channel that may shift mix. The consumer base is shifting incrementally toward males as convenience accelerates.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** Not specifically addressed in this transcript.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Management cited a post-pandemic focus on health and exercise as a structural global tailwind; the energy category is growing 10-15% with retailers allocating more shelf space.
