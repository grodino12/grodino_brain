## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** At Stifel's Cross Sector Insight Conference, CEO John Fieldly and EVP Toby David reinforced a strongly bullish narrative one year after the company's prior appearance. The headline message centered on the success of the PepsiCo distribution transition (announced about 10.5 months earlier): distribution points have roughly doubled since the October 1, 2022 switch, ACV stands at 95% (achieved 18-24 months ahead of internal plans), and the company averages about 13.6-14 SKUs per location. Management credited a smooth, "one light switch" transition (~80% of distributors moved on October 1, helped by 60-90 day termination clauses and partner buyouts), the hiring of ex-Pepsi sales reps, and a deliberate brand-education effort. Positive drivers cited were strong velocity acceleration on a two-year basis despite the distribution doubling (suggesting accelerating underlying demand), surprising early success in food service (~10% of Pepsi revenue), high incrementality (68% of consumption incremental — 24% new-to-category, 44% user intensification), an exponentially growing powder business, and a broadening consumer base spanning younger and older demographics. Headwinds and cautions were modest: management acknowledged quarter-end inventory "inconsistencies" (a Q4 2022 pullback and roughly $20 million of excess order timing in Q1 2023, which it now discloses each quarter), low single-digit household penetration, and the long, multi-year path to closing the SKU-per-location gap versus Monster/Red Bull (23-25 SKUs). The strategic outlook is confident and ambitious: management positions Celsius as a total-beverage opportunity, sees the US as the priority for capital given known ROI, and pursues a methodical, "no spray and pray" international expansion leveraging Pepsi's bottler network. The tone throughout was upbeat and energetic.

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - **Pepsi transition and what surprised management** — the moderator probed the smoothness of the transition and what could improve from here (Question 1).
  - **Channel inventory health heading into summer** — whether channel inventories are correctly positioned and whether more sell-in is needed (Question 2).
  - **Distribution point headroom** — the moderator repeatedly probed the "right" level of distribution points, benchmarking against Bang's peak and Monster (Questions 3, 4, 5).
  - **Velocity sustainability and incrementality** — how velocity keeps accelerating while distribution doubles, and how incremental Celsius is to the category (Questions 8, 9).
  - **Consumer profile, brand awareness, and international expansion** — who the core consumer is, US versus international brand awareness, the international strategy, media spend allocation, and innovation as a growth runway (Questions 11-14).

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - **Quarter-end inventory volatility** — the moderator pressed on whether channel inventories were correctly positioned; management conceded a Q4 2022 pullback and ~$20 million of excess Q1 order timing, and committed to disclosing these "inconsistencies" each quarter while steering attention to scan data (Question 2).
  - **The "right" level of distribution points** — the moderator repeatedly returned to this, noting Celsius is still ~20-30% below Bang's peak and ~50% of Monster; management would not commit to a target, deflecting toward velocity and optimization (Questions 3, 4, 5).
  - **Velocity skepticism** — the moderator noted Celsius's conference samples were free ("free cans versus purchase cans, this might be a different story if we were selling them"), an implicit gentle challenge to the demand narrative (Question 8).
  - **International ROI versus US** — Toby David repeatedly emphasized that every international dollar takes longer to return than a US dollar with known ROI, a self-imposed tension favoring US focus (Question 12).
  - **SKU-count gap versus Monster/Red Bull** — management acknowledged it will take "a couple of years" to climb to competitor SKU and facing levels and build a "billboard effect" (Question 3).

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **First summer at 95% ACV ("100 days of summer"):** Management is running a large display-activation and retail-marketing program with Pepsi frontline reps; the summer is the first peak season at full distribution and a key demand test.
  - **Convenience-channel maturation:** Celsius is "still in its infancy" in convenience (only entered 7-8 of Circle K's 13 divisions last year); deeper convenience penetration is a multi-year growth lever, especially with single-serve cold packs at the highest ring.
  - **Food service mix shift:** The surprising ~10% food-service share of Pepsi revenue could change the long-term channel mix and expand the total addressable opportunity beyond traditional energy.
  - **Powder / on-the-go sticks adjacency:** The powder business has grown exponentially at Walmart and Target; management sees on-the-go sticks as a sizable future adjacency.
  - **2024 marketing expansion and international entry:** Management is building 2024 plans to expand "drill deep" markets and pursue methodical international entry via Pepsi bottlers (Britvic/Centauri in the UK, Asahi in Australia).

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** Not explicitly framed; the doubling of distribution points and the ACV jump to 95% set a high prior-year base.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Management referenced Bang's distribution decline as a competitive backdrop Celsius capitalized on.
  - **Timing Shifts:** Management cited a Q4 2022 inventory pullback at the primary distributor's mixing centers and roughly $20 million of excess order-timing in Q1 2023; it will disclose such quarter-end "inconsistencies" each quarter, and pointed analysts to scan data over sell-in.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** Distribution points roughly doubled since the October 2022 Pepsi switch; ~80% of distributors transitioned on October 1, 2022. Convenience is the newest channel and "Sea Source"/convenience-related revenue is ~56%, up from 51-52% pre-Pepsi. Celsius expanded into 23 markets nationally over the prior ~18 months.

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** Not discussed in this transcript.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** The club channel launched concurrent with the distribution doubling and is "not tracked" in scan data — relevant because tracked velocity has accelerated even though untracked club volume could have masked it. The food-service channel is new and may structurally shift channel mix.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** Management flagged quarter-end order-timing distortions (Q4 2022 down, ~$20M up in Q1 2023) that affect quarter-to-quarter comparability of reported revenue versus sell-through.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Management cited declining tobacco and other convenience-store consumption, with retailers seeking energy's incremental dollar rings; health/wellness and fitness megatrends are structural tailwinds post-COVID.
