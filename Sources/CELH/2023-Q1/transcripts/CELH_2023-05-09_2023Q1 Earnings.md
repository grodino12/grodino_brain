## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** Celsius reported record Q1 2023 revenue of approximately $260 million, up 95% from $133 million a year ago — the first time the company exceeded $200 million in quarterly revenue and a 46% sequential increase over Q4 2022. North America revenue rose 101% to $249 million. Management emphasized that ACV reached a record 95.4% in MULOC (from 69.5% a year ago) — far faster than the 12-18 months management internally expected — with convenience ACV gaining 37.7 points to 93.4%. Celsius was the #3 US energy drink with a record 7.5% share (doubling from 3.7%) and the #2 brand on Amazon. The company achieved record non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA of $48.7 million (over 18% of sales), aided by sales/marketing timing and SG&A operating leverage; gross margin was ~44%. The principal headwinds and qualifying factors were transition-related noise: a ~$20-25 million inventory build within the Pepsi mixing centers (incremental sales), a few million dollars of inventory write-offs, slightly higher freight costs, and management's caution that gross margin would face some first-half pressure before back-half upside. Management's tone was confident and upbeat, signaling continued summer investment, a long growth runway, and an international expansion targeted for early 2024 with 2023 as the planning year.

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - The spring resets and shelf-space dynamics — how much expansion was into existing energy doors versus new doors, and items-per-store growth (Question 1).
  - How much of the Q1 reacceleration was initial channel fill / pipe-fill versus genuine reorders and sell-through, and the read-through to Q2 (Questions 2, 9).
  - The path to a 10% market share given ACV, items per store and velocity trends — and competitive launches (Monster Zero Sugar, Rainstorm, Prime) (Question 3).
  - Gross margin — the impact of inventory write-offs and the meaning of "first-half pressure" (Question 4).
  - The international expansion rationale, target markets and rollout cadence (Question 4).
  - SG&A run rate and the interplay of summer marketing step-up versus operating leverage (Question 7).

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - The recurring tension was disentangling the Pepsi mixing-center inventory build from true demand — analysts (Jeff Van Sinderen, Michael Lavery) probed whether the ~$20-25 million build was pulled-forward volume or a normalized reset; Langhans said it was "in large part" preparation for the summer selling season and activations (Questions 2, 8).
  - The scan-versus-reported gap persisted — Jonathan Keypour noted ~138% Q1 scan growth implies a ~$45 million gap to reported, similar to the ~$50 million Q4 gap; Fieldly attributed it to value-chain lumpiness through the Pepsi mixing centers and the mix of slower-growing Amazon/club businesses (Question 6).
  - Management declined to quantify several forward metrics — Fieldly repeatedly said "we don't provide any forward guidance" on velocity, and the company decided to stop disclosing the specific cooler count on a go-forward basis, citing competitive sensitivity (Questions 1, 10).
  - Kevin Grundy implicitly pressed management on whether the prior 10% share ambition is now too conservative given the pace of ACV gains; Fieldly framed 15-17 items per store by year-end as the path to ~10% share (Question 3).

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **Summer 2023 selling season (Q2/Q3) and the "100 days of summer" marketing program:** Management's biggest seasonal quarters, with planned marketing/activation step-up — a key test of velocity and demand.
  - **Items-per-store expansion to 15-17 by year-end:** Management framed this as the path to a ~10% category share from 7.5%.
  - **Back-half 2023 gross margin upside:** Management expects gross margin upside in H2 as the supply chain is fully integrated and freight lanes are optimized.
  - **International expansion launch in early 2024:** A handful of established energy markets across Europe and APAC, with 2023 as the planning year — a new growth vector.
  - **Summer flavor innovation:** New flavors (lemon lime, green apple cherry at 7-Eleven) and further launches to add SKU breadth at retailers.

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** Management implied future quarters will lap the rapid Q1 2023 ACV ramp; the ~$20-25 million Q1 mixing-center inventory build is incremental and will need to anniversary.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Q1 2023 S&M as a percentage of sales (18.3%) was well below the prior year's 23.7%, partly a timing benefit that will reverse as summer marketing ramps.
  - **Timing Shifts:** Management quantified a ~$20-25 million increase in days inventory outstanding within the Pepsi mixing centers (incremental Q1 sales), expected to be sustained through the summer; Q1 also benefited from the timing of sales and marketing activations.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** ACV reached 95.4% in MULOC (from 69.5%); convenience ACV up 37.7 points to 93.4%; club channel sales of $47 million (up 77%); items per store rose to 13.6 (recorded channel) from 8.6 a year ago; foodservice was ~10% of Pepsi revenues. Competitive launches (Monster Zero Sugar, Rainstorm, Prime) entered the category.

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** International sales grew 15% to $11.4 million. Gross margin improvement was driven by lower average can prices and orbit-model leverage, partially offset by higher freight and a few million dollars of inventory write-offs.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** The 18-pack transition is "pretty much" complete across all club channels; second variety packs (including the Vibe pack) are expanding. New flavor innovation (lemon lime, green apple cherry) launched in Q1. Amazon and club businesses grew slower than the overall scan rate, affecting the reported-vs-scan mix.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** The Q1 2023 cash balance of ~$634 million included ~$38 million primarily due back to Pepsi (excess distributor-transition funds, to be returned in Q2). Q1 operating cash flow was a use of $14 million (vs. $9 million provided in Q1 2022), driven by growth-related working capital and Pepsi-transaction timing. The can-label settlement was closed; the SEC review continues with no updates.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Q1 is a seasonally smaller quarter; production will increase significantly for the busy summer months. Aluminum cans are now sourced domestically, benefiting margins.

## STEP 10: Q&A Summary

#### Question 1 - Spring Resets, Shelf Space & Velocity

- **Q: Mark Astrachan (Stifel)**: Asked for an update on how the spring resets look from a shelf-space standpoint — how much is in existing energy doors versus new doors and overall incremental-space expectations; in follow-ups, asked whether the company still wants legacy energy doors, and how much of the surprising velocity strength is higher-velocity stores versus brand awareness.
  - "Maybe give us a bit of an update on how the spring resets look from a shelf space standpoint?... How much is coming in or going into new doors?" ... "How much of that is just higher velocity sea stores versus just overall brand awareness?"

- **A: John Fieldly, CEO**: Said the resets that began in January drove the biggest ACV gains in convenience (37.7-point increase), reaching 95.4% MULOC ACV; items per store rose to 13.6 (from 8.6). With ACV near 95%, the bigger opportunity now is breadth within retailers — better placements, cold availability and cooler placements. Legacy energy doors remain (the food channel HCC section at Publix, Kroger); the strategy there is unchanged, but new SKUs and summer flavor innovation (lemon lime, green apple cherry at 7-Eleven) add breadth. On velocity, he said it is increasing with room to go further north, supported by summer marketing programs, but declined to give forward guidance.
  - "We saw the biggest really ACV gains when you go from over a 37.7% really increase in points of distribution in convenience... we went from an average of about 13.6 items per location in a recorded channel versus the prior year was at 8.6." ... "We're seeing velocity increase, Mark... We don't provide any forward guidance, but something to look at."
