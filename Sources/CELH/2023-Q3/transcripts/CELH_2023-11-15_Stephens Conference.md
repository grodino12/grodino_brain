## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** At the Stephens Annual Investment Conference, EVP Toby David and Head of IR Cameron Donahue delivered a confident, growth-oriented update positioning Celsius as "Energy 2.0," competing directly with Red Bull and Monster. The core message: the brand capitalizes on a five-to-ten-year health-and-wellness movement, with a cleaner label (no sugar, no artificial preservatives/colors/flavors, seven essential vitamins) and fitness-rooted authenticity. Key positive drivers cited were the PepsiCo distribution partnership (ACV up from ~65% at the October 2022 transition to ~95% now), over 80% incremental consumption (44% new-to-category, ~40% user intensification), the #1 Amazon and #1 Instacart positions, and a ~24% South Florida market share (up from ~17.7% at the start of 2023) demonstrating market-level potential well above the ~10% national share. Management highlighted improving profitability — Q3 gross margin "just a tick north of 50%" (~49% normalized), Q3 EBITDA margin ~27% — and a strong balance sheet with ~$750 million of cash. Headwinds and tensions acknowledged were modest: convenience under-indexing (58% of sales versus ~70% for Monster/Red Bull), the "street war" with big players (50-100 competitor reps flooding South Florida), tracked-channel data understating true performance (~$250M of untracked Costco/Amazon run rate), and a still-low SKU/facing count (~15 per location versus ~25 for competitors). The strategic outlook is ambitious but disciplined: drill-deep market expansion (18 markets in 2022, 23 in 2023, 30 planned for 2024), food-service unlocks (Dunkin', Jersey Mike's, college/universities via Pepsi's ~60% campus coverage), a Q1 2024 Canada launch, methodical international expansion, an aspirational ~55% gross margin and 30-35% EBITDA margin, and ~20,000 branded coolers placed in 2023.

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - **Brand resonance and the Pepsi partnership** — what makes Celsius resonate with consumers and how the Pepsi partnership fuels growth (Questions 1, 2).
  - **Distribution breadth, SKUs and facings** — the opportunity to grow SKUs/facings versus Monster/Red Bull (Questions 3, 4).
  - **Drill-deep market expansion** — replicating South Florida's success across the country (Questions 6, 7, 8).
  - **Pricing, promotion and innovation** — promotional depth, price positioning versus competitors, LTOs/exclusives, and the lower-caffeine opportunity (Questions 11-13).
  - **Margins, balance sheet and international** — gross margin/EBITDA targets, ~$750M cash deployment, tracked-vs-untracked data, and international expansion including Canada (Questions 18, 19, 23, 25).

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - **Tracked-channel data understating performance** — an audience member raised the gap between scan data and reported results; David explained ~$250M run-rate of untracked Costco/Amazon sales (~$500M of retail) distorts tracked velocity/share and that the gap should narrow "a year out, two years out."
  - **Amazon data skepticism vs. brick-and-mortar** — David acknowledged "some skepticism of how that translates over to brick-and-mortar," citing the South Florida data as the brick-and-mortar proof point.
  - **Competitive "street war"** — David noted that disclosing the South Florida data "put a bull's eye" on the market, drawing 50-100 Red Bull/Monster reps to battle in the street.
  - **Red Bull pricing — real or rhetorical** — David questioned whether Red Bull is "really taking price" or "just saying they're taking price and then... deal down on their promotional spend."
  - **Cash deployment** — an audience member pressed on the ~$750M cash; David said the priority is the core brand, with M&A and vertical integration "on the table" but approached cautiously.

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **2024 shelf negotiations from a #3 position:** Management expects more SKUs and better shelf locations next year, negotiating from a ~10 share versus a ~3.5 share last year — driving trial and stickiness.
  - **Food service expansion (Dunkin', Jersey Mike's, college/universities):** Jersey Mike's (~2,000 locations) is a proof-of-concept; Pepsi caters to ~60% of the US college/university population, a major early-adopter marketing and revenue unlock.
  - **Canada launch in Q1 2024:** Celsius announced entry into Canada with Pepsi Canada, the first formal international expansion step.
  - **Drill-deep market expansion to 30 markets in 2024:** Replicating the South Florida playbook (24% share) across top energy markets is the central national-share growth lever.
  - **Lower-caffeine / new pack-size innovation:** A potential 250ml lower-caffeine offering and new core/Vibe flavors for 2024, plus the new 16-ounce Essentials line rolling nationally in January 2024.
  - **Margin expansion toward ~55% gross / 30-35% EBITDA:** Aspirational targets achievable with scale and potential vertical integration.

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** Q3 2023 gross margin (~50%+) included "some one-offs... to our benefit"; management said a ~49% normalized rate and the Q2 margin profile are better baselines for the near future.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Management noted Celsius negotiated 2024 shelf space from a ~10 share versus a ~3.5 share a year earlier, implying improved shelf positioning ahead.
  - **Timing Shifts:** Exclusive flavor launches with retailers (e.g., the 7-Eleven green apple cherry rolling nationally in January 2024, the Essentials 16oz line launched early at 7-Eleven two weeks before the conference) redistribute distribution timing.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** ACV rose from ~65% at the October 2022 Pepsi transition to ~95% now; convenience is 58% of sales (versus ~70% for Monster/Red Bull), with Circle K (most divisions entered in the prior 12 months) still ramping; SKUs per location ~15 versus ~25 for competitors.

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** Celsius took price at the beginning of 2022 (ahead of competitors); Red Bull is reportedly taking price now, with Monster's response uncertain. Celsius is priced between Red Bull and Monster (more expensive than both on a per-ounce basis).
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** The 16-ounce Essentials line is new; food service is a new channel via Pepsi; untracked Costco/Amazon (~$250M run rate, ~$500M retail) is not in scan data.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** Management flagged a ~4% gross-margin comparability gap versus Monster, which excludes outbound freight from cost of goods; branded coolers are amortized over five or seven years; a dividend is paid to Pepsi (offset by interest on ~$750M cash).
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** The energy category is highly promoted (~25 weeks/year); management did not flag specific weather or macro anomalies.
