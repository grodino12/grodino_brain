## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** Toby David used the Piper Sandler fireside chat to walk through the August 2025 expanded PepsiCo transaction (~$585 million) and frame Celsius Holdings as a newly portfolio-driven company. He broke the deal into four elements: (1A) the energy "captaincy" within PepsiCo's Energy portfolio, giving Celsius prioritization and control over how much Celsius, Alani Nu, and Rockstar shelf space and which SKUs go into PepsiCo-controlled planograms nationally; (1B) bringing Alani Nu into the PepsiCo distribution system (transition ~December 1, 2025, moving ~80% of distribution from the prior ABI network); (3) deeper PepsiCo alignment, including a second Board seat and the addition of Eric Hanson as President & COO; and (4) the Rockstar acquisition. Positive drivers cited: a strong energy category with most brands growing double digits, Alani Nu's strong convenience velocity (~65% ACV) and triple-digit growth, and the portfolio at a 20%+ market share — closer to Monster (~28% dollar share) than to the next-largest brand. The clearest acknowledged challenge was Rockstar, framed as a turnaround requiring a "conservative" "undersell and overdeliver" approach — step one is to stabilize the brand and stop share losses through SKU rationalization, with the cited ~$250 million revenue figure noted as a post-rationalization run rate. On margin, Toby David guided to a "low 50s" gross margin (~51-ish) for the back half, with Q4 potentially under more pressure from tariff-affected aluminum cans and "noise" from the Rockstar transaction; he reaffirmed an unchanged long-term aspiration of a mid-50s apples-to-apples gross margin and ~30% EBITDA margin, with no timeline. The overall tone was confident, measured, and execution-focused.

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - **The expanded PepsiCo deal (Q1, Q2):** Michael Lavery wanted the deal's portfolio impact, where management is most excited, whether C-store is the focus for distribution upside, and how each of the three brands changes from here.
  - **Assortment / SKU rationalization (Q4, Q5):** Lavery probed how much harder it has been to get the right assortment versus Red Bull/Monster's concentrated core SKUs, the improvement potential, and the timing of resets.
  - **Rockstar's role and innovation (Q6, Q7):** Lavery asked whether Rockstar could return to growth and have an innovation focus, and how innovation fits across the now three-brand portfolio.
  - **Category rebound and margins (Q8, Q9):** Lavery sought the drivers of the category rebound after the 2024 slump and a near-term and long-term margin framework, including incentive/distribution cost changes under the new PepsiCo agreement.
  - **International progress (Q10):** Lavery asked for an update on the newer international markets and the right way to think about the international build-out.

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - **Quantifying the captaincy upside:** Toby David repeatedly said the company has "not identified" how much incremental space the captaincy delivers ("We haven't identified what that is through this captaincy"), leaving the central deal benefit unquantified (Q3).
  - **Rockstar's growth potential:** When Lavery pushed on whether Rockstar could return to growth and have an innovation focus, Toby David declined to commit — "I don't want to make any promises today" — and emphasized stabilization first (Q6).
  - **Margin benchmark nuance:** Toby David pushed back on a direct Monster comparison, noting Monster's reported gross margin excludes outbound freight while Celsius's includes it — so an apples-to-apples Monster benchmark is mid-50s, not upper-50s — reframing the long-term margin target downward versus a naive read (Q9).
  - **2026 margin reticence:** Toby David explicitly declined to discuss 2026 margin ("We haven't really talked about 2026 yet. We'll see what unfolds over the next quarter or so"), leaving the post-transaction margin trajectory open (Q9).

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **Alani Nu distribution transition to PepsiCo (December 1, 2025):** ~80% of Alani Nu distribution moves into the PepsiCo system on 12/1 — a key execution test that also creates Q3/Q4 "noise."
  - **2026 planogram resets (January–April/May 2026):** Management said the bulk of distribution and assortment gains flow through with the standard reset cadence in Q1/Q2 2026 — the principal inflection for realizing captaincy upside.
  - **Celsius first LTO (Q4 2025):** Management confirmed a Celsius limited-time offering in Q4 2025 — the brand's first foray into LTOs.
  - **Rockstar SKU rationalization / stabilization:** Stopping Rockstar share losses and consolidating SKUs to top performers is the first milestone in the turnaround.
  - **Pepsi IOD/NOD large-display rollout for Alani Nu (Q1/Q2 2026):** PepsiCo's inventory-on-display capability is expected to build large Alani Nu displays in grocery and big box starting Q1/Q2 2026.

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** Not specifically flagged as a headwind. Management noted the strong 2020-to-mid-2024 "high peaks" for Celsius, implying difficult historical comparisons for the brand's standalone trajectory.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Management explicitly noted the category rebound is partly "a little bit of help from easy comps" and "some easier accounts versus last year," because 2024 was an industry-wide down year ("last year was really an anomaly").
  - **Timing Shifts:** The Alani Nu distribution transition (12/1) and planogram resets (Q1/Q2 2026) shift the realization of distribution/assortment gains; management expects "a little bit" of flow-through in December but more in Q1/Q2. Q3/Q4 2025 will carry "noise" from the Rockstar transaction.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** Major structural change — Alani Nu moves out of the fragmented ABI independent-distributor network into the PepsiCo (Tier 1) system; Rockstar joins the portfolio. Captaincy gives Celsius control of the energy lead within PepsiCo planograms, including 100,000+ independent convenience stores ("Medals Program"). Alani Nu is at ~65% ACV in convenience with strong scanner velocity, with convenience and foodservice cited as the largest white-space opportunities.
  - **Top Priority Follow-Up Questions:** See STEP 4 follow-up question block below.

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** No FX detail beyond international commentary. Management noted broad coffee pricing ($6–$7 per coffee) versus a $3 Celsius supports an energy-versus-coffee value substitution.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** Material — Rockstar (~$250 million post-rationalization revenue run rate) joins as a third brand; Alani Nu transitions distribution systems; PepsiCo gains a second Board seat. International is currently ~5% of revenue and will be a smaller share once Alani Nu (largely US) is fully consolidated.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** The PepsiCo agreement includes performance incentives that give PepsiCo "a little bit of enhanced margin." Q4 2025 gross margin faces pressure as FIFO inventory rolls through tariff-affected aluminum cans; Q3/Q4 carry transaction-related "noise."
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Management characterized the 2024 category slump as an industry-wide anomaly ("every category was down last year") now rebounding; it cited a coffee-to-energy consumer substitution, helped by coffee chains conditioning consumers toward cold beverages.
