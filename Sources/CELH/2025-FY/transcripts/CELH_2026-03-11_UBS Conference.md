## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** This was a UBS conference fireside chat held two weeks after the Q4/FY2025 earnings call, so there were no headline period results to beat or miss. Management used the forum to reinforce confidence in the three-brand "Modern Energy" portfolio (Celsius, Alani Nu, Rockstar) and address investor concerns over recent softer brand-Celsius scanner data. Positive drivers cited: energy remains the biggest growth driver in beverage (54% of LRB growth, 85% of energy growth from zero sugar); the first couple of months of 2026 were "great for both brands"; brand Celsius is outpacing the category in singles and convenience (over 20% growth in convenience most months); Alani Nu posted triple-digit growth and is described as having "outpaced our expectations"; and the PepsiCo captaincy is strengthening alignment. Headwinds and challenges acknowledged: brand Celsius scanner trends have softened in early Q1 2026, attributed to a deliberate SKU rationalization ("trimming the tail" of least-productive SKUs) and lapping four flavor launches from Q1 2025 with zero launches in Q1 2026; Essentials (the 16-ounce format) "has been a little bit of a drag"; the ~$25 million Q4 net benefit is now characterized as a pull-forward out of Q1 into Q4 (notably the Cherry Bomb LTO revenue captured in December); Rockstar is in stabilization mode; and aluminum/Midwest premium and tariff costs remain a watch item. The strategic outlook was steady and confident: management reaffirmed the gross-margin stair-step toward "low 50s" in the back half of 2026, expects "low 50s" near-term and a "handful of years" path to the mid-50s, sees brand Celsius layering on innovation in Q2–Q4, and frames 2026 as an efficiency year for coordinated multi-brand RGM. The overall tone was reassuring and measured, working to defuse the brand-Celsius slowdown narrative.
- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:** UBS analyst Peter Grom concentrated on the following themes —
  - Category growth expectations for 2026 and the household-penetration runway, plus the macro/gas-price impact on convenience channel volumes (Questions 1–2).
  - The sourcing of sales and brand overlap between Celsius and Alani Nu, and why core Celsius trends have softened while Alani accelerates (Questions 3–5).
  - The PepsiCo captaincy — what it means, tangible benefits, and summer-season unlocks (Question 4).
  - Celsius SKU rationalization, the Essentials drag, and the innovation/LTO cadence and strategy for both brands (Questions 6–11).
  - The ~$25 million Q4 net inventory benefit, whether shipments now track consumption, and how to think about Alani reported vs. tracked growth (Question 16).
  - Gross margin phasing, the aluminum/Midwest premium exposure, pricing/RGM, SG&A leverage, and capital allocation (Questions 17–21).
- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - The credibility of the brand-Celsius slowdown explanation — Grom directly relayed investor concern that softer core-Celsius trends "could be a sign of things to come" as comparisons get harder; management framed it as deliberate SKU rationalization and a tough launch comp, not a health issue (Question 5).
  - Whether Celsius and Alani Nu cannibalize each other — Grom asked whether greater overlap is emerging as Alani's distribution expands; management said the crossover-consumer data showed "no significant change" over 12 months (Question 4).
  - The risk of a repeat of the prior inventory-correction dynamic — Grom asked whether the Q4 ~$25 million net benefit signals a coming reversal; management said ~$25 million was a pull-forward (Cherry Bomb captured in December) and cautioned against expecting additional pipe-fill, while saying it does not anticipate a repeat of the earlier dynamic (Questions 13, 16).
  - Aluminum / Midwest premium cost uncertainty tied to Middle East events — management said it does not currently source from the region but would not "call that one way or the other" (Question 18).
  - Whether Alani could ultimately exceed Celsius in share — management acknowledged the dynamic but said it manages the portfolio for total health (Question 12).
- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **Brand Celsius ACV ramp on top SKUs (next ~8 weeks):** Management urged investors to watch the top 10–15 SKUs rise in ACV as the SKU rationalization plays out — a near-term proof point.
  - **Celsius innovation layering in Q2–Q4 2026:** After zero Q1 launches, innovation and multiple Celsius LTOs are planned for Q2–Q4, plus potential back-half non-LTO innovation.
  - **Spring 2026 planogram resets (January–May):** Alani Nu planogram resets are described as "barely scratched the surface"; full benefit materializes through May.
  - **Gross margin recovery to "low 50s" (back half 2026):** A stair-step recovery from the 47.4% Q4 trough.
  - **Big Beverage second production line (online back half 2026 / Q4):** Adds margin and efficiency from running two lines in the same plant.
  - **International expansion (Iberia launched March 2026; 3–5 year ramp):** Management expects "much more activity" in international over the next three to five years.
- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** Brand Celsius is lapping four flavor launches from Q1 2025 with zero launches in Q1 2026 — a deliberate innovation-timing comp headwind. Alani Nu comparisons "get a little bit more difficult" as the year progresses given triple-digit early-2026 growth off an already-robust base.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Brand Celsius was at negative YoY growth in Q1 2025, making the early-2026 base easier; Alani was at ~70%–75% growth when acquired.
  - **Timing Shifts:** The ~$25 million Q4 2025 net inventory benefit is characterized as "a little bit of a pull forward out of Q1 into Q4" — the Cherry Bomb LTO (a Q1 scanner phenomenon, sold completely in Q1) was loaded into the Pepsi system in December, so that revenue was captured in December and will not appear in Q1 2026 reported results.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** Alani Nu planogram resets occur January–May 2026; ~17% Celsius space gains and 100%+ Alani space gains are reset-driven. Retailers are shrinking beer cases in convenience and reallocating space toward energy; the brand sources from RTD/drip coffee, hydration, and premium water. Alani is under-served in regions such as New York City — a forward distribution runway.
  - **Structural:** Management expects Alani days-on-hand to stay roughly linear (a function of sell-through) rather than building inventory, and is working with Pepsi to mitigate peaks and valleys.
  - **Velocity:** As ACV expands on lower-ACV Celsius flavors (mid-50s toward 80%+), and Alani enters new regions, velocity dynamics shift.
  - **Margin Cadence:** Q4 2025 carried one-time scrap/returns/freight from the old-to-new distribution transition; gross margin steps up Q1→Q2→back half toward low 50s.
  - **Aluminum/Tariffs:** Aluminum and tariff increases kicked in the back half of 2025 — 2026 partly rolls over that prior-year step-up rather than absorbing it as incremental cost.
  - **Capital Allocation:** $40 million repurchased in Q4 2025, $260 million remaining; ~$700 million of debt to continue paying down.
- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** No FX guidance given. On pricing, management described a multi-brand price-pack-promotion strategy (Alani as "super premium," Celsius "premium," Rockstar "premium economy"), with RGM able to operate "from an exponential level" versus the prior single-brand approach.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** The most material structural change remains the move from single-brand Celsius to a three-brand portfolio (Alani Nu and Rockstar acquisitions). Rockstar 2026 is a stabilization year with SKU rationalization, refocused on a more male-heavy 16-ounce consumer.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** The Q4 2025 ~$25 million net benefit reshapes Q1 2026 reported revenue comparability (Cherry Bomb revenue captured in December 2025 rather than Q1 2026). Rockstar (acquired August 28, 2025) is ~a quarter behind Alani on integration, fully integrated by end of Q2 2026.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Management cited rising gas prices and an "uneasy consumer backdrop" but said reports show no correlation between gas prices/foot traffic and energy sales. Aluminum cost uncertainty is tied to Middle East events (~9% of global aluminum production); management does not currently source from there. Some Alani LTOs (Witch's Brew, Winter Wonderland) are seasonal and not expected to recur off-season.

#### Core Questioning Principles

**Q1 — Q1 2026 Brand Celsius Reported Revenue Magnitude**

**Building Blocks:** Context Reinstatement + Bracketing + Time-Bounding. **Q:** "You said brand Celsius scanner now looks very similar to the sell-in to Pepsi — for the full first quarter, do you expect reported brand Celsius revenue growth closer to flat or to high-single-digits given the zero-launch comp?"

- **Rationale:** Management said scanner and sell-in are aligned and that Q1 has zero launches versus four in Q1 2025, but gave no quantified Q1 reported-revenue expectation.
- **Question Type:** Context Reinstatement + Bracketing
- **Question Topic(s):** Brand Celsius reported revenue, Q1 2026, innovation comp
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 1
- **Question Importance Rank:** 1

**Q2 — Size of the Q1 2026 Pull-Forward Headwind**

**Building Blocks:** Component Breakdown + Precision. **Q:** "Of the roughly $25 million Q4 net benefit you describe as a pull-forward out of Q1, how much specifically was the Cherry Bomb LTO revenue captured in December that will be absent from Q1 2026 reported sales?"

- **Rationale:** Management said Cherry Bomb was "a big bulk" of the $25 million but did not isolate the dollar figure, which is needed to model the Q1 Alani headwind.
- **Question Type:** Component Breakdown + Precision
- **Question Topic(s):** Inventory pull-forward, Alani Q1 modeling
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 1
- **Question Importance Rank:** 2

**Q3 — Brand Celsius SKU Rationalization Endpoint**

**Building Blocks:** Reverse Chronology + Precision. **Q:** "When the brand Celsius SKU rationalization is complete, how many SKUs will the brand carry, and what target ACV do you expect for the flavors currently in the mid-50s?"

- **Rationale:** Management cited trimming the tail and lifting mid-50s ACV flavors toward 80%+ but gave no final SKU count or completion-state ACV target.
- **Question Type:** Reverse Chronology + Precision
- **Question Topic(s):** SKU rationalization, ACV, brand Celsius
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 2
- **Question Importance Rank:** 3

**Q4 — Mid-50s Gross Margin Timeline**

**Building Blocks:** Bracketing + Component Breakdown. **Q:** "You cited a 'handful of years' to reach the mid-50s gross margin — is that closer to 2027 or 2029, and how much of the bridge from the low-50s comes from the second production line versus RGM?"

- **Rationale:** "Handful of years" and "mid 50s" are imprecise; bracketing the timeline and splitting the drivers is essential for a multi-year margin model.
- **Question Type:** Bracketing + Component Breakdown
- **Question Topic(s):** Gross margin, multi-year trajectory, RGM
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 2
- **Question Importance Rank:** 4

**Q5 — Aluminum / Midwest Premium 2026 Sensitivity**

**Building Blocks:** Scenario Testing + Time-Bounding. **Q:** "If the Midwest aluminum premium stays elevated through 2026, what gross margin impact have you factored into the back-half low-50s target, and how much could ease in 2027 as new capacity comes online?"

- **Rationale:** Management said it "factored in those different things for the year" but gave no quantified sensitivity to a sustained-elevated Midwest premium scenario.
- **Question Type:** Scenario Testing + Time-Bounding
- **Question Topic(s):** Aluminum costs, Midwest premium, gross margin
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 3
- **Question Importance Rank:** 5
