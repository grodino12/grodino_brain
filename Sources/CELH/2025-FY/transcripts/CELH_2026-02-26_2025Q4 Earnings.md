## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** Management framed 2025 as a "defining year," highlighting full-year record revenue of $2.5 billion (with two $2 billion brands in the portfolio) and characterizing the year as one of disciplined execution while building a three-brand "Modern Energy" platform (Celsius, Alani Nu, Rockstar). The transcript contains no explicit consensus-comparison commentary, but the headline narrative centered on scale validation and integration progress rather than a clean beat/miss. Positive drivers: Alani Nu delivered record Q4 net sales of $370 million (+136% pro forma YoY) and contributed $1 billion of net sales in the nine months since acquisition; brand Celsius scanner data was a "healthy 12.8%" for the quarter and full-year brand Celsius net sales grew 7.5% to $1.46 billion; full-year adjusted EBITDA reached $619.6 million (24.6% margin); the Alani US DSD transition into Pepsi was substantially complete at year end; and the first Alani LTO in the Pepsi system (Cherry Bomb) "ran out in record time." Headwinds and challenges: Q4 GAAP gross margin compressed to 47.4% from 50.2% on Rockstar dilution, integration costs, and tariffs; brand Celsius reported GAAP sales declined 7.7% in Q4 due to timing/sequencing of inventory movements as Alani was loaded into the Pepsi network (though the net portfolio benefit was ~$25 million); Rockstar required split accounting ($45M Q4 net sales / $6M other income; $56M / $13M full year); and working capital reflected transition-driven timing dynamics. The strategic outlook was confident and execution-focused: management guided to gross margins returning to the low-50s in the back half of 2026 as integrations complete (Alani by end of Q1, Rockstar by end of Q2 / first half), expects triple-digit Alani space gains and ~17% Celsius space gains coming out of spring resets, and emphasized RGM, portfolio SKU prioritization, and international expansion. The overall tone was steady, disciplined, and reassuring — leaning heavily on the "transitory vs. underlying trend" framing of Q4 noise.
- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:** Analysts concentrated on the following themes —
  - The timing and magnitude of spring shelf-space resets and when reported sales would reflect the gains, plus whether brand Celsius reported sales improve in Q1 (Question 1).
  - Unpacking the ~$25 million net inventory benefit between Celsius and Alani, and whether Alani inventory stays elevated with a delayed unwind risk (Question 2).
  - The gross margin trajectory — phasing through 2026, the Midwest premium / tariff impact, and the multi-year path to the mid-50s (Question 3).
  - Three-brand SKU prioritization, the Celsius LTO cadence, velocity dynamics as shelf space expands, and sales predictability with Pepsi visibility (Question 4).
  - The source of category-wide shelf-space gains and how to think about velocity growth versus TDP gains in new geographies, especially convenience (Question 5).
  - Whether any Alani Q4 inventory benefit was a revenue pull-forward, and how to contextualize the ~100%+ Alani space gain versus Celsius's 2022 Pepsi transition (Question 6).
- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - The credibility and durability of the brand Celsius "7.7% GAAP decline vs. 12.8% scanner" gap — analysts repeatedly probed whether Q4 distortions were truly transitory and whether Q1 reported sales would improve (Questions 1, 2, 6).
  - Whether the ~$25 million net benefit and Alani load-in constitute a revenue pull-forward that must be given back in future quarters — Gerald Pascarelli asked this directly; management characterized it as "opportunistic," not a pull-forward (Question 6).
  - The risk that Alani inventory remains elevated with an unwind pushed out to a "4Q into '27 dynamic," echoing the Celsius/Pepsi transition years earlier (Question 2).
  - Velocity dilution risk — as Alani expands into many new geographies and channels, management acknowledged "we will likely see a lower velocity entering new segments," a tension against the strong TDP/space gains (Questions 4, 5).
  - The pace of margin recovery given tariff and Midwest premium uncertainty — management conceded timing "could be some impact" depending on where those costs go (Question 3).
- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **Spring 2026 shelf-space resets:** Management expects ~17% Celsius space gains and triple-digit (~100%+) Alani space gains to materialize "through the end of spring," a major reported-sales catalyst heading into the summer selling season.
  - **Alani integration completion (end of Q1 2026):** Completes the cost-structure alignment for Alani and should reduce timing noise.
  - **Rockstar integration completion (first half / end of Q2 2026):** Full transition to the finished-goods model (US in Q1, Canada in H1) and cost-structure alignment.
  - **Gross margin recovery to low-50s (back half 2026):** A key proof point after the 47.4% Q4 trough.
  - **Celsius and Alani LTO pipeline (lime slush already launched; more in 2026):** Innovation cadence positioned to drive trial and incremental occasions.
  - **Fizz Free national launch:** Rolled out nationally to kick off 2026, targeting consumers who prefer non-carbonated beverages.
- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** Q4 2025 GAAP gross margin of 47.4% was down from 50.2% in Q4 2024, depressed by Rockstar dilution, integration costs, and tariffs — a structurally lower base distorting straight margin YoY reads.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Management noted "Just a year ago, we were coming off a period in which both the category and brands CELSIUS experienced pressure in the back half of 2024, with some continued softness in the first quarter of 2025" — making 2026 comps easier for brand Celsius.
  - **Timing Shifts:** Q4 2025 brand Celsius GAAP sales declined 7.7% (vs. 12.8% scanner) because the company managed timing/sequencing of inventory movements within the Pepsi network — specifically adjusting "an additional week for brand CELSIUS" and loading in additional Alani at quarter-end; the combined net portfolio benefit was ~$25 million. Cherry Bomb LTO units loaded in at year-end depleted in record time, supporting triple-digit growth in the first six to eight weeks of 2026.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** Alani Q4 record net sales benefited from increased distribution points and increased orders during the move into the Pepsi system; ~17% Celsius and triple-digit (~100%+) Alani space gains expected from spring 2026 resets, biggest gains in convenience (~60% of energy sales). Management noted retailers are reallocating space to energy from juice, premium water, and beer coolers.
  - **Structural:** Management cautioned that periods of integration and large customer ordering cycles "can still create some quarter-to-quarter variability."
  - **Velocity:** Management warned Alani velocity will likely be lower as it enters new regions/channels, similar to the Celsius broad-distribution expansion experience.
  - **Operating cash flow / working capital:** Working capital reflected transition-driven inventory positioning and order cadence; management expects working capital volatility to moderate as cadence normalizes.
- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** No FX guidance provided. On pricing, management stressed RGM is "not about broad-based price increases" but about mix, channel-based price-pack architecture, pack strategy, and disciplined promotion.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** The most material structural change is the portfolio composition — Alani Nu (acquired ~April 2025, "$1 billion to our net sales" in nine months) and Rockstar are now consolidated, fundamentally changing revenue and margin comparability. The portfolio now includes two $2 billion brands.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** Rockstar required split accounting during integration — Q4: $45M in net sales and $6M in other income; full year: $56M in net sales and $13M in other income. The US Rockstar business transitions fully to the finished-goods model in Q1 2026; the Canadian portion transitions in the first half of 2026 (remaining in other income until then).
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Management cited the Midwest aluminum premium increase and tariffs as cost headwinds whose timing affects the margin-recovery pace; declining alcohol consumption is cited as a tailwind opening social occasions for energy.

#### Core Questioning Principles

**Q1 — Q1 2026 Brand Celsius Reported Sales vs. Scanner Gap**

**Building Blocks:** Context Reinstatement + Bracketing + Time-Bounding. **Q:** "You said brand Celsius orders are aligning more closely to track data in early 2026 — for the full first quarter, do you expect reported brand Celsius sales to land within roughly 2 points of the scanner reading, or could the gap still be wider?"

- **Rationale:** Management said early-2026 Celsius orders align more closely to track data but explicitly declined forward-looking specificity; bounding the expected gap is critical to modeling Q1.
- **Question Type:** Context Reinstatement + Bracketing
- **Question Topic(s):** Brand Celsius reported sales, scanner-shipment gap, Q1 2026
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 1
- **Question Importance Rank:** 1

**Q2 — Reversal of the ~$25 Million Q4 Net Benefit**

**Building Blocks:** Presumptive Framing + Time-Bounding. **Q:** "The roughly $25 million net Q4 portfolio benefit from inventory positioning — across which specific quarters of 2026 does that timing benefit reverse out of reported results?"

- **Rationale:** Management quantified the ~$25M Q4 net benefit and said it was opportunistic rather than a pull-forward, but did not specify when (or whether) the timing benefit normalizes — a key reported-sales modeling issue.
- **Question Type:** Presumptive Question with Time-Bounding
- **Question Topic(s):** Inventory timing, reported revenue cadence, 2026 modeling
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 1
- **Question Importance Rank:** 2

**Q3 — Q1 2026 Gross Margin Step**

**Building Blocks:** Baseline Establishment + Component Breakdown. **Q:** "Q4 gross margin was 47.4% and you said the one-time COGS write-offs and scrap are gone after Q4 — how many basis points of Q1 improvement come purely from removing those one-time items versus integration progress?"

- **Rationale:** Management said one-time Q4 transition costs benefit Q1 "directly" but did not size the step-up; quantifying the bridge from 47.4% is essential to phasing the margin recovery.
- **Question Type:** Baseline Establishment + Component Breakdown
- **Question Topic(s):** Gross margin phasing, one-time costs, Q1 2026
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 2
- **Question Importance Rank:** 3

**Q4 — Tariff / Midwest Premium Sensitivity**

**Building Blocks:** Bracketing + Scenario Testing. **Q:** "If tariffs and the Midwest aluminum premium stay at current levels through year-end, what gross margin do you reach in the back half — and how much higher could it be if both ease by mid-year?"

- **Rationale:** Management said tariff/Midwest premium timing "could be some impact" and that easing "quicker" gets to numbers "quicker," but gave no quantified sensitivity range.
- **Question Type:** Bracketing + Scenario Testing
- **Question Topic(s):** Input costs, tariffs, gross margin sensitivity
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 2
- **Question Importance Rank:** 4

**Q5 — Alani Velocity Trajectory in New Markets**

**Building Blocks:** Reverse Chronology + Precision. **Q:** "As Alani enters new convenience geographies at lower initial velocity, what velocity level do those new doors need to reach to be productive, and how many quarters does that build-up typically take based on the Celsius experience?"

- **Rationale:** Management acknowledged Alani velocity will be lower in new markets but gave no productivity threshold or ramp timeline.
- **Question Type:** Reverse Chronology + Precision
- **Question Topic(s):** Alani velocity, distribution productivity, convenience
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 3
- **Question Importance Rank:** 5
