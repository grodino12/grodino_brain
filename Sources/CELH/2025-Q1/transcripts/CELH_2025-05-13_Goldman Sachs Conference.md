## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** Speaking eight weeks into Eric Hansen's tenure as the company's first President & COO, management framed Celsius Holdings as being at a "pivotal point" — newly equipped with a two-brand portfolio (Celsius and the recently closed Alani Nu acquisition) and a combined ~16-17% dollar share. Hansen, a 28-year PepsiCo veteran, characterized his mandate as building the organization, people, tools and processes to shift Celsius from a "one brand-focused, very U.S.-centric" company toward a portfolio-oriented, global operator. Management explained the Q1 2025 revenue softness as "noise" — innovation timing shifting later into the quarter versus front-loaded in Q1 2024, the lapping of the Essentials launch, promotional timing, and a very tough ~35-37% prior-year comp — while emphasizing that core Celsius has shown "a couple of months of week-over-week growth in dollars" and is "working its way back," with Alani Nu described as "currently a rocket ship." Positive drivers cited were the better-for-you/zero-sugar category shift (now over 50% of the category), Q1 gross margin above 52% (up ~100 bps year-over-year), $50 million of identified Alani Nu synergies, the fully integrated Big Beverage manufacturing facility (kitted for a second line), and a planned fall national LTO and a new marketing campaign launching within ~30 days. Acknowledged headwinds and challenges included core Celsius dollar share down slightly year-over-year, too many flavors/SKUs in the portfolio that retailers have flagged and that need rationalizing, lower-than-target ACV on top sellers, second-half gross margin conservatism tied to tariffs and commodity inflation (full-year pegged at ~50%), and a still-early Alani Nu integration where most in-market plans were set before Celsius took control. The strategic outlook was confident and constructive: management is focused on integrating Alani Nu over the next ~18 months, paying down debt, building a Dublin-based global operations team, and leveraging portfolio "optionality." Overall tone was measured, execution-focused and forward-looking.

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - **Q1 underlying growth and the path to reacceleration (Q5, Q7):** The moderator sought to strip the "noise" out of Q1 to understand the true underlying Celsius growth trend and what specifically improves the trajectory through the back half.
  - **Core Celsius share softness and SKU rationalization (Q7, Q8):** The moderator probed what is weighing on core Celsius share and pressed on retailer feedback that the portfolio has too many flavors, asking whether rationalization is coming.
  - **Alani Nu integration, synergies and P&L flow-through (Q3, Q12):** The moderator asked what Alani Nu is doing better, what synergies and margin convergence look like, and how the brands will interact under common ownership.
  - **Gross margin and tariff exposure (Q15, Q16):** The moderator questioned why management is more cautious on second-half margin, what share of COGS is aluminum, and what mitigation levers exist.
  - **International strategy and competitive environment (Q19, Q20, Q21):** The moderator explored how Celsius drives daily consumption internationally, how it balances U.S. versus global expansion, and how it views Monster and Red Bull competitively.

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - **Underlying Q1 trend obscured by "noise":** Management repeatedly characterized Q1 as full of timing "noise" rather than quantifying a clean underlying Celsius growth rate, leaving the moderator to infer the true trend (Q5).
  - **Core Celsius share decline vs. portfolio framing:** Pressed on why core Celsius share slipped year-over-year, Hansen pivoted toward the combined ~17% portfolio share and channels already over 20%, and notably declined to reaffirm the prior ~15% Celsius share goal — "I don't know if I were talking about goals in particular" (Q9).
  - **SKU/flavor overload acknowledged by retailers:** The moderator cited direct retailer feedback that there are "too many flavors," and management conceded the need to rationalize SKUs and improve ACV on top sellers — an implicit admission of an execution gap (Q8).
  - **Second-half margin conservatism:** Management framed the ~50% full-year gross margin (vs. 52%+ in Q1) explicitly as "conservatism" against unknown tariffs and commodity inflation rather than a known cost headwind, leaving the actual back-half margin path uncertain (Q15, Q16).
  - **Aluminum COGS exposure unquantified:** Asked directly what percentage of COGS is aluminum, Langhans could not provide a figure — "off the top of my head, I don't have it, but it's consistent with the beverage category" (Q16).

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **Alani Nu modeling call (Memorial Day week, ~late May 2025):** Langhans confirmed the public Alani Nu modeling call is targeted for Memorial Day week. It is a catalyst because it will provide pro forma combined-business detail and frame Street modeling of the consolidated P&L.
  - **New Celsius marketing campaign (within ~30 days of May 13, 2025):** Management said a marketing campaign launches within ~30 days, timed to capture summer growth and refocus on the core Celsius consumer — a test of whether marketing can re-accelerate core velocity.
  - **Fall 2025 national winter-themed LTO:** Celsius is launching its first national, all-channel limited-time-offer with a winter theme in the back half, with marketing resources behind it — a departure from front-loaded SKU launches and a test of the Alani Nu-style LTO playbook applied to Celsius.
  - **SKU rationalization / set right-sizing (end of 2025):** Management said it will start balancing the flavor portfolio and right-sizing sets toward year-end — a potential margin and velocity inflection.
  - **Big Beverage second production line / Alani Nu insourcing (over next ~18 months):** Adding a second line at Big Beverage and moving a portion of Alani Nu volume in-house (~25% of current capacity once two lines run) is a forward margin lever.

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** Hansen flagged Q1 2025 lapped a Q1 2024 with "something like 35%, 37% growth," with most 2024 innovation front-loaded into early Q1 and the nationwide Essentials launch occurring in Q1 2024 but not Q1 2025. "We came out super strong with most of our innovation at the beginning of Q1 last year, and it happened more towards the end of Q1 this year, excluding the launch of Essentials, which happened last year and didn't happen this year."
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Hansen noted the category "softened a little bit" in the back half of 2024, and that Celsius has "got some softer comps" looking into Q2 and especially the back half of 2025.
  - **Timing Shifts:** Innovation timing shifted from front-loaded in 2024 to spread "more coming across throughout the year" in 2025, including a back-half LTO; promotional timing and activity also differed across the quarter, contributing to the Q1 "noise."
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** Management expects combined-portfolio distribution to expand ~15% to 20% in 2025, with space coming from larger-customer set growth, Subway, Home Depot expansion (~18,000 stores), an additional ~15,000 small-format coolers, growth of energy space within total beverage, and independent-set increases within the Pepsi system. 2025 reset plans are largely locked; further space gains are a 2026 opportunity. Management said the rumored Walmart energy-space expansion timing is "TBD." Essentials ACV is in the mid-60s, with distribution opportunity in the convenience-and-gas channel.

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** Celsius took headline pricing in Q4 2024 but has been promoting "a lot of it back"; management said it took the headline price to "get it" and retains future "optionality." A recent mix shift toward singles (growing faster than multipacks) created an optical price/mix uplift in scanner data that is "really just a mix impact."
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** The Alani Nu acquisition has closed; management identified $50 million of synergies (a significant portion headcount/integration-related), a 12-month transition service agreement, a carved-out Alani Nu marketing/brand team, and a ~18-month integration runway after which Alani Nu's P&L profile is expected to converge with Celsius's. Alani Nu is "a couple of years behind" Celsius on its P&L profile.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** Management referenced the upcoming Alani Nu modeling call (Memorial Day week) for purchase-accounting and pro forma detail; no specific accounting figures were quantified in this transcript.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Management cited tariffs as the primary source of second-half conservatism — "We don't really know where the tariffs are going to go. Knock on wood, they seem to be in better shape this week. So granted they were... probably at their peak a couple of weeks ago when we had the call" — alongside expected commodity/aluminum inflation. The fall LTO is winter-themed.
