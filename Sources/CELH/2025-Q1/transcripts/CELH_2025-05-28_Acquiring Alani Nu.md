## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** This was a dedicated financial-modeling call held to help analysts model the combined Celsius Holdings business following the April 1, 2025 close of the Alani Nu acquisition. Management framed the deal as creating a leading better-for-you functional lifestyle platform — a strong #3 player in the U.S. energy category at roughly 16% combined share and approximately $2 billion in 2024 pro forma revenue — with Alani Nu (the #4 U.S. energy drink, founded 2018, a 2022-2024 revenue CAGR of ~49%, ~$605 million in 2024 net revenues and ~$88 million / ~15% adjusted EBITDA) complementary to Celsius via its female-centric, lifestyle positioning. The CFO delivered the substantive modeling guidance: full-year 2025 combined GAAP gross margin in the high 40s; an approximate $15 million inventory step-up (allocate ~80% to Q2, ~20% to Q3, ~1% full-year gross-margin impact); GAAP SG&A as a percentage of revenue in the low 30s; an additional ~1% SG&A impact from purchase-accounting customer-list amortization; a ~25-26% effective tax rate (front-half-weighted); ~$19 million per quarter of interest expense beginning Q2 2025 (~$57 million full-year, on $900 million of debt at a 7.5% assumed rate, SOFR + 325 bps); a resulting adjusted EBITDA margin profile of ~17% to 20%; and $50 million of run-rate cost synergies (mostly headcount) reaching full run rate by April 2026 (~$4.2 million per month), with only $5-10 million captured across Q4 2025 and Q1 2026. Acknowledged headwinds and complexities included the purchase-accounting noise (inventory step-up, amortization), the interest-expense burden from the acquisition debt, the variability of the floating rate, the difficulty of reconciling Alani Nu's GAAP-recognized revenue against Circana scanner data given LTO timing, and the deferred timing of synergy realization (mostly 2026). Notably, management explicitly declined to provide a revenue outlook for either Alani Nu or core Celsius. The strategic outlook was confident: management reiterated cash-EPS accretion in the first full year, a 24-month integration plan (most work in the first 12 months under a 12-month transition services agreement with Congo Brands), and significant cash-flow generation. Overall tone was technical, disciplined and execution-focused.

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - **Revenue growth for Alani Nu and core Celsius (Q1):** Peter Grom pressed for any color on revenue growth given management deliberately withheld a revenue outlook, and sought help interpreting the large scanner-vs-GAAP gap heading into Q2.
  - **Scanner-vs-GAAP reconciliation and LTO timing (Q1):** Analysts wanted to understand how to model the disconnect between Circana scanner data and Alani Nu's GAAP-recognized revenue, given LTO launch spikes and pipe-fill.
  - **Marketing spend allocation and LTO cadence (Q2):** Jim Salera asked how marketing spend will be allocated across the two brands, whether it tracks as a percentage of sales, and whether seasonal LTOs alternate or run concurrently.
  - **Product mix and adjacent categories (Q3):** David Shakno asked whether the non-energy mix (sticks, shakes, snacks, protein) will persist and whether those categories are incremental to legacy Celsius.
  - **Distributor termination fees and network plans (Q4):** Robert Moskow asked who received the ~$10.7 million distributor termination fees and whether there is an update on the distributor network.

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - **Absence of a revenue outlook:** Peter Grom opened by noting "my guess is there is a reason you didn't want to provide a revenue outlook," and management indeed gave only qualitative color, redirecting analysts to track scanner data — leaving the revenue line for the combined business analyst-modeled rather than guided (Q1).
  - **Scanner-vs-GAAP credibility:** The CFO conceded the reconciliation is "tricky" and "really contingent on how successful our LTOs are," citing swings from +39% (Q3) to +53% (Q4) consolidated growth, and Alani Nu up 65-70% in Q1 partly due to Sherbet Swirl pipe-fill — underscoring how dependent reported growth is on LTO timing rather than clean underlying demand (Q1).
  - **Synergy timing skewed to 2026:** Despite a $50 million headline synergy figure, management clarified only $5-10 million would be captured across Q4 2025 and Q1 2026, with full run rate not reached until April 2026 — a longer ramp than the headline number might imply (Prepared Remarks).
  - **Non-core product portfolio under review:** Management said several Alani Nu products beyond RTD energy and sticks are "under evaluation based on our internal profitability algorithms," signaling possible rationalization rather than a committed plan for the full inherited portfolio (Q3).
  - **No distributor-network update:** Asked about the distributor network, management said it is focused on integration and existing customers/partners and "we don't have an update at this time," leaving the future distribution architecture (including any Pepsi role) unresolved (Q4).

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **Q2 2025 results (first quarter with Alani Nu consolidated):** Q2 2025 will be the first reported period including Alani Nu, the ~$19 million quarterly interest expense, and ~80% of the $15 million inventory step-up — a key test of the modeling framework laid out on this call.
  - **Synergy run-rate ramp (Q4 2025 through April 2026):** Synergies inflect from $5-10 million across Q4 2025/Q1 2026 to a full ~$50 million run rate (~$4.2 million/month) by April 2026 — a margin catalyst that builds through 2026.
  - **End of the 12-month transition services agreement (~April 2026):** The TSA with Congo Brands runs 12 months; its expiration coincides with full synergy realization and the bulk of the integration work being complete.
  - **Adjacent-category expansion decisions (back half 2025 into 2026):** Management is "further analyzing" the protein and stick-pack opportunities and evaluating other inherited products; decisions on building out or rationalizing these could shift the mix and margin profile.
  - **Holiday/seasonal LTO launches (Q4 2025):** Management said it will re-launch the Witch's Brew holiday edition that drove the Q4 2024 spike, a near-term scanner-data and revenue catalyst.

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** The CFO highlighted that Alani Nu's reported growth is heavily LTO-driven and lumpy: consolidated growth ran +39% in Q3 2024 and +53% in Q4 2024, the latter inflated by the brand-new holiday-edition launch — meaning future quarters that lap a strong LTO launch will face difficult comparisons. Alani Nu was up 65-70% year-over-year in Q1 2025, partly inflated by the Sherbet Swirl LTO pipe-fill.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** None specifically quantified as "easy" comps in this transcript.
  - **Timing Shifts:** The CFO repeatedly flagged that LTO launches cause pipe-fill that shifts revenue between periods — "with those LTOs, they spike up and then they come back down." Alani Nu recognizes GAAP revenue on delivery (to retailer distribution centers, consumers and distributors), while Circana recognizes consumer scans at the register, creating timing disconnects that "over a longer period of time" converge. Celsius's own innovation timing also shifted from front-loaded in 2024 to spread through 2025.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** A ~$10.7 million distributor termination-fee item reflects Alani Nu switching distributors before the acquisition (notification-period multiples of gross profit paid on exit). Management gave no update on the go-forward distributor network. Alani Nu is currently distributed internationally only through Canada, with other countries "in the pipeline."

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** No specific FX or pricing-anniversary figures were disclosed on this call.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** Alani Nu was acquired April 1, 2025 and is excluded from Q1 2025 results — only three quarters of Alani Nu results appear in CELH's 2025 financials. Combined 2024 pro forma revenue was ~$2 billion. Alani Nu's 2024 sales mix was ~83% RTD energy and ~6% protein shakes (remainder pre-workouts, snacks, stick packs); the combined business is more than 90% RTD energy. The $50 million run-rate cost synergies are expected over two years post-closing.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** Purchase accounting creates an ~$15 million inventory step-up (opening inventory $15 million higher on April 1; allocate ~80% to Q2, ~20% to Q3; ~1% full-year gross-margin drag) and customer-list amortization in SG&A (~1% impact full-year and per quarter). Brand/trademark assets and goodwill will be recorded but not amortized monthly — instead evaluated annually for impairment. The 2025 effective tax rate is projected at ~25-26%, front-half-weighted, due to tax-vs-book treatment of stock-based compensation and timing of purchases/sales/amortization within the org structure.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Interest expense of ~$19 million per quarter (~$57 million full-year) begins Q2 2025 on $900 million of debt at a 7.5% assumed rate; the rate is variable (SOFR + 325 bps excluding debt issuance costs) and fluctuates with SOFR. Alani Nu's revenue exhibits LTO-driven seasonality, with stronger holiday-edition launches creating Q4 spikes.
