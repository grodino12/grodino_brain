## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** In a presentation and Q&A at the William Blair 45th Annual Growth Stock Conference, Celsius Holdings' Chief of Staff Toby David framed the company around four key themes: category disruption (Celsius and Alani Nu drove 50% of all U.S. energy category growth in 2024), a large and growing functional-beverage TAM beyond traditional energy, a compelling "more people, more places, more often" growth strategy, and a strong cash-generation profile deployed through disciplined capital allocation. He positioned Celsius Holdings as the #3 U.S. energy portfolio at a 16.6% tracked-channel share — closer to #2 Monster than to the #4 brand below it — with $3.5 billion in 2024 retail sales and ~$2 billion in 2024 pro forma net revenue. Positive drivers cited included Celsius brand ACV of 98.7% across 241,000+ U.S. outlets, Alani Nu growing at triple digits with ACV in the low-80s (significant upside), strong strong-market shares (South Florida mid-20s, New York/Boston near 20, L.A. ~15), the sugar-free shift (over 50% of the category) and female consumers driving category growth, Big Beverage vertical integration, the new "Live Fit Go" marketing campaign, and Q1 2025 gross margin of 52%. Acknowledged headwinds were candid: Celsius had a "rocket ship" run of triple-digit growth from 2020-2024 but hit "a little bit of a slow period last year," and has maintained roughly an 11% market share over the trailing nine months — management's framing is that this is a strong consumer foundation to re-accelerate from, not a structural decline. Alani Nu's lower gross margin (roughly 8-10 points below Celsius) was attributed to scale, not mix. The strategic outlook was confident and execution-focused: a first-ever Celsius limited-time offering in Q3/Q4, a comprehensive 360-degree marketing push, 15-20% greater core-Celsius distribution largely already on shelf, a 24-month Alani Nu integration toward Celsius's margin profile, ~one-times net leverage with debt paydown, and continued opportunistic M&A evaluation with Alani Nu integration as the explicit number-one priority. Overall tone was upbeat, promotional in the positive sense, and oriented toward a return to share growth.

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - **Category growth trajectory and 2025 expectations (Q1):** The moderator asked what Celsius is seeing more recently in the energy category after last year's slowdown and what expectations are for 2025.
  - **Shelf-space gains — brand mix and timing (Q2):** The moderator asked whether the 15-20% distribution gain is Celsius brand, Alani Nu, or both, and when those gains hit shelves.
  - **Alani Nu distribution (ABI vs. Pepsi) (Q3):** The moderator probed whether Alani Nu would become part of the Pepsi distribution system.
  - **Marketing spend and broadening the brand (Q4):** The moderator asked whether broadening Celsius beyond the gym user to mainstream occasions means spending more on marketing.
  - **Alani Nu margin gap, innovation/caffeine, and Big Beverage capacity (Q5, Q6, Q7):** The moderator explored why Alani Nu margins lag Celsius, whether a lower-caffeine/hand-size innovation could broaden appeal, and the ~20% Big Beverage production opportunity.

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - **Celsius's share plateau vs. the growth narrative:** Management candidly acknowledged Celsius's "slow period" and that it has "maintained a market share of roughly about 11%" over the trailing nine months — reframing a flat share as a strong foundation to re-accelerate from rather than a decline (Presentation; Q1).
  - **Alani Nu distribution uncertainty:** Asked directly whether Alani Nu would move into the Pepsi system, management said "we haven't made any decisions yet and there's really nothing to talk about publicly," citing optionality between Pepsi and the fragmented AB Independent Beer Network — leaving the route-to-market architecture an open question (Q3).
  - **Alani Nu margin gap framed as scale, not mix:** Asked whether Alani Nu's ~8-10-point lower margin reflects its non-energy mix or scale, management firmly attributed it to scale ("It's just scale"), citing shared ingredients, can suppliers and manufacturing facilities and the buying-power benefit of moving from $1.5 billion to $2-plus billion (Q5).
  - **Crossover/cannibalization skepticism:** Management pushed back on the market view that there is "a lot of crossover between Celsius and Alani Nu," asserting only ~1-2% of Alani Nu followers also follow Celsius and that the brands serve very differentiated consumers (Presentation).
  - **Marketing spend ambiguity:** Asked whether broadening the brand means more spending, management said marketing-spend percentages were addressed on the modeling call (Celsius Holdings level, including Alani Nu) and anticipated "probably a little bit of additional spend" behind the Live Fit Go initiative — without quantifying it (Q4).

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **First-ever Celsius limited-time offering (Q3/Q4 2025):** Management said Celsius will run its first LTO in Q3/Q4, framed as an excellent way to drive consumption frequency within the existing base — a near-term test of the Alani Nu-style LTO playbook.
  - **"Live Fit Go" marketing campaign (announced the week of the conference):** A newly announced comprehensive 360-degree marketing initiative (digital, TV, social) to broaden the Celsius message beyond the gym user to mainstream consumers — a key enabler of re-accelerated share.
  - **Summer 2025 innovation (a couple of new SKUs) and incremental distribution:** New SKUs launch over the summer with additional placements, on top of the 15-20% core-Celsius distribution already largely on shelf.
  - **Big Beverage second production line (~20% of total production opportunity):** Adding a second line at the North Carolina plant — already partly built out — to bring ~20% of combined Celsius/Alani Nu production in-house and serve most of the Southeast, a forward margin catalyst.
  - **Alani Nu integration over 24 months (toward Celsius's margin profile):** Rolling Alani Nu into Celsius's supplier contracts and scaling buying power toward a Celsius-like margin profile is a multi-quarter margin catalyst.
  - **Lower-caffeine / smaller-can innovation for core Celsius:** Management said it is exploring an Alani Nu-style 100-milligram-caffeine smaller can for core Celsius — a potential occasion-broadening innovation.

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** Management noted Celsius was a "rocket ship" with triple-digit growth for ~3.5 years through 2024, then hit "a little bit of a slow period last year" — implying difficult prior-year comparisons as Celsius cycles its peak growth.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** None specifically quantified as "easy" comps in this transcript.
  - **Timing Shifts:** Management flagged that Alani Nu, like Celsius a few years ago, has a discrepancy between reported net revenue and Circana/Nielsen scanner data — driven by order ebbs and flows during a growth phase — and said the modeling call's five-quarter view was provided for that perspective.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** Core Celsius distribution is up 15-20% in 2025 (CEO-cited target), with the bulk already on shelf from January-May shelf resets and a couple of large retailers still to come. Alani Nu news/distribution gains in 2025 were described as "substantial" though not publicly detailed, with further distribution upside for the remainder of 2025 and into 2026. Celsius distributes via Pepsi (signed August 1, 2022, on trucks October 1, 2022) and the AB Independent Beer Network; Alani Nu distributes via the AB network. Convenience-channel foot traffic improved (helped by lower gas prices).
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics (continued):** Combined Celsius + Alani Nu hold 16.6% U.S. tracked-channel share; Celsius brand has 98.7% ACV across 241,000+ outlets while Alani Nu ACV is in the low-80s. LTOs (Monster, Red Bull, Alani Nu) were cited as a major driver of category growth.

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** No specific FX or pricing-anniversary figures were disclosed. Management cited lower gas prices as a tailwind to convenience-channel foot traffic.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** The Alani Nu acquisition closed April 1, 2025; Alani Nu did roughly $600 million in 2024 net revenue (~46% year-over-year growth) and is now growing at triple digits. Combined 2024 pro forma net revenue was ~$2 billion with a ~48% combined pro forma gross margin. Alani Nu's 2024 sales were ~83% energy drinks and ~6% protein shakes (remainder pre-workout, snacks, stick packs). Big Beverage (a North Carolina contract-manufacturing plant) was acquired in November 2024 for vertical integration. A 24-month Alani Nu integration is expected to bring its margin profile in line with Celsius.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** Management referenced the May 2025 modeling call for detailed 2025 figures and purchase-accounting context; on this call it cited net leverage of approximately one-times post-Alani Nu with ample balance-sheet cash and a goal of reducing leverage over time. The Dublin center of excellence was set up in 2024 for innovation, global procurement, supply chain and global marketing.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Management said the category grew ~7% in Q1 2025 and accelerated in the trailing four weeks, with last year's slowdown attributed largely to macro issues; convenience foot traffic improved on lower gas prices heading into the summer season.
