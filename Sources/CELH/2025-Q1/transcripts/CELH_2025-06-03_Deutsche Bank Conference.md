## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** In a Deutsche Bank dbAccess fireside chat in Paris — the company's first appearance at the conference — management framed Celsius Holdings as a global maker of premium lifestyle energy drinks at the center of an energy-category "renaissance," now armed with a two-brand portfolio (Celsius and the recently acquired #4 U.S. brand Alani Nu) generating roughly $2 billion in 2024 pro forma revenue and an approximately 16-16.5% combined U.S. category share. Management emphasized that the category is being reshaped by new consumers — particularly females — entering for the first time and by blurring "need-state" lines, with energy drinks increasingly consumed throughout the day and with meals. Positive drivers cited included the portfolio's pricing/promotional optionality, ~$50 million of identified Alani Nu synergies layering on a planned two-year integration to lift Alani Nu's gross margin toward Celsius's low-50s profile, Celsius's strong index in markets with a level playing field (Amazon, South Florida, New York City, L.A. near or above 20% share), a robust international runway (Celsius is now a ~$100 million international business growing off a small base, with ~88% ACV in France and a mid-teens share in Sweden), and adjacency optionality in powders, hydration and Alani Nu's ~$50 million RTD protein business. Acknowledged headwinds were candid: Celsius has lagged the growing U.S. category so far in 2025, with U.S. share down from a ~12.3% 2024 peak to roughly 10.8-11% currently — management said it is "not pleased with that" — driven by 2024's front-loaded innovation being cycled and by unusually heavy Red Bull and Monster sugar-free innovation activity that Celsius's lighter 2025 promotion/innovation calendar did not match. Convenience-channel under-indexing was flagged as the single biggest U.S. share opportunity. The strategic outlook was confident and execution-focused: management pointed to a more balanced 2025 innovation cadence, the first-ever Celsius limited-time offering in the back half, a new holistic "Live Fit" marketing campaign, Alani Nu's largest-ever innovation year, debt paydown, continued global expansion and adjacency development. Overall tone was upbeat, candid about the U.S. share shortfall, and oriented toward a return to share growth.

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - **U.S. category lag and the path back to growth (Q7):** Stephen Powers pressed on why Celsius has lagged the growing U.S. category year-to-date and what the enablers of a second-half return to growth are.
  - **Combined financial profile and synergy timing (Q5, Q6):** The moderator sought a level-set on the pro forma financial profile and probed why the $50 million synergy harvest is concentrated in early 2026.
  - **Distribution architecture — dual vs. single system (Q12, Q13, Q14):** Powers explored the merits of keeping Celsius on PepsiCo and Alani Nu on ABI versus consolidating Alani Nu into Pepsi, and whether two systems can be made to cooperate.
  - **SKU assortment and innovation complexity (Q15, Q16):** The moderator questioned the right number of SKUs given heavy flavor innovation and the complexity it creates for retailers, distributors and consumers.
  - **Adjacent categories and capital allocation (Q18, Q20):** Powers asked how powders, protein, snacks and hydration fit alongside the core can, and about free cash flow, debt paydown and appetite for further M&A.

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - **U.S. share decline candidly acknowledged:** Management openly conceded Celsius slipped from a ~12.3% 2024 peak to ~11% and that it is "not pleased," attributing the gap to front-loaded 2024 innovation being cycled and a lighter 2025 promotion calendar versus heavy Red Bull/Monster sugar-free activity — an unusually direct admission of competitive share loss (Q7, Q20).
  - **Distribution uncertainty (Pepsi vs. ABI for Alani Nu):** A central investor question — whether Alani Nu moves from ABI into the PepsiCo system — was left explicitly unresolved; Fieldly said "there's been no decisions on the path forward" and referenced that PepsiCo's CEO had noted discussions are taking place, leaving the route-to-market architecture an open overhang (Q12).
  - **Synergies decoupled from distribution decision:** Powers pressed whether the $50 million synergy figure depends on the distribution outcome; Langhans stated the company is "comfortable with the $50 million either way, whether it's a dual distribution system or a single distribution system" — but the question highlighted investor uncertainty over whether a dual system can match a single system's portfolio support (Q13, Q14).
  - **Synergy harvest concentrated in 2026:** Powers questioned why synergy realization is limited to a couple of quarters in early 2026; management explained the 12-month TSA and methodical headcount transition push most capture into 2026, a slower ramp than the headline figure implies (Q6).
  - **SKU proliferation vs. innovation:** The moderator raised the tension between heavy flavor innovation and operational/shopper complexity; management acknowledged SKU consistency is "a big opportunity" and that supply chain "want less" SKUs, while committing to continued new-flavor introductions (Q15, Q16).

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **First-ever Celsius limited-time offering (back half 2025):** Management said Celsius will run its first LTO in the fall, designed to attract new consumers and disrupt the path to purchase — a near-term test of the Alani Nu-style LTO playbook applied to Celsius and a potential share-growth catalyst.
  - **New "Live Fit" / "Fit Go" marketing campaign (summer 2025):** Management described the first holistic company-history marketing campaign, built on data analytics with top agencies, to drive awareness, trial, purchase consideration and loyalty — a key enabler of the targeted U.S. share recovery.
  - **Synergy harvest ramp (early 2026):** The bulk of the $50 million synergy capture occurs in 2026 as the 12-month Congo Brands TSA rolls off and headcount/supply-chain integration completes — a margin catalyst.
  - **Alani Nu's largest-ever innovation year (2025, including Witch's Brew for Halloween and a winter edition):** A heavy Alani Nu innovation calendar provides scanner-data and revenue catalysts through the back half.
  - **Distribution decision on Alani Nu (timing undefined):** A future decision on whether Alani Nu consolidates into the PepsiCo system would remove an investor overhang and could reshape route-to-market.
  - **Big Beverage second production line (15-18 months to install):** A second line would bring ~20% of combined Alani Nu/Celsius production in-house over time, a forward margin/leverage catalyst.
  - **Hydration and RTD protein adjacency build-out (multi-year):** Celsius's hydration powder launch (now in Walmart) and Alani Nu's ~$50 million RTD protein business are adjacency opportunities under active evaluation.

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** Management said Celsius front-loaded a lot of its innovation in early 2024, which it is now cycling in 2025; Fieldly noted Celsius peaked at a ~12.3% U.S. category share in 2024 versus ~10.8-11% currently. He also flagged that 2024 saw the heaviest Red Bull and Monster innovation activity (especially in sugar-free) ever seen in the category, against which Celsius's lighter 2025 calendar compared unfavorably.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** None specifically quantified as "easy" comps in this transcript.
  - **Timing Shifts:** Management described Alani Nu's LTO-driven revenue as inherently lumpy — a launch is followed by ~60 days of pickup then a trail-off; a pipe-fill spike is recorded in financial results, followed by a couple-week delay before it hits scanner data, so cross-month timing can create reported-vs-scanner gaps. Celsius has shifted from front-loaded innovation in 2024 to a more balanced cadence in 2025, with its first LTO in the fall.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** Celsius distributes via PepsiCo in the U.S./Canada and Suntory overseas; Alani Nu distributes via ABI. Subway distribution is rolling to up to 20,000 locations; Celsius is also in Jersey Mike's, with foodservice/convenience meal-pairing deals (Casey's, New York City). The combined Celsius + Alani Nu portfolio represents almost 25% share in U.S. MULO (large format); Alani Nu is barely present in convenience, where Celsius is also under-indexed — the largest U.S. share opportunity. Big Beverage will add a second production line over 15-18 months, eventually bringing ~20% of combined production in-house.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics (continued):** Management is shifting from regional "lead launches" with specific retailers toward more national launches, and rebalancing pack sizes — upsizing 12-packs and reducing 4-packs — as consumption shifts toward large-format pantry purchasing.

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** No specific FX figures were disclosed. Management emphasized the category is highly promotionally driven and that the two-brand portfolio enables high-low pricing/promotional strategies.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** The Alani Nu acquisition closed April 1, 2025; combined 2024 pro forma revenue was ~$2 billion. Celsius runs low-50s gross margin and low-30s SG&A; Alani Nu is expected to converge to that profile over a two-year integration, with $50 million of synergies on top. Big Beverage (acquired in 2024, mostly Celsius product) is being expanded with a second line. The combined business is more than 90% RTD energy; Alani Nu adds powders, pre-workout, a ~$50 million RTD protein business and smaller snack/supplement lines.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** Management referenced the prior week's modeling call for detailed purchase-accounting items; on this call the CFO noted ~$450 million remains on the balance sheet after paying ~$450 million toward the acquisition, and that the company will pay down some (not necessarily all) acquisition debt to build credit. A 12-month transition services agreement with Congo Brands governs the integration.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Management cited a category "renaissance" with over 10% category growth recently and high-single-digit growth expected over the next several years. The GLP-1 trend was cited as a broadening tailwind for better-for-you energy and protein. Alani Nu's revenue exhibits LTO-driven seasonality (e.g., Halloween Witch's Brew, winter editions).
