## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** At the 2021 Annual Shareholder Meeting, CEO John Fieldly recapped a record first half of 2021 — total revenue up over 98% to $115 million, North America up over 130% to $92.6 million, and international up 25% to $22.5 million — with gross profit of $48.8 million (up 88%), net income of $4.5 million, and adjusted EBITDA of $12.9 million (up 139%). Management framed Celsius as an "ENERGY 2.0" brand disrupting the energy category by leveraging three tailwinds (better-for-you ingredients, functional benefits, and the growth of the fitness channel), and cited Nielsen data showing Celsius outpacing the category by over 10% with a 1.6% share of the ~$20 billion US energy drink market (up from a 0.2 share in 2018). The principal headwind acknowledged was COVID-driven supply chain constraints on cans and raw materials that pressured margins, alongside a strategic inventory build (inventory days at ~156-160 versus a normalized ~90-100). Fieldly emphasized the national DSD network build-out (189 distributors, ~95% population coverage, ~87% of US counties, with approximately full US coverage expected by year-end) and continued international expansion in Europe and Asia. The CFO highlighted a 50%+ multi-year revenue CAGR (~80% over the last four-to-six quarters), a strong debt-free balance sheet bolstered by the June capital raise (~$67-68 million), and a GAAP gross margin of 43.4% (~51-52% ex-freight). The two proposals — election of eight directors and ratification of Ernst & Young as auditor — were both approved. The tone was confident and forward-looking.

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:** No analysts participated; this was a shareholder meeting. The CEO opened the floor for questions from the in-person audience and online chat, but no questions were submitted ("There's no questions, we do appreciate everyone joining.").

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:** None. There was no Q&A engagement and no points of friction; the meeting proceeded directly to the voting results.

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **Approximately full US DSD coverage by year-end 2021:** Management expects to reach effectively complete US coverage by the end of 2021, enabling closure of critical regional accounts and improved store execution and velocity.
  - **Amazon UK and Germany launch (end of Q3 2021):** A first European e-commerce step that leverages Amazon as the online provider across Europe.
  - **Margin recovery as can supply normalizes:** Management indicated a path to improving gross profit as COVID-related can/raw-material constraints ease.
  - **Inventory normalization toward ~90-100 days:** A reversion from the elevated ~156-160 inventory days would release working capital.
  - **Asian market expansion:** Celsius is "solidifying and seating" markets across Hong Kong, Malaysia, South Korea, and Australia, with more on the target list.

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** None explicitly flagged.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Management noted the COVID-driven "dip" in retail sales in 2020 created an easy comparison base; the fitness and vending channels (most affected by COVID) rebounded, contributing over $4.5 million of incremental revenue in Q2 versus the prior year.
  - **Timing Shifts:** None explicitly flagged.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** US store count surpassed 100,000 (over 20,000 added in 2021); the DSD network reached 189 distributors covering ~95% of the population and ~87% of US counties; Target and Walmart converted over 95% to DSD; the Murphy Oil win added 15,000 stores with six facings.

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** Not specifically quantified in this meeting; Europe grew 22-25% in the quarter.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** Celsius is building adjacent categories — the Celsius BCAA recovery line, Celsius HEAT performance line, and the FAST protein-snack portfolio. International revenue is now ~15% of total revenue.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** A June 2021 capital raise generated ~$67-68 million of cash; the CFO noted "a couple of charges" booked in Q2 to ensure adequate reserves. Ernst & Young was ratified as the FY2021 auditor.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** COVID-19 supply chain disruption (can and raw-material constraints) was the dominant structural factor cited, driving the elevated inventory build.
