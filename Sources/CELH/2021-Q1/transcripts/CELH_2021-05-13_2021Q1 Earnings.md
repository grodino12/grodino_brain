## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** Celsius reported a record first quarter, with revenue exceeding $50 million, up 78% year-over-year from $28.2 million, driven by triple-digit North America growth (101%) and 22% international growth led by Nordic operations. Management framed the quarter as a demonstration of accelerating brand momentum, citing third-party scan data showing Celsius up 218% (two-week Nielsen) and a 1.2% category share, alongside a build-out of its national DSD network (now 180+ regional partners covering ~85% of major metro markets) and a US door count exceeding 92,000. The principal headwinds acknowledged were an industry-wide aluminum can shortage and a Texas freeze that shut two co-packers and a warehouse for over 2.5 weeks, leaving the company shipping at roughly an 80% fill rate; rising freight, raw material, and repackaging costs compressed gross margin to 41.1% from 46.1% a year earlier. Management reaffirmed confidence that gross margin would run in the low-40% range through 2021 and indicated production capacity is being expanded to meet pent-up demand expected to be served in the back half of Q2 and beyond. The overall tone was confident and growth-focused, emphasizing that demand (sell-out) materially outpaced the company's constrained ability to ship (sell-in).

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - Analysts pressed on the gap between sell-in and sell-through, and when the company expects to return to a 100% fill rate given the can shortage (Question 1, Question 2).
  - The aluminum can shortage drew repeated attention — when it would be alleviated and what mix of imported vs. domestic cans implies for margins (Question 1, Question 6).
  - Europe and the FAST protein-snack portfolio were a focus, including production constraints and the planned US Amazon launch of FAST (Question 3, Question 5).
  - The DSD network strategy and the ceiling on penetration of the customer base were probed (Question 6).
  - International expansion plans (UK, Germany, Middle East) and the associated marketing investment intensity were questioned (Question 6, Question 7).

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - Whether supply constraints would force Celsius to delay capturing new shelf-reset distribution wins into the April 2022 reset cycle — management pushed back, stating it has enough product to fulfill new-distribution demand (Question 2).
  - The durability and timing of the can shortage resolution — management deferred to back-half 2021 and "into 2022 and beyond," acknowledging uncertainty tied to whether consumers return to on-premise (Question 6).
  - The amended FY2019 10-K filed the same morning regarding internal-control disclosure; management proactively characterized it as "inconsequential" with "no impact to the reported figures," and an analyst explicitly thanked them for putting the question "to rest" (Prepared Remarks; Question 6).

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **Back-half 2021 fill-rate normalization:** Management expects to reach a position to fulfill all orders in the back half of Q2, which would unlock pent-up demand currently capped near an 80% fill rate.
  - **Spring 2021 planogram resets:** New distribution wins (e.g., Murphy USA's 1,500 locations) and additional national-chain resets in the April/May window are a near-term door-count and revenue catalyst.
  - **FAST brand US launch on Amazon (Q2 2021):** A two-SKU exclusive Amazon launch tests Celsius's entry into the protein-snacking category.
  - **US can-manufacturer capacity additions (back-half 2021 into 2022/2023):** New domestic capacity would shift can mix toward lower-cost domestic cans, supporting gross margin recovery.

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** None explicitly flagged as a tough comp; management characterized Q1 2021 as a record quarter.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** None explicitly flagged.
  - **Timing Shifts:** Management indicated demand was being "pulled" toward the back half of Q2 and back half of 2021 due to Q1 supply constraints — "pull demand towards the back half of Q2 and into Q -- the back half of 2021" (Question 1).
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** US door count exceeded 92,000 (later cited as 192,000 locations in Q&A), up ~10,000 since the start of 2021; Murphy USA national launch added 1,500 locations from late March; transitions to DSD ongoing across Target, CVS, Walmart, Racetrac, 7-Eleven; FAST protein-snack supply constraints in Europe impacted the Nordic results.

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** A stronger euro accounted for approximately 8.4% of the increase in European revenue versus the prior-year quarter; favorable currency added ~$700,000 to gross profit dollars. Revenue growth was driven by volume, not pricing.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** The October 2019 acquisition of Func Food (Nordic wellness business) continued to be integrated; can mix (wrapped vs. imported vs. domestic) is a margin variable — wrapped cans carry a higher cost, so a shift toward imported cans represents a "slight margin improvement going forward."
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** An amended FY2019 10-K was filed the same morning relating to internal-control disclosure for the European acquisition; management stated there is "no impact to the reported figures."
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** The Texas freeze shut two co-packers and one warehouse for over 2.5 weeks during Q1; a US gasoline/freight shortage was an active headwind during the week of the call ("truckers just don't have gas, especially in the Northeast").

- **Top Priority Follow-Up Questions:**

#### Topic: Fill-Rate Recovery Timing

**Building Blocks:** Reference management's "back half of Q2" language (Context Reinstatement) + bracket the endpoint (Bracketing) + bound the timeframe (Time-Bounding)
**Q (Good):** "You expect to be in a position to fulfill all orders in the back half of Q2 — does 'fulfill all orders' mean a 100% fill rate by June 30, or a fill rate in the low-90s exiting the quarter?"

- **Rationale:** Management said "able to fulfill all orders" in the back half of Q2 but separately quoted an ~80% Q1 fill rate without quantifying the exit-rate target.
- **Question Type:** Context Reinstatement + Bracketing
- **Question Topic(s):** Supply chain, fill rate, capacity
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 1
- **Question Importance Rank:** 1

#### Topic: Gross Margin Trajectory

**Building Blocks:** Use the company's exact "low-40%" framing (Mirroring Language) + request component breakdown (Component Breakdown)
**Q (Good):** "Of the roughly 500 basis points of gross margin decline versus the prior-year quarter, how much is attributable to wrapped-can premium versus freight versus rework, and which component reverses first?"

- **Rationale:** Management cited freight, repackaging, raw material, and processing costs plus wrapped-can mix but never split the 41.1% vs. 46.1% bridge.
- **Question Type:** Component Breakdown
- **Question Topic(s):** Gross margin, cost structure, can mix
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 1
- **Question Importance Rank:** 2

#### Topic: DSD Penetration Ceiling

**Building Blocks:** Ask for the end state first (Reverse Chronology) + establish the current baseline (Baseline Establishment)
**Q (Good):** "What percentage of North America revenue do you expect to be serviced through DSD at a steady state, and where does that figure stand today?"

- **Rationale:** Management explicitly declined to give a DSD penetration percentage ("We haven't provided percentages of where that is") despite calling it a core strategic pillar.
- **Question Type:** Reverse Chronology + Baseline Establishment
- **Question Topic(s):** Distribution strategy, DSD network
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 2
- **Question Importance Rank:** 3

#### Topic: International Expansion Investment

**Building Blocks:** Mirror the "positive ROI driven model" phrase (Rapport Maintenance) + bracket the spend (Bracketing)
**Q (Good):** "For the UK and Germany entries you are exploring, is the incremental marketing and operating investment in 2021 closer to immaterial — under $1 million — or could it scale toward several million as distributor agreements are signed?"

- **Rationale:** Management said investment would be "generally immaterial" but gave no quantitative anchor and provided no timeline.
- **Question Type:** Bracketing + Time-Bounding
- **Question Topic(s):** International expansion, marketing investment
- **Topic Importance Rank:** 3
- **Question Importance Rank:** 4
