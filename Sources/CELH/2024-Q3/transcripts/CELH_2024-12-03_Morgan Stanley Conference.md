## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** Celsius Chief of Staff Toby David used the Morgan Stanley conference to reframe a difficult 2024 — a year in which Celsius growth slowed far more than expected — as a cyclical, macro-driven trough rather than a structural maturation of the brand or category. His core message was that energy remains the best category in beverage and consumer goods, that consumers are not abandoning caffeine but shifting toward sugar-free (now over 50% of the category for the first time) and functional benefits — boxes Celsius "checks all" — and that 2024's setup positions Celsius well for a 2025 recovery. Positive drivers cited included substantial 2024 shelf-space gains (about 45% uptick in convenience) ready to be capitalized on once the category turns, a six-SKU 2025 innovation slate (two each in Essentials, Vibe, and Core lines) plus flavors coming off exclusivity (Cherry Cola, Watermelon Lemonade, Kiwi Strawberry), strong drill-deep market shares (mid-20s in South Florida, near 20 in New York and Boston, 15+ in Los Angeles and Midwest markets), an Amazon position toggling for #1 at 20+ share, and strong international starts (Canada hit a 5 share in six weeks; Australia and the UK off to good starts). The acknowledged headwinds were the macro-driven slowdown in new-to-category consumers (Celsius's historical ~45% new-to-category rate makes it more exposed than peers), softer velocity, the Q3 Pepsi inventory drawdown (with up to ~$15 million more Q4 headwind), an aggressive category promotional environment, and a margin-dilutive Pepsi incentive agreement. The strategic outlook was confident and recovery-oriented: management reiterated a "more people, more placements, more often" framework, sees the category returning to "normalcy" in 2025, and pointed to "breadcrumbs" (scanner pickup over six to eight weeks, retailer NACS optimism, falling gas prices) suggesting recovery is "coming sooner rather than later." Tone was measured, candid about the year's difficulty, but firmly optimistic on 2025.

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - **Cyclical vs. structural slowdown drivers (Q1):** Eric Serotta opened by asking management to disaggregate the 2024 slowdown into cyclical (macro/category) and structural (larger base, increased competition) factors.
  - **Competition — sugar-free pushes and Alani (Q2, Q3):** Serotta probed how Celsius defends its sugar-free/lifestyle position against Red Bull and Monster sugar-free pushes, and the competitive overlap with a momentum-gaining Alani.
  - **Velocity recovery (Q5, Q6):** Analysts asked what drives improved Celsius velocity from here and how management can stimulate existing-user consumption frequency.
  - **Pepsi inventory destocking and Q4 tracking (Q7):** Serotta asked for the drivers of the 2024 destocking and whether the Q4 outcome was tracking versus the ~$15 million-headwind framework.
  - **Margin path and pricing (Q11, Q12, Q13):** Analysts explored 2025 gross margin puts and takes, the building blocks to Monster-like margins, what pricing Celsius has taken, and the category's aggressive promotional environment.
  - **International progress and 2025 innovation (Q14, Q15):** Serotta asked for updates on Canada, the UK, and Australia, and how the 2025 US innovation pipeline compares to prior years.

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - **Cyclical vs. structural debate:** A central tension was whether the slowdown reflects a maturing brand/category or a temporary macro trough; management argued firmly it is cyclical — "This doesn't feel like a maturation of Celsius or of the energy category" — pointing to peers also struggling with new-to-category as evidence (Q1, Q9).
  - **New-to-category exposure:** Serotta pressed whether Celsius is "just bringing in fewer users because more people have tried it"; management conceded the macro hit new-to-category harder for Celsius (~45% rate, above Red Bull and Monster) but framed this as cyclical exposure, not exhaustion (Q9).
  - **Pricing without benefit:** Management confirmed it took pricing in H2 2024 but "dealt the back" through promotions because "the consumer wasn't ready," leaving the 2025 capture amount explicitly undisclosed — "the amount that we end up fully capturing, we haven't yet spoken to" (Q13).
  - **Refusal to give 2025 guidance:** Asked about 2025 gross margin puts and takes, David deferred — "We haven't given any kind of direction yet for 2025, so I'll leave that to John or Jarrod" (Q11).
  - **Space gains coinciding with the slowdown:** Serotta noted the large 2024 shelf-space gain "unfortunately coincided with the category slowing"; management framed the captured-but-uncapitalized space as stored upside for when the category turns (Q4).

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **Category return to "normalcy" in 2025:** Management sees the energy category primed to recover in 2025 as consumer confidence rebounds — the central swing factor for Celsius's reacceleration.
  - **2025 shelf resets:** Positive NACS retailer conversations suggest further space gains in 2025; capitalizing on already-captured 2024 space is a velocity catalyst once the category turns.
  - **2025 innovation slate:** Six new SKUs (two each in Essentials, Vibe, Core) plus flavors coming off exclusivity (Cherry Cola, Watermelon Lemonade, Kiwi Strawberry) are positioned to drive consumption frequency and, as the category turns, new-to-category trial.
  - **Big Beverage co-packer (Southeast production):** The North Carolina co-packer acquisition is expected to be margin-accretive, producing for a large part of the Southeast US business.
  - **Monster's November 1 price increase data (early 2025):** By January, one-and-a-half to two months of data will show whether Monster captured its 5% increase — a read on category pricing and a potential Celsius pricing opportunity.
  - **Falling gas prices / convenience traffic:** Declining gas prices could restore convenience foot traffic, driving single-serve trial and energy-category consumption.
  - **International ramp (Canada, UK, Australia):** Continued momentum across recently launched markets, aided by global sugar-free/functional trends and sugar taxes, is a multi-year growth catalyst.

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** Management flagged that the Pepsi incentive agreement pressures margin "especially compared to Q4 last year, where it wasn't into place yet" — a structural margin headwind on the Q4 2024 / Q4 2023 comparison. The large 2024 shelf-space gains (about 45% convenience uptick) followed 2023 gains, so distribution growth is lapping an already-elevated base.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Management implied 2025 should benefit from easier comparisons once the Pepsi inventory situation normalizes and the macro/new-to-category headwind anniversaries — David repeatedly invoked a "return to normalcy" and a "normalized cadence" for 2025 sales/marketing and gross profit.
  - **Timing Shifts:** The defining timing distortion remained the Pepsi inventory destocking — small pullbacks in Q1/Q2 2024 followed by a large Q3 drawdown; David said the Q4 2024 framework of up to ~$15 million of headwind is unchanged six weeks on. Velocity historically dipped then recovered as Celsius grew into added distribution points, but in 2024 the expected recovery coincided with the category slowdown.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** Celsius saw about a 45% convenience distribution uptick in 2024. Convenience is ~55% of Celsius sales versus ~65% for Red Bull/Monster; closing the convenience share gap (toward the company's strong 7-Eleven position) is the stated opportunity. Celsius carries ~20 SKUs on shelf at 98% ACV versus ~6 SKUs at ~65% ACV for Ghost/C4. The category is heavily promotional (Monster BOGOs, Bang $1/can, Rain/Rainstorm/Ghost/C4 BOGOs); Celsius promotes with greater frequency but less depth and does not run BOGOs.
  - **Distribution Detail:** Drill-deep markets number roughly 20 (expanded from 5 in 2013), with 12-plus markets above a 15 share. Non-tracked foodservice (Jersey Mike's, hospitals, hotels, vending, colleges) is a differentiation lever via the Pepsi system.

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** No FX figures quantified. On pricing, Celsius took pricing in H2 2024 but "dealt the back" via promotions/shopper marketing because the consumer was not ready; 2025 offers flexibility to capture more, with the full-capture amount undisclosed. Monster took a 5% list-price increase November 1; Red Bull is described as having captured most of its 2024 pricing.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** Celsius's 2025 slate is six SKUs (two each Essentials/Vibe/Core); the 2024 slate also had six SKUs (including the Essentials launch) plus three Vibes. Big Beverage, a North Carolina co-packer, was acquired and is expected to be margin-accretive for the Southeast. Management raised the possibility of a future West Coast production facility ("down the road, not now").
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** Management referenced Q3 2024's "exaggerated quarterly volatility" from the Pepsi inventory move and "related promotional accounting." Sales and marketing runs roughly 20%–23% of revenue (Monster lower); management said Celsius is "not ready to lever the company yet" and would refine promotional, S&M, and G&A lines when it is.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Management attributed the 2024 slowdown to a macroeconomic downturn affecting all consumer goods over the prior six to seven months, with reduced convenience foot traffic; "breadcrumbs" of recovery cited include scanner pickup over the prior six to eight weeks, retailer optimism at NACS, and falling gas prices. Global tailwinds cited include sugar-free/functional trends and government sugar taxes abroad.
