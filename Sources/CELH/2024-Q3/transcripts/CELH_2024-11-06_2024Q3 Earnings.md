## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** Celsius reported a Q3 2024 that management itself described as "anything but normal" — total revenue fell 31% year-over-year to approximately $266 million, driven primarily by roughly $124 million of inventory optimization at its largest distributor (PepsiCo), even as Celsius retail sell-through grew 7.1% and the company contributed more than 16% of all energy-category growth. Management framed the quarter as a distortion between a sharply reduced sell-in and a continued (if slower) sell-through, with the headline GAAP results — adjusted EBITDA down 96% to ~$4.4 million and net income down 92% to ~$6.4 million — well below the prior-year quarter. Positive drivers cited included resilient share (11.6% MULO+ with convenience, up 10 basis points year-over-year), foodservice strength (~12.3% of North America PepsiCo sales; lodging and restaurant points of distribution up 46% and 27%), Amazon sales up 21% to $27 million (20.4% Amazon share), the Big Beverage co-packer acquisition as a capital-efficient vertical-integration step, a new Ireland center of excellence, and the October launch into Australia and New Zealand. The principal acknowledged headwinds were the Pepsi inventory optimization, a margin-dilutive new Pepsi incentive program that fully ramped in Q3, reduced unit velocity, softer macroeconomic conditions, and reduced convenience-store foot traffic. The strategic outlook was confident but explicitly non-committal: management reiterated three growth drivers (new consumers, expanded availability, increased consumption frequency), guided Q4 inventory impact to a range of a slight benefit up to ~$15 million of pressure, and declined to provide formal forward guidance. Overall tone was reassuring and reset-focused while transparently acknowledging a difficult, distorted quarter.

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - **Pepsi inventory completion and Q4 impact (Q2, Q4):** Multiple analysts pressed whether the distributor inventory optimization was finished, where inventories now stand, and the magnitude of any residual Q4 impact.
  - **Q4 sales cadence and modeling approach (Q1, Q4, Q5):** Analysts asked whether scanner trends would improve sequentially and how to model Q4 North America growth (scanner data minus an inventory assumption).
  - **Market share erosion and consumer behavior (Q3, Q6):** Analysts probed where the consumer that drove the May share peak had gone and what is genuinely different in Celsius's plans versus peers doing similar promotions/innovation.
  - **Formal guidance (Q7):** Kevin Grundy directly challenged management on introducing formal near- and long-term guidance, citing a strong marketplace desire for visibility and credibility.
  - **International expansion pace and capital deployment (Q7, Q8):** Analysts asked whether Celsius should expand internationally more aggressively to offset US deceleration, and how it will deploy its large cash balance after the Big Beverage acquisition.
  - **2025 playbook, competitive dynamics, and pricing (Q9):** Eric Serotta asked how the execution playbook evolves against Red Bull's and Monster's sugar-free pushes, and for the timeline/magnitude of any Celsius price increase.

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - **Credibility after a far-worse-than-expected quarter:** Peter Grom explicitly asked how to avoid "a similar negative update" to the September miss; management leaned on "tighter correlation" between sell-in and depletions and "better alignment" to argue Q4 visibility is improved (Q4, Q5).
  - **Refusal to provide formal guidance:** Kevin Grundy pointedly argued that nearly all of staples (Monster being an outlier) issues guidance and the marketplace wants credibility; management firmly declined — "at this time, the company is not providing forward guidance" (Q7).
  - **Wide and ambiguous Q4 range:** The CFO's Q4 inventory range — "a bit of positive benefit... all the way up to around $15 million of pressure" — drew a clarifying follow-up from Kaumil Gajrawala and a request from Mark Astrachan to benchmark it against scanner trends, exposing the imprecision of the outlook (Q2, Q3).
  - **Whether to expand internationally more aggressively:** Grundy framed faster international expansion as a "potential value trigger" to offset US deceleration; management agreed on the opportunity but reiterated a measured, "temper expectations" approach and declined to set expectations (Q8).
  - **Share loss attribution:** Management attributed the ~1–1.5-point share decline from the May peak to convenience foot-traffic weakness and shoppers taking "one less trip," and to competitor innovation expanding trial — a multi-factor explanation that did not isolate Celsius-specific causes (Q3, Q6).

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **Pepsi sell-in / sell-through realignment (by end of Q4 2024):** The CFO said sell-in and sell-through should realign before Q4 ends — removing the reported-revenue distortion that defined Q3.
  - **New Pepsi incentive program (2025):** Featuring priority periods and aligned resources, fully ramped in Q3 and carrying into 2025 with additional focus — positioned as a tailwind to availability and share, though margin-dilutive.
  - **Jake Paul vs. Mike Tyson title sponsorship (mid-November 2024):** A Netflix event management called potentially the largest live boxing event in history — a major brand-awareness and household-penetration catalyst.
  - **2025 shelf resets:** Management reported positive retailer feedback at NACS and expects to gain shelf space, better placements, and secondary cold placements in the 2025 reset cycle.
  - **2025 innovation slate:** New Vibe and Core line innovation previewed at NACS, plus new RTDs (Sparkling Watermelon, Lemonade, Cherry Cola going nationwide) and Essentials flavors (Grape Slush, Watermelon Ice).
  - **Big Beverage vertical integration:** The co-packer acquisition is expected to deliver innovation/LTO flexibility and Orbit-model leverage with financial benefits in the near and long term, with further leverage gains anticipated in 2025.
  - **Sugar-free category crossing 50%:** Management noted the category crossed 50% sugar-free for the first time in the quarter — a structural tailwind for Celsius's sugar-free portfolio as the category recovers.

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** The CFO flagged that Q3 club-channel comparisons were difficult — Sam's Club and BJ's sales were "negatively affected due to timing of promotions and innovation load-in in the year-ago period," contributing to total club sales falling 4% to $60.5 million from $63.2 million despite Costco sales rising 15%.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Not explicitly framed as easy comps. Management noted year-to-date results (revenue +5%, gross profit +10%) were "more indicative of our full-year operations" than the distorted Q3.
  - **Timing Shifts:** The defining timing distortion was the ~$124 million of Pepsi inventory optimization that reduced Q3 sell-in well below sell-through; the CFO said sell-in and sell-through should realign before Q4 ends. The Q4 inventory impact is guided to a range from slight benefit up to ~$15 million of pressure. Promotional allowances from increased retail sales also created revenue headwinds from the sell-in / sell-through imbalance.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** Foodservice was ~12.3% of North America PepsiCo sales, with lodging and restaurant points of distribution up 46% and 27% year-over-year. The new Pepsi incentive program (priority periods, aligned resources) fully ramped in Q3 and is margin-dilutive but positioned as a future availability tailwind. Management expects energy to keep gaining shelf space in 2025 resets. Competitive note: KDP's acquisition of Ghost was flagged as a potential source of distribution-network transitions and opportunity.
  - **Distribution Detail:** Celsius MULO+ with convenience share was 11.6% in the four weeks ending October 6 (up 10 bps YoY); Amazon share was 20.4% (Stackline 14-week read ending October 5).

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** No FX figures quantified; international revenue grew 37% to $18.6 million. On pricing, management confirmed it had rolled out a price increase but said it does "not expect significant benefit into 2025," has not disclosed the amount, and is "being conservative" — declining to confirm any response to Monster's November 1 list-price increase.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** Celsius acquired Big Beverage, a long-term Celsius co-packer, the prior week — framed as capital-efficient vertical integration for innovation/LTO capability and Orbit-model leverage; the CFO stressed Celsius does "not have a desire to become a co-packer" and remains "asset-light." A new Ireland center of excellence was established for innovation, global procurement, supply chain, and marketing. New products include Vibe on-the-go powders, RTDs going nationwide, and new Essentials flavors. Two new board members (Hans Melotte, Israel Kontorovsky) were added; Israel replaced Jim Lee.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** Results presented on both GAAP and non-GAAP bases (adjusted EBITDA / adjusted EBITDA margin). Sales and marketing expense was 37.6% of revenue in Q3 (26.1% year-to-date) — elevated as a percentage because the ~$124 million inventory optimization reduced the revenue denominator while spend itself was in line with forecast. G&A rose 11% to $25.5 million (10% of Q3 sales vs. 6% prior year; 7% year-to-date, in line with expectations).
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Management cited "softer macroeconomic conditions," reduced convenience-store foot traffic (major retailers reported reduced convenience traffic), and shoppers taking "one less trip" per Circana data. Management noted the category grew only 2% in Q3 versus Celsius retail sales up 7.1%, and that the category showed "additional growth... over the last several weeks from the lows" of Q3.
