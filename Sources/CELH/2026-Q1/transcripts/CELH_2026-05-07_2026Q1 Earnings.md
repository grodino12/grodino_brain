## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** Celsius Holdings delivered a record first quarter with revenue of $783 million, framed by management as proof that the multi-brand portfolio strategy (Celsius, Alani Nu, Rockstar) laid out coming into the year is working; the company's combined portfolio now represents roughly one-fifth of the US energy drink market in tracked channels, with portfolio dollar share reaching 20.9% in the four weeks ending April 12. Positive drivers cited included Alani Nu's pro forma growth of approximately 60% year-over-year (approximately 85% on a clean scanner basis), completion of the Alani Nu integration with the full approximately $50 million of targeted synergies captured, strong innovation traction (Lime Slush LTO became Alani Nu's top-selling tracked-channel flavor; Celsius Fizz-Free platform expanding), and meaningful operating leverage that lifted adjusted EBITDA margin approximately 370 basis points to 24.9% and more than doubled GAAP net income to $110 million. Acknowledged headwinds were Celsius brand growth of only approximately 6% (described by an analyst as "moderating"), an in-progress Rockstar integration explicitly framed as a 2026 "stabilization year," and discrete Q1 cost pressures — severe Northeast winter weather creating incremental freight, Rockstar inventory rebalancing freight, and rising Midwest aluminum premium and LME prices. The strategic outlook is constructive but measured: management reiterated a path back to "low-50s" gross margin (Q1 was approximately 48.3%) while cautioning that sustained elevated commodity costs could affect the timing and sequencing, and characterized Q2 margin as a "side step" with a "stair step" up in Q3/Q4. Overall tone was confident, disciplined, and execution-focused, with repeated emphasis on portfolio breadth, the PepsiCo distribution partnership, and entering the peak summer selling season well positioned.

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - **Celsius brand growth deceleration (Q1):** Analysts pressed management to disaggregate the moderating Celsius brand growth into its drivers — limited Q1 innovation versus prior year, SKU rationalization, and potential cannibalization from Alani Nu — and to frame the second-half trajectory.
  - **Alani Nu revenue bridge and shipment quality (Q2):** Analysts sought to reconcile the gap between approximately 100% scanner growth and approximately 60% reported growth, and to verify whether reported revenue included any one-time shipment benefit or inventory build into the Pepsi system rather than clean underlying demand.
  - **Shelf space realization and food service (Q3):** Analysts wanted specificity on how much of the approximately 17% Celsius space gain is food service versus tracked channels, and how far along the over-100% Alani Nu gas-and-convenience space gains are in actually flowing through tracked data.
  - **Margin path and commodity sensitivity (Q5):** Analysts focused on the gross margin trajectory given rising aluminum costs, asking how a resolution of geopolitical conflict or aluminum downside would affect the low-50s timeline over a 12–18 month horizon.
  - **Comparable-basis Celsius performance and sequential margin (Q6):** Analysts asked whether well-set planogram locations are performing acceptably, whether Alani/Celsius cannibalization is phasing off, and for precise sequential margin shape into Q2 and the second half.

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - **Celsius brand growth quality vs. portfolio framing:** A central point of tension was whether the approximately 6% Celsius brand growth reflects genuine softening; management consistently redirected toward portfolio-level share gains and "optionality," and notably did not quantify the cannibalization, innovation-timing, or SKU-rationalization drags Bonnie Herzog explicitly asked them to "frame or quantify" (Q1, Q6).
  - **Scanner vs. reported revenue credibility for Alani Nu:** Peter Grom probed whether the approximately 60% reported number was inflated by Pepsi-system shipment timing or inventory build; management attributed the bridge to ACV expansion and a roughly $5 million non-cash amortization item, but the multi-layered adjustment (Cherry Bomb timing, DSD mix, Canada/non-US add-backs) left the "clean" growth figure dependent on management's own construction (Q2).
  - **Margin timeline conditionality:** Management repeatedly hedged the return to low-50s gross margin on commodity costs ("if elevated costs do remain... we may see some impact on the timing and sequencing"), creating tension between the reaffirmed "structural margin algorithm" and an explicitly uncertain near-term path (Q5, Q6).
  - **Food service disclosure opacity:** Eric Hanson said it was "difficult to break that out" when asked to size food service within the approximately 17% Celsius space gain, leaving an analyst-requested decomposition unanswered (Q3).

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **Second North Carolina manufacturing line begins producing (2026 back half):** Management said this will start to deliver benefit in the back half with full benefit in 2027 — a tangible vertical-integration step supporting the margin recovery narrative.
  - **Rockstar integration completion (H1 2026):** With 2026 framed as a "stabilization year," completion of the integration and any early evidence of improved core-item velocities would be the first read on whether Rockstar becomes a growth contributor.
  - **Summer innovation slate and activations (Q2–H2 2026):** New Celsius and Alani Nu LTOs (Electric Vibe now live, a summer Celsius LTO planned), plus Formula 1, global soccer tournament, and music/motorsports activations, are positioned as the key test of the innovation pipeline ahead of the peak selling season.
  - **Aluminum / Midwest premium / LME trajectory:** Management flagged commodity costs as the swing factor for the timing of the low-50s margin recovery; a resolution lowering aluminum costs would accelerate the margin "stair step."
  - **Price pack architecture and vertical integration programs (2027–2028):** Management said these will show "initial impacts" in H2 2026 and "a lot more" in 2027–2028 — a multi-year margin catalyst.

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** Management did not flag specific tough revenue comparisons in prepared remarks. Implicitly, the Celsius brand's approximately 6% Q1 growth is being compared against a period with more Q1 innovation; Bonnie Herzog referenced "limited innovation during Q1 versus last year" as a drag, and management noted Q1 2026 was deliberately focused on "prioritizing the assortment" rather than launching innovation.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Not explicitly framed by management. The Rockstar business ($67 million in Q1) is in a "stabilization year," implying current-period results are a depressed base for future comparisons rather than an easy comp now.
  - **Timing Shifts:** Management flagged a meaningful scanner-data timing distortion for Alani Nu: "The cleanest comparison number is 85%. That adjusts for Cherry Bomb, which sold in during Q4 2025 but landed in Q1 2026 scanner data." (CFO Jarrod Langhans) — i.e., a Cherry Bomb sell-in straddled the Q4 2025 / Q1 2026 boundary, inflating reported scanner growth from approximately 85% to approximately 100%.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** Alani Nu's growth is being lifted by the PepsiCo system / DSD transition, with the majority of the distribution transition work completed across December and January 2026. Management guided to space gains heading into summer of approximately 17% for brand Celsius (cooler placements and additional points of sale across national chains), over 100% for Alani Nu across all channels, and maintained net space for Rockstar alongside reconfigured assortments. The Rockstar SKU reconfiguration and reset activity is substantially complete; resets are expected to be largely finalized through May and June.
  - **Top Priority Follow-Up Questions:** See STEP 4 follow-up question block below.

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** No FX guidance was quantified. On historical pricing, the CFO noted Celsius "were taking pricing with brand CELSIUS as we're moving in. That was back in 2022," explaining part of the historical DSD-to-direct mix difference between Celsius and Alani Nu — context for why Celsius and Alani Nu scanner-to-reported bridges differ.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** Alani Nu was acquired April 1, 2025; Q1 2026 is therefore the first quarter where Alani Nu is a full-period contributor, and growth is reported on a pro forma basis (approximately 60% YoY reported). The Alani Nu integration is now complete with approximately $50 million of synergies captured. Q1 2026 is also "the first quarter of the organization managing a portfolio of brands under the category captaincy within the energy category of PepsiCo" (CEO John Fieldly), a structural change unlocking food service and non-reported channels.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** The CFO flagged a roughly $5 million non-cash GAAP item embedded in the Alani Nu revenue bridge: "there is a bit of GAAP impact in terms of roughly $5 million... That's really a non-cash item. That's just the amortization from the balance sheet" — tied to 2022 preferred-share issuance and Pepsi-funded distributor terminations being amortized off the balance sheet. He noted the analogous Celsius brand impact is "about $1 million a quarter."
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Management flagged "severe winter weather in parts of the Northeast" creating incremental freight and freeze-protection costs in February 2026, partially offsetting Q1 gross margin progress; this is characterized as largely behind the company moving through Q2.
