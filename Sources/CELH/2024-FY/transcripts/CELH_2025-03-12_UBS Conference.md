## STEP 4: Executive Summary

- **Management's Core Message:** In a UBS fireside chat, Celsius management addressed head-on the energy-category slowdown and its own market-share erosion. CEO John Fieldly explained that after a strong early-2024 start with record distribution gains, the category went negative for the first time in Q3 2024 amid macroeconomic pressure, slowing convenience-store traffic and shifting consumer habits, and that competitors "woke up" with their largest-ever sugar-free innovation push. Celsius peaked at nearly a 12 share in early Q2 2024 and has since hovered around 11 (excluding coffees/teas). Fieldly candidly admitted "we're not happy where we ended 2024 out and share" and acknowledged the company "could have timed our innovation better." Headwinds management cited include a deliberately slower Q1 2025 start (a "step change" shifting innovation timing from mid-January to mid-April), challenged core-brand velocities in the trailing 4- and 12-week scans, and a tougher first-half comp. Positive drivers emphasized: the structural shift to sugar-free (now the majority of the category), Celsius's #9 LRB rank on $2.7 billion retail sales (+22% in 2024), spring-reset shelf-space gains of 15%-20%, foodservice expansion (Subway up to 18,000 locations), the pending Alani Nu acquisition, and a strengthening Pepsi partnership augmented by the hire of 27-year PepsiCo veteran Eric Hanson. CFO Jarrod Langhans framed 2025 as a building year — softer first half, accelerating second half — with gross margin "circled" at ~50% and S&M likely between the 22%-23% historical level and the elevated 26% of 2024. Management's tone was candid about past missteps but firmly bullish on the recovery thesis.

- **Analyst Focus & Key Questions:**
  - **Category and Celsius slowdown diagnosis** — the host pressed on why the category slowed in 2024 and what Celsius could have done differently (Questions 1, 2).
  - **Market-share trajectory and recovery path** — analysts wanted to understand the path from ~11 share back toward 15+ and whether retailers would pull shelf space given soft velocities (Questions 4, 5).
  - **Top-line cadence and comp normalization** — the host sought a conceptual framework for lapping 2024's inventory destocking and promotional impacts across 2025 (Question 9).
  - **Pepsi partnership health and inventory dynamics** — analysts probed how the relationship is being strengthened after 2024's incentive-structure changes and inventory surprises (Question 8).
  - **Alani Nu positioning, overlap and integration synergies** — questions covered Alani Nu's category positioning, cannibalization risk, and where the biggest synergies lie (Questions 11, 12).

- **Key Areas of Debate or Tension:**
  - **Whether soft velocities threaten shelf space** — the host directly asked if retailers are pulling back shelf space; Fieldly conceded that if Celsius "can't grow share... can't grow revenue," year-end buyer meetings could bring "hard decisions," while insisting the 15%-20% gains were already sold in (Questions 5, 6).
  - **The deliberate slow Q1 start** — management framed the soft 4-/12-week scans as a self-inflicted "step change" in innovation timing, a narrative analysts must take on faith pending second-half acceleration (Questions 2, 9).
  - **Alani Nu cannibalization** — the host raised brand overlap given both brands' health-and-wellness roots; Fieldly countered with Mintel/panel data showing only ~15% of Celsius consumers also buy Alani Nu, lower than the overlap with Red Bull and Monster (Question 12).
  - **Margin direction under tariffs** — Langhans "circled" 50% as a good number with upside and downside, declining to model the recently imposed 25% tariff given how frequently it changes (Question 14).
  - **2024 ending share versus expectations** — Fieldly acknowledged "we all wanted to be a 30 share ending the year," a candid concession that 2024 fell well short of investor and management hopes (Questions 2, 6).

- **Potential Catalysts & Inflection Points:**
  - **Spring shelf resets (15%-20% space gain) and summer beverage season:** Management expects soft first-half scans to give way to second-half acceleration as resets, new customers (Subway, Home Depot, Kroger/Walmart front-checkout coolers) and the summer "healthy Halo" program take hold.
  - **Innovation timing shift to mid-April:** The deliberate move of innovation launches to mid-April (from mid-January) is the explicit lever management says will drive the back-half recovery.
  - **Eric Hanson joining (March 24):** The 27-year PepsiCo veteran's arrival is positioned as a near-term strengthener of the Pepsi partnership and synergy unlock.
  - **Alani Nu acquisition close:** Closing converts the deal into a ~16.5 combined energy share and unlocks pricing/promotional flexibility, supplier and infrastructure synergies; management said synergies are largely "known cost" they can "put a line in" by the second half.
  - **Q3 2024 comp lap:** Langhans flagged a "big pick up" in Q3 2025 against the significant inventory movement that depressed Q3 2024.
  - **Hydration line ramp and powder business:** Hydration is rolling from Amazon to Walmart.com and other retailers; the powder/energy-stick business is "almost $100 million" and growing.

- **Year-over-Year Comparability Factors:**
  - **Tough Comparisons:** Langhans said "the first half of the year is going to be a tougher comp than the back half" — 2024 retail growth of ~22% was stronger in the front half. Comp pressure is concentrated in Q1 2025 due to the innovation-timing shift (launches moved from mid-January to mid-April) and a promotional calendar skewed toward late Q1/Q2.
  - **Easy Comparisons:** Q3 2025 should see "a big pick up" against significant inventory movement that hurt Q3 2024; "sometime between beginning to end of May, there's definitely some comps that will be rolling over that... [are] easier from a reported perspective." Celsius's soft comps "coincide with some of the energy category soft comps, Q3 being the main one."
  - **Timing Shifts:** Innovation launches moved from mid-January to mid-April; promotional calendar skewed toward the back end of Q1 into Q2 — both redistribute reported growth toward the second half of 2025.
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics:** Expected 15%-20% spring-reset shelf-space gains (sold in last year); new customers include Subway (up to 18,000 locations), Home Depot, and expanded Kroger/Walmart front-checkout placement. Celsius is targeting placement of over 15,000 incremental coolers. 2024 included a "pretty significant optimization" of Pepsi's footprint (which also moved the Frito program into mixing centers); management says that inventory optimization is now "behind us."
  - **Distribution & Channel Dynamics (continued):** Convenience-store share lags total-company share because c-store was the last channel Celsius entered (Circle K only mid-2022); 7-Eleven share already exceeds the ~11 company share, illustrating the maturation path.

- **Structural & Reporting Comparability Factors:**
  - **Currency & Pricing Impacts:** Management noted a competitor took a price increase that aided category trends. Langhans said Celsius has "some price slots and things in place" leaving it comfortable on 2024-set pricing for the year.
  - **Portfolio & Mix Changes:** The pending Alani Nu acquisition (combined ~16.5 energy share, accretive to revenue and EPS in year one) and the Q4 2024 Big Beverages contract-manufacturer acquisition are the two structural changes; Alani Nu integration synergies (sales force, supply chain, procurement, back-office) are largely "known cost" expected to be lined up in the second half.
  - **Accounting & Calendar Effects:** Not specifically addressed beyond comp-timing dynamics.
  - **Macro & Seasonal Anomalies:** Management cited 2024 macro pressure — slowing convenience traffic, stimulus rolling off, interest-rate shock factors — as drivers of the Q3 2024 category contraction. A newly imposed 25% tariff was flagged as a swing factor management declined to model given its volatility; the center of excellence provides alternative ingredient routes to mitigate.
